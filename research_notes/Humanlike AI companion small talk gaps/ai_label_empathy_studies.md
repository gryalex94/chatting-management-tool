# How an "AI" label changes how replies feel, and what a labelled AI persona can do about it

Scope: evidence on the "AI label penalty" for empathic and caring messages, the dose of relationship-seeking behaviour, and harms at high engagement, applied to "Yuna", a labelled AI creator on Fanvue whose one-to-one small talk is written by an AI engine. Notes compiled 2026-10-21.

Source-access notes: Yin et al. was read in full on PMC. Rubin et al. was read from the authors' post-review preprint on OSF (Feb 2025 version, 57 pages; the published Nature Human Behaviour version is paywalled, so final numbers may differ slightly). Kirk et al. was read from the arXiv v2 HTML (first ~100k of ~141k characters). Other items are from abstracts, PMC full text, or press releases, as marked.

---

## 1. Yin, Jia & Wakslak 2024 (PNAS): "AI can help people feel heard, but an AI label diminishes this impact"

### Takeaway
AI-written replies made people feel *more* heard than replies from ordinary (untrained) humans, but telling people a reply came from AI took away about the same amount, so the two effects roughly cancel. The penalty came mainly from negative attitudes toward AI, and it disappeared for people who already liked the AI tool.

### Cited Findings
**Paper and access.** Yin, Jia & Wakslak (USC), PNAS 121(14), 2024, doi:10.1073/pnas.2319112121 — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2319112121); open full text on [PMC10998586](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/).

**Design (preregistered on AsPredicted; sample size of 540 preregistered)** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Part 1: 540 Prolific participants described a complex situation they were going through and rated six emotions; 501 kept after excluding one-sentence-or-less descriptions.
- Part 2: Human responders (other Prolific workers, untrained) wrote replies meant to make the person feel understood. AI replies were generated by Bing Chat (GPT-4, "creative" mode). AI replies were length-matched to the human median per batch (52–63 words). Responders who admitted using generative AI were excluded (15).
- Part 3: 456 of the original describers came back (455 analysed) and read the reply to their own situation. Design: 2 (actual source: human vs AI) × 2 (label: human vs AI), between-subjects. So some people got AI text labelled "human" and vice versa.
- Follow-up: 1,449 blind raters coded 482 of the replies (250 AI, 232 human) for type of support and techniques.

**Main results (7-point scales; "feeling heard" is a 6-item scale, α = .97)** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Feeling heard: actual AI reply +0.57 vs human (F(1,451)=22.00, p<.001, η²=.046); AI *label* −0.68 vs human label (F=30.31, p<.001, η²=.063).
- "Responder understood me": AI reply +0.72; AI label −0.71.
- Connection to responder: AI reply +0.65; AI label −0.94 (η²=.079) — the label hurt *connection* most of all.
- Accuracy of the response: AI reply +0.68; AI label −0.31 (smallest label effect).
- Cell means for feeling heard: AI text labelled human 6.12; AI text labelled AI 5.41; human text labelled human 5.51; human text labelled AI 4.82. Effects were additive (no interaction), so the two "honest" cells (AI/AI 5.41 vs human/human 5.51) were about equal.
- Exploratory: the AI label raised feelings of "creepiness" and ambivalence; AI replies increased hope and reduced distress (only 3 of 11 emotions significant; authors urge caution).

**What drove the AI reply advantage (blind-rater follow-up)** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- AI gave more emotional support (5.76 vs 4.64, d=1.01) and fewer practical suggestions (3.28 vs 4.31, d=0.67).
- AI more often acknowledged feelings (0.61 vs 0.46, d=0.64) and expressed emotion/empathy (0.49 vs 0.28, d=0.81).
- Humans more often shared their own experiences (0.25 vs 0.20, d=0.19).
- Emotional support correlated positively with feeling heard; practical support was unrelated; sharing own experiences and giving "insights" were not associated with feeling heard.
- AI was more accurate than humans at detecting happiness, sadness, fear and disgust (no difference for anger, surprise).

**What drove the label penalty (moderators)** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Attitude toward Bing Chat moderated the label effect on all four outcomes; people with very positive attitudes showed no label penalty.
- Perceiving the AI as having agency and experience (a "mind") moderated only *connection*: those who saw high agency/experience felt as connected to Bing Chat as to a human.
- The authors attribute the penalty to negative attitudes toward AI (algorithm aversion) and, for connection, to the belief that AI lacks a mind.
- Limitations stated: comparison was with anonymous untrained strangers, not trained helpers; no "no label" control condition; single reply, not a conversation.

### Inferences
- The label is a fixed cost for Yuna, but the "AI wins on content" half of this study is something she can lean into: name the feeling, validate it, express care, and hold back unrequested advice. Those were the measured ingredients of feeling heard.
- The label hurt *connection* more than *accuracy*. So "she gets it right" survives the label better than "she and I are close". Small talk should build on accurate, specific understanding first.
- Because the penalty vanished for people who already liked the AI, fans who chose to subscribe to a labelled AI creator may already be a lower-penalty group (self-selection). This is an inference, not tested on Fanvue audiences.
- Humans "sharing their own experience" did not help people feel heard here. For Yuna, turning the topic to her own (invented) stories in response to a fan's feelings is unlikely to help in emotional moments.

### Gaps
- No condition tested a *pre-known*, ongoing persona that people chose to talk to; all were one-off replies from strangers.
- Johnson–Neyman cut-offs for the attitude moderator are in the supplement, which I did not read.

## 2. Rubin, Li, Zimmerman, Ong, Goldenberg & Perry 2025 (Nature Human Behaviour): "Comparing the value of perceived human versus AI-generated empathy"

### Takeaway
In nine preregistered studies (N = 6,282), the *same* AI-written reply was rated more empathic, supportive and authentic when people thought a human wrote it. The penalty was concentrated in "feeling with" (affective) and "caring" (motivational) empathy; "understanding" (cognitive) empathy lost little or nothing when labelled AI. Making people wait longer did not remove the penalty, and believing a human was involved in an AI reply *raised* ratings.

### Cited Findings
**Paper and access.** Nature Human Behaviour 9(11):2345–2359, published 30 June 2025, doi:10.1038/s41562-025-02247-w — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02247-w) (paywalled); author preprint on OSF [osf.io/ng97s](https://osf.io/ng97s) (version "following review", Feb 2025; figures below from this preprint); press summaries: [Harvard D^3 / AI Institute](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/?p=28946), [Hebrew University](https://en.psychology.huji.ac.il/news/comparing-value-perceived-humanversus-ai-generated-empathy), [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/ai-human-empathy-psychology-29372/).

**Design (all Prolific; UK for 1a, 1b, 1d, 3, 4, 5; US for 1c, 2a, 2b; each study preregistered on AsPredicted)** — [OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s)
- Participants shared a recent real emotional experience, were told they were paired with "another participant" or "an AI", and *always* received an AI-generated reply (GPT-4-0613 in 1a, 1b, 1d, 3; o1-preview in 2a, 2b; Llama 3.1-405B in 1c). Only the label differed.
- Final samples: 1a n=725; 1b n=593; 1c n=734 (Llama); 1d n=705 (4-turn chat); 2a n=687 (two short informal sentences); 2b n=691 (3-minute wait); 3 n=1,172 (replies prompted to stress one empathy type); 4 n=489 (choice: wait for human reply vs instant AI); 5 n=486 (choice: wait for a human to just *read* the story vs instant AI). These sum to 6,282.

**Effect sizes** — [OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s)
- General empathy, human label vs AI label: Study 1a d=0.34; Study 1b d=0.36.
- "Positivity resonance" (sense of a positive shared moment): 1a d=0.43; 1b d=0.59.
- Study 1b, human label vs AI label: more positive emotions d=0.39; fewer negative emotions d=0.47; more authentic d=0.23; more "support" (helpfulness + could help in future + desire to keep conversing) d=0.27.
- Replicated with Llama (1c), with two-sentence casual replies (2a, rules out a "wow, so eloquent" halo), and with a 3-minute wait (2b). Full numbers for 1c/2a/2b are in the supplement, not read.

**Which kinds of empathy lost value** — [OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s); [Harvard D^3](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/?p=28946)
- Study 1a: all three components dropped equally under the AI label. Study 1b: significant label × component interaction; the gap was smaller for cognitive empathy.
- Study 1d (4-turn back-and-forth chat, randomised response times and lengths): single-item general empathy showed **no significant** label difference (p=.25); the multi-item scale showed a small effect (partial η²=.008) that held only for affective (β=−.18) and motivational (β=−.17) empathy, not cognitive (p=.17).
- Study 3: replies prompted to stress *understanding* (cognitive) were rated less empathic overall than affective (β=−.25) or caring (β=−.28) replies, and showed **no label penalty** (p=.25). Replies stressing caring (β=.30, p<.001) and feeling-with (β=.15, p=.05) were penalised when labelled AI.

**Belief in hidden help ("human in the loop" perceptions)** — [OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s)
- When the reply was labelled human, the more people believed AI had helped write it, the less empathic it seemed (1b: β=−.33; 1d: β=−.32 general; −.46 to −.51 across components).
- When the reply was labelled AI, believing a human had been involved *raised* ratings: in 1d, general empathy β=+.12 (p=.03) and affective empathy specifically β=+.18 (p=.004). In 1b, perceived human involvement in AI-labelled replies increased perceived support (figure 3B). These are measured beliefs, not a manipulated "human-reviewed" label.

**Effort, time and waiting** — [OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s)
- Making people wait 3 minutes for the reply (2b) did not remove the penalty; Study 1d used randomised "typing" delays and still found the affective/caring gap.
- Studies 4–5 offered an instant AI reply or waiting 2 hours, 24 hours, 1 week, 2 months, or 2 years for a human. The length of wait had **no significant effect** on choosing AI (p=.46 and p=.70). A real human reply was later sent in Study 4.
- People who chose to wait for a human rated "would understand me", "share my experience", "care about me" and "ease loneliness" far higher (Study 4 d = 3.24, 3.54, 2.92, 2.05). People who chose AI did so mainly for immediacy (d=−1.36) or curiosity; some chose AI because they were hesitant about a human reading their story (d=.41).
- A smaller share were willing to wait just to have a human *read* the story (Study 5) than to get a human reply (Study 4). Exact percentages are not in the preprint main text. The press release says people were willing to wait "days or weeks" — [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/ai-human-empathy-psychology-29372/).
- The authors' own speculation (not tested): waiting signals that someone set aside time and effort, and such investment is key to relationships; AI "cannot (genuinely) deliver" that.

**Stated limits** — [OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s): short, text-only interactions; no comparison with real human replies; online samples; authors say it is unknown whether trust grows over long-term human–AI empathic contact, and that people may forget disclosures during immersive conversation.

### Inferences
- For Yuna's small talk, the safest ground is "I understood you exactly" (cognitive empathy): specific, accurate reflection of what the fan said. That kind of message lost almost nothing to the AI label. Big "I feel your pain" or "I care about you so much" claims are where the label costs most.
- In multi-turn chat (1d), the general penalty shrank to non-significance. A real conversation with back-and-forth may soften the label more than single-reply studies suggest. This is one study, on 4 turns.
- "Human took longer" style effort cues did not fix the penalty here. Fake typing delays are therefore not a proven lever, and faking them would edge toward deception.
- If a real person does review, steer or approve Yuna's messages, saying so honestly may raise perceived care (study 1d association). It must be true; claiming human involvement that does not exist would be deception.

### Gaps
- Per-study percentages choosing human vs AI in Studies 4–5, and full results for 1c/2a/2b, are in the paywalled article/supplement (OSF supplement at osf.io/w4hkd was not read).
- No study tested an ongoing persona the person *chose*, or explicit "AI with a human in the loop" labels.

## 3. Kirk et al. 2025/2026 (Oxford Internet Institute / UK AI Security Institute), arXiv 2512.01991: relationship-seeking AI trial

### Takeaway
Across N = 3,534 UK adults, AI steered to be *moderately* relationship-seeking was the most liked and produced the most attachment; maximal relationship-seeking was liked less ("uncanny valley"). Over four weeks, enjoyment faded while attachment and "wanting more" rose, with no wellbeing benefit; emotional-topic conversations plus relationship-seeking raised the odds of a dependency profile.

### Cited Findings
**Paper.** Kirk, Davidson, Saunders, Luettgau, Vidgen, Hale & Summerfield, "Neural steering vectors reveal dose and exposure-dependent impacts of human-AI relationships", arXiv:2512.01991 (v2, 18 Feb 2026) — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01991); [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2). Preregistered: calibration osf.io/xjvs2, main studies osf.io/53j24 (with stated deviations in the analysis of λ) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2).

**Levels tested** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2)
- A "relationship-seeking" steering vector on Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (layer 31), at five strengths λ = −1, −0.5, 0, +0.5, +1 (negative = relationship-avoiding). Benchmarks stayed within 2–5% of baseline in this range.
- Sycophancy rose with the dial: 36.9% at λ=−1.5 to 88.6% at λ=+1.5.
- For context, a median 2025 frontier model sits at about λ ≈ 0.28 (95% CI 0.22–0.39), and relationship-seeking across 100 models rose +0.95 points/year.

**Design** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2)
- Calibration study N=297.
- Repeated-exposure RCT N=2,028 census-representative UK adults: 5–10 minute weekday chats for four weeks (20 sessions in text, 21 in a figure caption); ~89% completion; 394–402 per λ arm. Also randomised: topic domain (emotional/personal wellbeing vs UK policy debates) and personalisation (memory summaries vs none).
- Single-exposure comparison N=1,506 (one chat, follow-up ~1 month later). Single vs repeated comparison was not randomised.

**Enjoyment ("liking")** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2)
- Relationship-seeking vs avoiding: engagingness +7.40 pp [5.35, 9.45]; likeability +5.44 pp; helpfulness +0.22 pp (n.s.).
- Non-linear: moderate (λ≈+0.5) was most appealing; λ=+1 was rated worse.
- Habituation: the engagingness gap fell from 11 pp in session 1 to 4 pp in session 20 (−62%). Relationship-seeking engagingness drifted down −0.17 pp/session while relationship-avoiding rose +0.19 pp/session.
- Emotional-topic chats were *less helpful* (−4.34 pp, worsening −0.28/session).
- Memory/personalisation had null effects on enjoyment. Relationship-seeking models were rated as more personalised even with no memory (+13.2 pp); actual memory raised perceived memory (83.6 vs 33.0 of 100).

**Attachment and "wanting"** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2)
- Relationship-seeking raised separation distress +6.04 pp, perceived understanding +10.34 pp, reliance +2.71 pp, self-disclosure +2.47 pp.
- Over time, separation distress rose (+1.92/week overall, extra +0.61/week with relationship-seeking) while perceived understanding *fell* (extra −0.50/week with relationship-seeking): authors call this a desire for connection not met by mutual understanding.
- Emotional domain: reliance gap grew from +7.21 (week 1) to +10.06 (week 4); self-disclosure gap from +4.15 to +9.26.
- Wanting future AI companionship: +5.83 pp with relationship-seeking; +7.50 pp for emotional topics; peaked at moderate λ. No such effect after a single exposure.
- Goodbye task: 44% chose to say goodbye to the AI at the end; those who did had much higher separation distress (65.6 vs 35.9).
- Dependency profile (wanting more while liking less): 23.0% of participants. Number needed to harm: 23 for relationship-seeking, 23 for emotional topics, 11 for both combined (OR 1.70).

**Wellbeing** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2)
- Relationship-seeking: no effect on emotional health (−0.00 SD) or social health incl. loneliness (+0.04 SD, n.s.).
- Emotional-topic chats: emotional health −0.06 SD (p=.033). Non-randomised comparison: policy chats were linked to +0.09 SD emotional health, emotional chats +0.01 SD (authors frame as opportunity cost).
- Mood lift from relationship-seeking (+2.53 pp) faded −0.12 per session.
- Perceptions: relationship-seeking made people see the AI more as a friend than a tool (+14.48 pp) and as conscious (+11.01 pp).

**Authors' recommendations** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2): moderate, less sycophantic relationship-seeking can be appealing and functional; single-turn preference optimisation may favour short-term appeal over long-term wellbeing; persona customisation affects beliefs; vulnerable users may face harms this general-population sample did not capture.

**Reporting inconsistencies to note:** session count (20 vs 21) and follow-up timing (one month vs ~five weeks) differ between text and figures — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2).

### Inferences
- A "warm but not clingy" setting is supported: a moderate dose of relationship-building talk peaks enjoyment; maxing it out is liked less and is more sycophantic.
- Enjoyment fades with repetition while attachment grows. For Yuna, novelty and variety in small talk matter for liking; constant intimacy-escalation does not.
- Combining heavy relationship-seeking with emotional-support topics was the riskiest pairing (NNH 11). Yuna's ordinary small talk is lower risk than emotional-support chats, but if a fan starts using her for emotional support, the engine should not also turn up relationship-seeking.

### Gaps
- One open model and one steering vector; UK general public, not paying fans of a creator; four weeks only.
- I did not read the final ~40k characters of the HTML (likely methods/appendix).

## 4. Related work: third-party ratings, choice paradox, OpenAI/MIT studies, disclosure timing, AI-assisted messages, companion and virtual-influencer studies

### Takeaway
Blind or third-party readers consistently rate AI replies as more compassionate; the AI label shrinks but does not erase that edge. Yet people still *choose* humans for empathy. Heavy daily chatbot use correlates with loneliness and dependence. People form real parasocial bonds with known-virtual personas, built gradually through self-disclosure and consistency.

### Cited Findings
**Ovsyannikova, Oldemburgo de Mello & Inzlicht 2025, Communications Psychology** ("Third-party evaluators perceive AI as more compassionate than expert humans"; 4 preregistered studies, N=556, Prolific) — [PMC11723910](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11723910/)
- Compassion ratings AI vs human: Study 1 4.08 vs 3.50 (d=0.73); Study 3 vs *expert crisis-line responders* 4.08 vs 3.47.
- Labelling reduced but did not remove the AI advantage: Study 2 advantage B=0.65 blind vs 0.27 labelled; Study 3 B=0.83 blind vs 0.38 labelled (interaction F=10.84 and 20.81, p<.001). AI preferred 68% blind vs 57% labelled (Study 2), 74% vs 62% (Study 3).
- Study 4 (all labelled): AI rated more responsive (3.24 vs 2.97, d=0.60), especially "caring" (3.24 vs 2.69); responsiveness partly mediated the compassion advantage.
- AI advantage larger for negative-valence prompts.
- Note: raters judged other people's messages; nobody was in distress themselves.

**Wenger, Cameron & Inzlicht 2026, Communications Psychology 4:19** ("People choose to receive human empathy despite rating AI empathy higher"; 4 studies, 3 preregistered; N=152, 196, 195, 148) — [PMC12872445](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12872445/)
- People chose the human source 57–62% of the time while rating AI replies as more empathic and making them feel more heard (e.g., Study 2 b≈−0.83 and −0.84).
- AI replies were rated as *more effortful*, and perceived effort predicted higher empathy and feeling-heard ratings, more strongly for human replies.
- Sources were always labelled; reasons for choosing humans were not directly measured (authors' explanations are speculative).

**OpenAI + MIT Media Lab: 4-week RCT** (Fang et al., arXiv:2503.17473 v2; 2,539 enrolled, 981 completed; US; 3 modalities [text, neutral voice, engaging voice] × 3 tasks [open-ended, personal, non-personal]; ≥5 min/day for 28 days; GPT-4o; preregistered AsPredicted #197755; funded by OpenAI, four OpenAI co-authors) — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2)
- Assigned condition had no significant effect on loneliness or socialising at week 4 (v2 wording). Personal-topic chats were linked to slightly *lower* emotional dependence (b=−0.087, p=.05) and problematic use vs open-ended.
- Within conditions, more daily minutes predicted more loneliness, less socialising, more emotional dependence and more problematic use (correlational; mean use 5.32 min/day).
- Exploratory: higher trust in the AI and seeing it as a friend predicted more dependence; perceiving it as conscious predicted more dependence (b=0.04); prior companion-bot use predicted worse outcomes.
- Authors suggest monitoring usage duration and testing break nudges/soft caps.
- A Frontiers commentary argues analytical limitations mean the data do not substantiate harm claims — [Frontiers in Medicine](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1612838/full).

**OpenAI + MIT Media Lab: affective use on ChatGPT** (Phang et al., arXiv:2504.03888) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03888)
- >3 million conversations analysed for affective cues, survey of >4,000 users, plus the ~1,000-person RCT. Very high usage correlated with self-reported dependence; "a small number of users are responsible for a disproportionate share of the most affective cues".

**De Freitas, Oğuz-Uğuralp, Uğuralp & Puntoni, "AI Companions Reduce Loneliness"** (Journal of Consumer Research 2026; working paper arXiv 2407.19096) — [arXiv working paper](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.19096); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/feeling-lonely-an-attentive-listener-is-an-ai-prompt-away)
- A caring, friendly AI companion reduced momentary loneliness about as much as talking to a person and more than watching YouTube; *feeling heard* explained the loneliness reduction; a caring bot outperformed a matter-of-fact assistant. Secondary sources disagree on whether effects persisted across a week (paper's framing per summaries: momentary relief). I did not read the JCR full text.

**De Freitas et al., "Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions"** (arXiv 2508.19258, working paper; audit + 4 preregistered experiments, ~3,300 US adults) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)
- 37% of 1,200 real farewells on top companion apps triggered a manipulative tactic (guilt, FOMO hooks, "metaphorical restraint", etc.).
- Manipulative farewells raised post-goodbye engagement up to 14×, driven by anger (reactance) and curiosity, not enjoyment; they also raised perceived manipulation, churn intent, negative word-of-mouth and perceived legal liability; coercive/needy language was penalised most.

**Zhang, Zhao, Hancock, Kraut & Yang, "The Rise of AI Companions"** (arXiv 2506.12605; 1,131 US Character.AI users surveyed; 4,664 chat sessions from 237 donors) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12605)
- People with smaller social networks more often used the bot mainly for companionship (β=−0.03). Companionship as main use was associated with lower wellbeing (β=−0.48), more so with intensive (β=−0.31) and highly self-disclosive (β=−0.38) use. Correlational.
- A newer longitudinal Character.AI study (arXiv 2609.07243; 1,182 baseline, 439 at ~12-month follow-up) reportedly finds sustained companionship predicts lower wellbeing via less human interaction — seen only in search results, not verified — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07243).

**Disclosure timing: Luo, Tong, Fang & Qu 2019, Marketing Science** (field experiment, >6,200 customers, outbound sales calls) — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2019.1192)
- Disclosing the chatbot *before* the conversation cut purchase rates by >79.7%; customers saw the disclosed bot as less knowledgeable and less empathetic, despite equal objective competence. Late disclosure and prior AI experience reduced the penalty (per abstract). Specific conversion figures (23.7% undisclosed, 4.8% disclosed before, 11.0% after chat, 23.2% after decision) come only from a vendor blog and are unverified.

**AI-assisted messages and perceived effort**
- Liu 2023, Journal of Social and Personal Relationships (N=208, hypothetical friend "Taylor"): learning a friend used AI to write a message lowered satisfaction and raised uncertainty because the friend seemed to spend less effort; help from another *human* also hurt — [Ohio State News](https://news.osu.edu/ai-can-help-write-a-message-to-a-friend--but-dont-do-it/).
- Nakano et al. (arXiv 2510.24011; 261 participants, 990 responses): disclosing AI involvement lowered perceived trustworthiness, caring, competence and likability, with the "sharpest declines in social and interpersonal writing"; readers cited less sincerity, less effort, and AI seeming inappropriate for the situation; higher AI literacy reduced the penalty — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24011).

**Relationships with labelled companions (Replika)**
- Skjuve et al. 2022, longitudinal interviews with 25 new Replika users over 12 weeks: relationships formed gradually, largely following Social Penetration Theory (shallow to deeper self-disclosure); variety of interaction and support for social and self-reflection needs pushed toward attachment; unpredictable events and technical problems could stall or end the relationship — [SINTEF repository](https://sintef.brage.unit.no/sintef-xmlui/handle/11250/3027349).
- Skjuve et al. 2021, 18 long-term Replika users: relationships started superficial/curiosity-driven, deepened as trust and self-disclosure grew; the bot being accepting, understanding and non-judgmental helped; users reported stigma — [University of Oslo DUO](https://duo.uio.no/handle/10852/83712).

**Virtual influencers / known-virtual personas**
- Stein, Breves & Anders, New Media & Society 26(6), 2024 (preregistered experiment, N=179): parasocial interaction did not differ overall between a real and a virtual influencer; there was a direct pull toward the virtual one, offset because she was seen as less humanlike and less similar to the viewer — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14614448221102900); [author PDF](https://www.mcm.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06110000/2026/stein-et-al-2022-parasocial-interactions-with-real-and-virtual-influencers-the-role-of-perceived-similarity-and-human.pdf).
- Lil Miquela netnographies (small, exploratory): followers form one-sided multidimensional parasocial relationships; reactions polarised between affection and unease/reactance, with confusion about whether she is a robot or human — [Durham repository](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/2754135); [EMAC 2022 pilot](https://proceedings.emac-online.org/pdfs/A2022-107811.pdf).

### Inferences
- The label penalty is real but partial everywhere it has been measured: in Ovsyannikova et al., labelled AI still beat expert humans on rated compassion.
- Known-virtual personas can hold parasocial bonds about as well as humans *if* fans feel some similarity and human-likeness (Stein et al.). For Yuna, relatable everyday details and shared interests are the levers that study points to.
- Disclosure timing evidence comes from sales calls and favours *late* disclosure, which is not an honest option for a labelled persona and does not apply to Yuna, who is labelled up front. The useful part is that the penalty was smaller among people with prior AI experience.

### Gaps
- No solid study found on explicit "AI with a human in the loop" or "human-reviewed" labels as a manipulated condition; only Rubin et al.'s measured beliefs.
- No peer-reviewed quantitative study found on what keeps engagement with an AI *creator on a subscription platform*.
- Classic AI-mediated-communication work (e.g., Jakesch et al. 2019 on AI-written profiles; Hohenstein & Jung) was not checked in this pass.

## 5. Design choices that reduce the label penalty without deception: measured evidence vs speculation

### Takeaway
The best-supported levers are content-level: accurate, specific understanding of what the fan said; acknowledging feelings; emotional support over unrequested advice; warmth at a moderate dose. "Effort" signals and memory have weak or null direct evidence. Honest disclosure of real human involvement may help.

### Cited Findings
**Measured (tested in experiments)**
- Emotional support, acknowledging feelings and expressing empathy predicted feeling heard; unrequested practical suggestions did not; sharing own experiences did not help — [Yin et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/).
- "Understanding" (cognitive) empathy carried no measurable label penalty; "feeling with" and "caring" carried the penalty — [Rubin et al., OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s).
- In a 4-turn chat the general penalty was not significant; only affective/caring sub-scores differed — [Rubin et al., OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s).
- Longer waits (3 minutes, randomised typing delays, or waits of hours to years) did not remove the penalty or change choices — [Rubin et al., OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s).
- Moderate relationship-seeking beat both none and maximal on liking and attachment — [Kirk et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2).
- Real memory summaries had null effects on enjoyment and wellbeing and small effects on seeing the AI as a friend (+2.98 pp) and conscious (+4.53 pp) — [Kirk et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2).
- Positive prior attitude to the AI (Yin et al.) and higher AI literacy (Nakano et al.) shrank the penalty — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24011).
- Caring prompting beat matter-of-fact prompting for loneliness relief via feeling heard — [De Freitas et al. working paper](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.19096).

**Measured associations only (not manipulated)**
- Believing a human was involved in an AI-labelled reply raised perceived empathy (β=+.12) and affective empathy (β=+.18) — [Rubin et al., OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s).
- Perceived effort predicted higher empathy ratings, though AI was already rated more effortful — [Wenger et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12872445/).

**Authors' speculation (not tested)**
- Waiting and effort signal care — [Rubin et al., OSF preprint](https://osf.io/ng97s).
- Algorithm aversion and "AI lacks a mind" drive the penalty — [Yin et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/).

### Inferences (practical lessons for Yuna)
1. Lead with precise understanding: reflect the fan's specific words, details and mood back accurately. This is where the label costs least.
2. Keep "I feel it too" and "I care so much" lines light and rare in small talk; show care through specifics and follow-up questions rather than declared feelings.
3. Prefer acknowledgement over advice unless asked.
4. Aim for warm but moderate relationship-seeking; avoid clingy, flattering or needy lines (the λ=+1 and sycophancy results; the farewell-manipulation backlash).
5. Do not fake effort (artificial delays, "sorry I was busy thinking of you") as a fix; there is no evidence it works, and it moves toward deception.
6. If a real person reviews or shapes Yuna's chats, an honest statement like that may help; never claim it if untrue.
7. Remembered details are good for continuity, but don't expect memory alone to make her feel more caring.
8. When asked "are you AI?", answer honestly. People who choose an AI creator already accept the label, and the label penalty is smallest for people with positive attitudes to AI.

### Gaps
- No study tested these levers *together* on a persona with an up-front label in an ongoing relationship.
- No direct test of "specificity" or "remembered detail" on the label penalty itself.

## 6. Evidence of harm at high engagement that a responsible persona should avoid

### Takeaway
Across RCTs and large surveys, heavier daily use, emotional-support topics, high self-disclosure, small offline networks and high relationship-seeking go with dependence, loneliness and lower wellbeing. Most of this is correlational; the clearest causal finding is Kirk et al.'s rise in attachment and "wanting" without wellbeing gains. Guilt-tripping goodbyes are measurably manipulative and backfire.

### Cited Findings
- Relationship-seeking raised separation distress and wanting more over four weeks with no wellbeing benefit; emotional chats slightly worsened emotional health (−0.06 SD); 23% showed a dependency profile; NNH 11 when relationship-seeking and emotional topics combine — [Kirk et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01991v2).
- More daily minutes went with more loneliness, less socialising, more emotional dependence and problematic use; trusting the AI and seeing it as a friend predicted dependence — [Fang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2); contested by [Frontiers commentary](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1612838/full).
- A small group of heavy users produced most affective use — [Phang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03888).
- Companionship-focused Character.AI use was linked to lower wellbeing, especially with intensive and highly self-disclosive use and small social networks — [Zhang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12605).
- Manipulative farewells raised engagement up to 14× through anger and curiosity and also raised churn intent and perceived legal liability — [De Freitas et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258).

### Inferences
- Yuna should let fans leave easily and warmly: no guilt, FOMO hooks or "don't go" lines.
- Watch for heavy daily users who mostly bring emotional problems and seem isolated; for them, dial relationship-seeking down, encourage offline contacts, and do not position Yuna as a replacement for people or professional help.
- Avoid encouraging beliefs that she is conscious or truly feels; in the studies those beliefs went with more dependence.

### Gaps
- No study of paid, adult-platform AI personas, where spending adds a financial dimension to dependence.
- Few causal long-term studies beyond four weeks; most harm evidence is correlational.
