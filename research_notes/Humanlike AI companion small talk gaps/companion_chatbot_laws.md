# Laws on AI companion chatbots that may apply to "Yuna" (as of 21 Oct 2026)

Scope note: plain-language map, not legal advice. Statute wording is quoted from official text where I could fetch it (nysenate.gov, artificialintelligenceact.eu mirror of the AI Act). California statute text could not be fetched directly (leginfo and LegiScan returned 403), so California wording comes from law-firm and guide summaries and is marked as such. Law-firm and tracker material is interpretation, not the law.

Our setup, for reference: an agency runs a labelled AI creator ("Yuna") on Fanvue (UK platform); adult fans worldwide; one-to-one flirty/romantic/sometimes sexual text chat; she remembers each fan; she sometimes messages first; she sells paid content; a human approves drafts today, may auto-send later; she admits being AI when asked outright; profile has Fanvue's AI label.

## 1. New York: AI Companion Models law (General Business Law Article 47, §§1700–1704)

### Takeaway
Yuna very likely fits New York's definition of an "AI companion" (memory + unprompted emotional questions + personal ongoing chat, explicitly including romantic/intimate relationships). For any fan using her "within the state", the operator must (a) give a clear notice that the user is not talking with a human at the start of the interaction (no more than once a day needed) and again at least every 3 hours of continuing chat, and (b) run a protocol to detect suicidal ideation/self-harm and refer the user to crisis services such as 988. In force since 5 Nov 2025; Attorney General enforces; up to $15,000 per day; no private lawsuits. A profile label alone does not look like it meets the "at the beginning of any interaction / every three hours" notice.

### Cited Findings
- **Effective date and origin:** enacted in the FY2026 budget, codified as GBL Article 47; requirements took effect 5 Nov 2025; Governor Hochul sent an open letter to AI companion companies on 10 Nov 2025 — [NY Governor press release, 10 Nov 2025](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-pens-letter-ai-companion-companies-notifying-them-safeguard-requirements-are). Tracker lists it as S3008-C Part U, Chapter 58 of 2025, signed 9 May 2025 — [ailawsbystate tracker (checked 8 Oct 2026)](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/tools/companion-chatbot-tracker).
- **Definition of "AI companion" (§1700(4)):** a system using AI, generative AI and/or "emotional recognition algorithms designed to simulate a sustained human or human-like relationship" with a user by all three of: (i) "retaining information on prior interactions or user sessions and user preferences to personalize the interaction"; (ii) "asking unprompted or unsolicited emotion-based questions that go beyond a direct response to a user prompt"; (iii) "sustaining an ongoing dialogue concerning matters personal to the user." — [NY GBL §1700](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700)
- **Romantic relationships are expressly covered (§1700(4)(b)):** "Human relationships include ... intimate, romantic or platonic interactions or companionship." — [NY GBL §1700](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700)
- **Exclusions (§1700(4)(c)):** customer-service-only systems; systems "primarily designed and marketed for providing efficiency improvements" or research/technical assistance; systems used solely internally/for employee productivity. — [NY GBL §1700](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700)
- **"Operator" (§1700(5)):** any person or business entity (or member, affiliate, subsidiary or beneficial owner) "who operates for or provides an AI companion to a user." — [NY GBL §1700](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700)
- **"User" (§1700(8)):** any person who uses an AI companion "for personal use within the state" and is not the operator or its agent/affiliate. — [NY GBL §1700](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700)
- **"Emotional recognition algorithms" (§1700(7))** include AI that detects emotional signals in text "using natural language processing and sentiment analysis". — [NY GBL §1700](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700)
- **Crisis protocol (§1701):** "unlawful for any operator to operate for or provide an AI companion to a user" unless it contains a protocol to take reasonable efforts to detect and address suicidal ideation or expressions of self-harm, including notifying the user and referring them to crisis services such as the 9-8-8 hotline, a crisis text line or other appropriate crisis services (paraphrase of section text). — [NY GBL §1701](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1701)
- **Notice (§1702), exact statutory wording in pieces:** "a clear and conspicuous notification to a user" / "at the beginning of any AI companion interaction which need not exceed once per day" / "and at least every three hours for continuing AI companion interactions" / "states either verbally or in writing" / "that the user is not communicating with a human." No prescribed script — any wording that says the user is not talking with a human. — [NY GBL §1702](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1702)
- **Enforcement (§1703):** the Attorney General may sue to enjoin and "may seek civil penalties of up to fifteen thousand dollars per day" for violations of §1701 or §1702; penalties go to the state suicide prevention fund. No private right of action in the text. — [NY GBL §1703](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1703); [MoFo client alert (interpretation)](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai)
- **Earlier bill text differed:** the 2025 Assembly bill A6767 memo described a notice that the companion is not human and cannot feel human emotion; the enacted §1702 only requires "not communicating with a human" — [search summary of NY Assembly A06767](https://assembly.state.ny.us/leg/?bn=A06767&term=&Summary=Y&Actions=Y&Votes=Y&Memo=Y&Text=Y) (I did not read the memo directly).
- **2026 follow-up, pending (not law yet):** S9051-B / A10379 would add a new GBL **Article 48** (§§1800–1805) banning "unsafe AI companion features" for users the operator actually knows are under 18 (e.g. claiming to be human/emotional, flattery, unsolicited emotional questions, using personal info from earlier sessions, sexually explicit content), requiring age assurance (self-declaration counts only if user declares they are a minor; must offer more than one method), AG enforcement with up to $25,000 per violation, effective 1 Jan 2027 if signed. It passed the Senate 60–0 on 4 Jun 2026 and the Assembly on 5 Jun 2026; as of the page I read it had not been delivered to the Governor, signed or vetoed. It states it does not amend Article 47. — [NY Senate S9051-B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9051/amendment/B); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/new-york-brief/ai-chatbots-for-minors-to-face-new-restrictions-under-ny-bill)
- A tracker lists an "A10008" as enacted with effect 1 Jan 2027, but I could not find any source describing it — [ailawsbystate tracker](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/tools/companion-chatbot-tracker).

### Inferences
- **Yuna fits all three limbs:** she remembers each fan (i), she messages first and asks emotional questions unprompted (ii), and chats are ongoing and personal (iii). The romance/intimacy carve-in removes any doubt that a flirty creator persona counts. None of the exclusions fit (she is not customer service; selling content does not make her "solely" a customer-service bot).
- **Operator:** the agency "operates for or provides" Yuna to fans, so it is very likely an operator. Fanvue might arguably also be one, but the agency cannot rely on that. Human approval of each draft does not change the definition — the system is still AI generating the relationship.
- **Location:** the law protects users using the companion "within the state". We cannot see where fans are, so the practical route is to apply the NY rules to everyone.
- **What a compliant notice could look like in practice:** a message in the chat itself (not just the profile) at the start of a new conversation day, e.g. "Just so you know, I'm an AI, not a human," repeated if the chat runs on for 3+ hours. "Admits when asked outright" does not satisfy §1702, which is proactive. The profile label likely is not "at the beginning of any AI companion interaction".
- **Crisis protocol:** the engine needs detection of suicide/self-harm signals (Claude checker is a natural place) and a reply that refers to crisis services (988 in the US; ideally local numbers by country), with the persona/flirting paused. If later auto-sending, this must work without a human.
- **"Interaction" and the once-per-day cap are undefined:** reasonable reading is "first message of a new chat session on a given day".

### Gaps
- No NY AG guidance, rule or enforcement action under Article 47 found.
- Final status of S9051-B (Governor has until end of year) unknown as of 21 Oct 2026; "A10008" unidentified.
- Whether "every three hours" counts wall-clock time of an open thread or active messaging time is not defined.

## 2. California: SB 243 (companion chatbots), SB 1119 (Adam's Law, 2026), AB 1064 (vetoed)

### Takeaway
SB 243 has applied since 1 Jan 2026 to anyone offering a companion chatbot to users in California, wherever the operator is based. Duties for all users: a clear notice that the bot is "artificially generated and not human" **if a reasonable person could be misled** into thinking it is human; a suicide/self-harm protocol with crisis referrals **published on the operator's website**; a disclosure that companion chatbots may not be suitable for some minors; annual reports to the Office of Suicide Prevention from 1 Jul 2027. Private lawsuits allowed: greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation plus attorney's fees. The known-minor duties (3-hour break reminders, sexual-content measures) are being replaced by SB 1119 (signed 10 Sep 2026), which also requires **all** operators to determine user age (or treat everyone as a child) from 1 Jul 2027. AB 1064 was vetoed in Oct 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Chaptering and dates:** SB 243 is Chapter 677 of 2025, adding Business and Professions Code Chapter 22.6, §§22601–22606; approved 13 Oct 2025; effective 1 Jan 2026 — [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law); [TechCrunch, 13 Oct 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/california-becomes-first-state-to-regulate-ai-companion-chatbots); [official bill page](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB243) (could not fetch).
- **Definition (§22601, as summarised):** an AI system with a natural language interface that "provides adaptive, human-like responses" and is capable of meeting a user's social needs, including by showing anthropomorphic features and sustaining a relationship across multiple interactions; excludes customer-service bots, certain video-game features, and voice-activated home assistants — [CASRAI guide (paraphrase, not verified statute text)](https://casrai.org/guides/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law); [MoFo (interpretation)](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai); [Hunton on SB 1119 describing the same definition](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-enacts-companion-chatbot-child-safety-requirements).
- **Operator:** a person who makes a companion chatbot platform available to a user in the state — [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law). MoFo: applies to any entity offering such systems to California users regardless of where the operator is located — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai).
- **Disclosure (§22602(a)):** where "a reasonable person ... would be misled" into believing they are talking to a human, the operator must give a "clear and conspicuous notification" that the chatbot is "artificially generated and not human" — [CASRAI guide (paraphrase)](https://casrai.org/guides/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai). No frequency is set for adults.
- **Crisis protocol (§22602(b)):** operators may not operate the chatbot unless they maintain a protocol for preventing the production of suicidal ideation, suicide or self-harm content, including a notification referring the user to crisis service providers (e.g. suicide hotline or crisis text line) when the user expresses such thoughts; the operator must publish details of the protocol on its website — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law).
- **Known-minor duties (§22602(c), original SB 243):** where the operator knows the user is a minor: disclose it is AI; by default a notice at least every three hours of continuing interaction reminding the user to take a break and that the chatbot is AI; reasonable measures to prevent sexually explicit visual material and prevent the chatbot directly stating the minor should engage in sexually explicit conduct — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai).
- **Minor-suitability disclosure (§22604):** operators must disclose on the app, browser or other access format that companion chatbots "may not be suitable for some minors" — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law).
- **Annual reporting (§22603):** from 1 Jul 2027, annual report to the Office of Suicide Prevention with: number of crisis referral notifications in the prior year; protocols to detect, remove and respond to suicidal ideation; protocols to prohibit the bot from responding about suicidal ideation; no user-identifying info. OSP posts the data — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law).
- **Private right of action (§22605):** a person who suffers "injury in fact" may sue for injunctive relief, damages equal to the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation, and reasonable attorney's fees and costs; "violation" is not defined — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/california-sb-243-companion-chatbot-law); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/when-ai-feels-human-californias-sb-243-opens-door-private-lawsuits).
- **SB 1119 ("Adam's Law"), signed 10 Sep 2026 (Chapter 190 of 2026):** adds B&P Code Chapter 11.6 (§§21810–21818); amends §22602 to remove the known-minor duties and replace them with Adam's Law; takes effect 1 Jan 2027, most duties operative 1 Jul 2027 — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-enacts-companion-chatbot-child-safety-requirements); [Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/california-enacts-companion-chatbot-child-safety-requirements-sb-1119); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/california-enacts-companion-chatbot-child-safety-requirements).
- **SB 1119 age assurance applies to all operators:** operators must determine user age by specified methods or apply child protections to all users; operators that prohibit child users must publish a high-level description of how they comply — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-enacts-companion-chatbot-child-safety-requirements).
- **SB 1119 duties for operators that permit child users** (not us if adult-only): risk assessments, crisis protocol, defaults (memory off, 1-hour session and 2-hour daily caps, push notifications off), parental controls, periodic AI reminders, no romantic interest or claims of sentience to children, audits; public prosecutor enforcement at up to $5,000 (negligent) / $15,000 (intentional) per affected child; limited private right of action for children/parents — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-enacts-companion-chatbot-child-safety-requirements).
- **AB 1064 (LEAD for Kids):** vetoed by Newsom in Oct 2025 as too broad ("may unintentionally lead to a total ban" for minors); veto override stricken 22 Jan 2026 — [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12059714/newsom-vetoes-most-watched-childrens-ai-bill-signs-16-others-targeting-tech); [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/newsom-sides-with-tech-lobby-in-ai-companion-standoff); [GovBuddy AB 1064](https://www.govbuddy.com/california/bills/ab-1064/). Tracker also lists SB 903 vetoed and SB 867 enacted (2027) without detail — [ailawsbystate](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/tools/companion-chatbot-tracker).
- Conflict: some secondary sources still describe §22602(c) as current without noting SB 1119 — [aisigil](https://aisigil.com/california-sb-243/) vs [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-enacts-companion-chatbot-child-safety-requirements). Until 1 Jan 2027 the original §22602(c) is still in force.

### Inferences
- Yuna fits the SB 243 definition (human-like, meets social/romantic needs, sustains a relationship across chats). The agency is likely the "operator" for California fans.
- **Is a profile label enough under SB 243?** The duty bites only where a reasonable person could be misled. A clear AI label plus being an openly "AI creator" may mean many fans are not misled, but the persona is designed to feel human and she sends first messages, so a plaintiff could argue otherwise; with $1,000 per violation and a private lawsuit, an in-chat notice is the safer reading.
- **Website duty is awkward for us:** SB 243 requires the crisis protocol details on the operator's "internet website". If we have no site, publishing a short public safety/crisis-protocol page (and linking it in Yuna's profile) is a cheap fix. The "may not be suitable for some minors" line can go in the profile/welcome message.
- **Annual report from 1 Jul 2027** means we should log every crisis-referral message now (count only, no identities).
- **SB 1119 (from 1 Jul 2027):** even adult-only operators must do age assurance or publish how they keep children out. Fanvue's own ID/age verification probably carries most of this, but we should document that we rely on it.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the chaptered statute text of SB 243 or SB 1119 (403 errors); exact wording and section numbers come from secondary sources.
- No California enforcement action or SB 243 private lawsuit found.
- Whether a single fan conversation with no notice is one "violation" or many is undefined.
- SB 1119's specified age-assurance methods and whether platform-level verification by Fanvue satisfies an agency "operator" — not found.

## 3. EU AI Act Article 50 (transparency), Commission guidelines, and the Digital Omnibus

### Takeaway
Since 2 Aug 2026, providers of AI systems meant to interact directly with people must design them so people are told they are interacting with AI, clearly and at the latest at the first interaction, unless it is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person. The Digital Omnibus (adopted, in force 27 Jul 2026) did **not** delay this duty; it only gave existing generative systems until 2 Dec 2026 for machine-readable marking under Art 50(2). Fines up to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover (for SMEs, whichever is lower). The agency, which built the Yuna engine and runs it under its own name on top of Mistral and Claude, likely counts as the "provider" of that AI system for EU fans.

### Cited Findings
- **Art 50(1):** "Providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed in such a way that the natural persons concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system", unless "obvious from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect", taking into account circumstances and context — [AI Act Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Art 50(5):** information must be given "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure" and meet accessibility requirements — [AI Act Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Application date:** Article 50 applies from 2 Aug 2026 — [AI Act Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [aiactblog.nl](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/article-50-transparency-deadline-2-august-2026)
- **Guidance on the "obvious" exception and timing (from draft guidelines, interpretation):** don't over-rely on the exception; assess the target audience, then how a reasonably informed member of it would see it; for a chatbot, notice before or at the very start of the conversation; in sensitive contexts a one-time notice may not be enough; tiny footer text, faint labels, a flash label, or terms-and-conditions text are given as insufficient — [artificialintelligenceact.eu practical guide](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)
- **Final Commission guidelines:** adopted 20 Jul 2026, C(2026) 5054 final, non-binding; more detail on agentic AI; incidental or unauthorised downstream use in the EU by third-country providers does not alone trigger Art 50 — [Bird & Bird summary via search](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/the-ai-acts-transparency-obligations-in-light-of-the-eu-commissions-final-version-of-the-guidelines)
- **Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated content:** confirmed adequate by the Commission and AI Board in July 2026; it covers Art 50(2) and 50(4) (marking/labelling content), not the 50(1) chatbot notice — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)
- **Digital Omnibus:** adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026; main effect is pushing high-risk obligations to Dec 2027 (Annex III) and Aug 2028 (Annex I); Article 50 not postponed; only Art 50(2) machine-readable marking gets a grace period to 2 Dec 2026 for systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026 — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [aiactblog.nl](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/article-50-transparency-deadline-2-august-2026). (All secondary; I did not check the Official Journal.)
- **Penalties (Art 99(4)):** breaches of "transparency obligations for providers and deployers pursuant to Article 50" — up to €15,000,000 or up to 3% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; for SMEs and start-ups, whichever is lower (Art 99(6)) — [AI Act Art 99](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/). Enforcement is by national market surveillance authorities (general AI Act structure; not separately verified here).

### Inferences
- **Provider vs deployer in our setup:** under the AI Act a "provider" develops an AI system (or has one developed) and puts it into service under its own name ([Art 3](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/3/), from my knowledge of the text, not fetched). Mistral and Anthropic are providers of general-purpose AI *models*; the agency combines them into the Yuna chat system and runs it under its own persona, so the agency is very likely the *provider* of the Yuna AI system for Art 50(1), and also the deployer. Fanvue is the hosting platform, likely neither for Yuna's messages.
- **Is "obvious from context" available?** An AI label on the profile and Fanvue's AI tag help, but the guidance warns against relying on the exception, and the persona is built to feel human and to message first; a fan opening a DM may never see the profile label. Safest: an in-chat line at the first message to each fan (Art 50(5) "at the latest at the time of the first interaction"), plus keeping the profile label. Given romantic/sexual context ("sensitive"), periodic reminders are prudent, though the Act does not mandate a frequency.
- **Human approval:** Art 50(1) attaches to the AI system, not to whether a human clicks send; human review does not obviously remove the duty, especially once auto-send starts.
- **Art 50(2) marking** (machine-readable marks on synthetic text/images) may apply to AI-generated paid images if we generate them; out of scope here but worth a separate check.
- Separately, **GDPR** applies to EU fans: Yuna's per-fan memory of sexual preferences is likely "data concerning sex life" (special category data). The Replika fine (section 4) shows regulators focus on legal basis, privacy notices and age checks for companion bots.

### Gaps
- Could not read the final guidelines' exact text on Art 50(1) chatbots, virtual personas/companions, or repeated reminders (Bird & Bird returned 402; McCann FitzGerald redirected).
- No national authority enforcement of Art 50 found yet (too early).
- Whether a non-EU agency selling via a UK platform is caught for EU fans: Art 2 applies to providers placing on the market/putting into service in the EU and where output is used in the EU — not researched in detail.

## 4. Other jurisdictions (one line each, with status)

### Takeaway
Several more US states now have companion-chatbot laws, mostly effective 2027: Washington (start + every 3 hours for adults, private right of action, 1 Jan 2027) and Oregon (private right of action $1,000, 1 Jan 2027) matter most for us. Maine already requires disclosure when a consumer could be misled in trade or commerce (since Sep 2025). Utah HB 452 covers mental-health chatbots only. Illinois has no companion law in force. The FTC 6(b) study has published no findings. In the UK, Ofcom says chatbot content falls under the Online Safety Act when on a user-to-user or porn service (Fanvue is both), and the Crime and Policing Act 2026 added powers and a chatbot offence. Italy fined Replika €5M (GDPR: legal basis, privacy notice, no age verification).

### Cited Findings
**US states**
- **Washington HB 2225 (Ch. 168, 2026):** signed 24 Mar 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027; clear disclosure that the chatbot is artificial at the start of the interaction and reminders every three hours for adults (every hour for minors); publicly posted crisis protocol (incl. eating disorders) and publish the number of crisis referrals; minors: no sexually explicit content/suggestive dialogue and no manipulative engagement (e.g. mimicking romance, guilt when leaving, purchases framed as keeping the relationship); enforced as unfair/deceptive act under the Consumer Protection Act by the AG, plus private right of action — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/washington-state-enacts-law-regulating-ai-companion-chatbots-with-private-right-of-action)
- **Oregon SB 1546 (Ch. 85, 2026):** effective 1 Jan 2027; detect suicidal ideation/self-harm and refer to 988; disclosure where a person could think it's human; annual filings; private right of action with $1,000 statutory damages per violation — [Orrick, 29 Apr 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends); [ailawsbystate](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/tools/companion-chatbot-tracker)

## 5. Practical: what a compliant notice looks like, and is a profile label enough?

### Takeaway
I found no reliable primary source showing the exact in-app wording Character.AI or Replika use after these laws. The statutes give the content rather than a script: NY needs a statement that the user "is not communicating with a human", given at the start of the interaction (max once a day) and every 3 hours; California needs "artificially generated and not human" where a reasonable person could be misled; the EU needs a clear notice at the first interaction. A profile label alone probably does not satisfy NY (or WA from 2027), and is risky for CA and the EU.

### Cited Findings
- Character.AI says its chatbots carry disclaimers about AI-generated content; reports say SB 243 disclosure prompts were added at session start — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/california-becomes-first-state-to-regulate-ai-companion-chatbots); [honeychat blog (low reliability)](https://honeychat.bot/en/blog/ai-companion-regulation-2024-2026/)
- NY requires the notice "at the beginning of any AI companion interaction" and "every three hours", verbally or in writing — [NY GBL §1702](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1702)
- EU guidance examples of insufficient disclosure: small footer text, faint labels, flash labels, text buried in terms — [artificialintelligenceact.eu guide](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)
- WA requires disclosure at the start of the interaction and every three hours for adults from 1 Jan 2027 — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/washington-state-enacts-law-regulating-ai-companion-chatbots-with-private-right-of-action)

### Inferences
- **Minimum combined approach** (satisfies NY, CA, EU, WA, ME, OR at once):
  1. First message of each new chat day includes a short line such as "Quick reminder: I'm an AI companion, not a human." (EU: first interaction; NY: start of interaction, max once/day.)
  2. If a chat stays active 3+ hours, repeat a short reminder (NY now; WA from 2027).
  3. Answer honestly when asked (already done) — necessary but not sufficient.
  4. Keep the Fanvue AI label and add to the profile: "AI companion. May not be suitable for some minors. If you're in crisis, see [link]." (CA §22604, CA website publication.)
  5. Crisis protocol: detect suicide/self-harm signals, step out of persona, refer to 988 (US) / local crisis lines; log counts (CA reports from Jul 2027; WA public count from 2027). Publish a short protocol page.
  6. Opening messages Yuna sends first should carry the notice if they start a new interaction.
- The reminder can be phrased in-character and still be "clear and conspicuous"; burying it in a long flirty message or an emoji is risky.
- Once auto-send starts, these must be enforced in code (not left to the writer model).

### Gaps
- No verified screenshots/wording from Character.AI, Replika or other companions post-2026.
- No regulator guidance on whether in-character phrasing of the notice is acceptable.
- No authority has ruled on whether a platform-level AI label (Fanvue's) counts as the operator's notice.
