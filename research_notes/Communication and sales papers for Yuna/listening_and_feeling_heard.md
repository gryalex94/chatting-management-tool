## Paper 1. West, Huston, Chandler, Zhou and Fredrickson (2025), "High-quality listening behaviors linked to social connection between strangers", Communications Psychology 3:165, published 21 Nov 2025

### Takeaway
Two preregistered secondary analyses (total N = 646) of video-recorded stranger conversations: in a 10-minute "deep talk" task, the number of relevant follow-up questions and of verbal validation statements a person produced predicted faster turn-taking and the partner's and observers' sense of connection (but not the speaker's own); in a 5-minute staged small talk, validation and a global "attention + comprehension + positive regard" rating still predicted connection, but follow-up questions did not. A 48-hour "connect with strangers" intervention raised follow-up questions by about 23% and that rise statistically carried part of the intervention's effect on connection; effects are small and the senior reviewer called them "TINY".

### Cited Findings

Design and samples
- Two studies, both preregistered (Study 1 on 17 May 2023, Study 2 on 14 Oct 2023), both secondary analyses of earlier intervention datasets; total N = 646 — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Study 1: 335 UNC students recruited Fall 2021 to Winter 2022; 300 analysed (157 social-connectedness, 143 active control); 225 women, 66 men, 4 other; ages 18-35, mean 20.02; 50% White, 25.5% Asian, 10.4% Hispanic, 4.7% Black — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Study 1 intervention: a 3-minute video plus a scripted conversation with a virtual human ("Ellie", operated Wizard-of-Oz style) that asked for If-Then implementation plans; treatment = seek quality connection with strangers and weak ties over 48 h; active control = diaphragmatic breathing. Ellie's connection script includes "You might try making more eye contact and smiling with people. You know, just be attentive to others and try to connect with them" — no instruction about listening or asking questions — [West SI](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- Study 1 conversation: 48 h later, a 10-minute audio/video-recorded "fast friends" task (stack of printed questions of increasing self-disclosure) with one of ten trained female confederates blind to condition; the confederate always answered the first question with a standardized response and then matched the participant's intensity and intimacy — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Study 2: 416 working adults (ages 20-65, not undergraduates) recruited Mar-Nov 2019; 348 analysed (mean age 34.02, SD 11.28; 282 women, 62 men; 69.8% White); randomized to passive control (n = 89), social connectedness in general (n = 85), social connectedness with weak ties (n = 86) or mindfulness (n = 88); 35 days of nightly reports and daily email reminders of the goal — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Study 2 conversation: at the final lab visit the experimenter (one of four, blind to condition) pretended to be locked out of the laptop and struck up small talk from a script: "So, how does the rest of your day look?", a good-news story about getting the keys to a first apartment, and "You doing anything fun this weekend?"; up to 5 minutes or until the conversation died — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Sensitivity power: Study 1 models could detect f² = 0.03 and Study 2 f² = 0.023 (small) at 80% power — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)

The listening coding scheme (what was counted, what was excluded)
- Operationalization based on Kluger and Itzchakov's definition (attention, comprehension, positive intention); two verbal cues coded as frequencies per 30-second bin: relevant follow-up questions and verbal validation. Follow-up questions = "questions in which the listener conveys wanting to learn more, better understand, or clarify something the speaker said" (the task's own printed prompts excluded). Verbal validation = "expressing understanding, paraphrasing, or reflecting information the listeners heard, or as offering support or a friendly opinion on something the speaker said"; it "did not include short backchanneling indicators (e.g., yeah, mhmm) or swiftly turning the conversation back to the self by expressing simple agreement (e.g., 'me too'), rather than first acknowledging or reflecting the speaker's statement" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Full coder instructions (Section III): follow-up/exploring questions include questions that "convey wanting to learn more or further understand either the topic that the speaker is discussing or about the speaker themselves"; the question "is related to the topic the speaker is discussing and is not changing topics or not relevant to something the speaker said"; asking for clarification or repetition; asking "about the speaker's feelings about the topic"; even stating "I was going to ask about that". Excluded: questions about the task prompts or the study. Verbal validation "does not include yeah/mhmms", "does not include simply expressing agreement (e.g., I agree, same, me too)"; it "may include paraphrasing, reflecting information they heard from speaker, offering support, phrases that express understanding ('I get that', 'gotcha', 'really', 'right', 'sure', 'I've heard of that')" and exclamations of "opinion or active interest about what the speaker is discussing - could be positive or negative ('wow', 'jeez', 'that's great')"; each unique statement counted ("I can't believe that. You did the right thing" = 2) — [West SI](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- Reliability: follow-up questions ICC = 0.97 and validation ICC = 0.94 (Study 1); 0.96 and 0.92 (Study 2); every video had two coders and a 20% subset had all five — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2); [West peer review](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
- Why "me too" was excluded: the authors "interpreted it as a plausible attempt to shift the conversation back to oneself rather than acknowledging or reflecting the speaker's statement" — [West peer review](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
- Nonverbal listening cues were preregistered but coding was abandoned in week 2 of 6 of coder training: average ICC 0.75, single-coder ICC 0.37, and "a loss of confidence in the nonverbal cues coded as valid measures of high-quality listening when considered in isolation" — [West SI](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- Study 2 added a "global listening" rating (0-4 per bin by a separate team of three, ICC 0.87): "Did the participant exhibit high-quality listening? That is, did they show attention via backchannel behaviors, comprehension through paraphrasing and asking open questions, or convey positive intention?" The coder sheet defines positive regard as "exhibiting conversational receptiveness, including the use of hedges; that is, phrases such as 'somewhat' and 'might,' which soften the tone of the conversation and indicate a non-judgmental attitude" — [West SI](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- Validation of the global scale: correlated r = 0.54 [0.47, 0.61] with (log) follow-up questions and r = 0.67 [0.61, 0.73] with (log) validation; validation and questions correlated r = 0.32 [0.23, 0.41] — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)

Connection measures
- Conversational response time: gap in milliseconds between the end of one speaker's turn and the start of the next, averaged per speaker, from AssemblyAI transcripts (12 unusable transcripts dropped from these analyses only) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Perceived positivity resonance: 3 items (Study 1; participant alpha 0.87, confederate 0.93) or 7 items (Study 2, experimenter only, alpha 0.98) on a 0-100% slider: mutual warmth and concern, mutual energizing/uplift, feeling "in sync" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Behavioral indicators of positivity resonance (BIPR): 0-2 per 30-s bin for shared smiles/laughter and positive affirmation, coded by separate teams (ICC 0.73 Study 1; 0.89 Study 2) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Base rates, Study 1 (per 30-s bin): follow-up questions 0.19 (SD 0.28) i.e. roughly 1.9 per 10 minutes; validation 0.72 (SD 0.59) i.e. roughly 7 per 10 minutes; participant gap 1.01 s, partner gap 0.86 s; participant-reported positivity resonance 7.87/10, partner 7.91/10. Study 2: follow-up questions 0.32, validation 1.10, global 1.0 (SD 0.55) on 0-4, gaps 0.43/0.44 s, experimenter-reported resonance 8.08/10 — [West PMC Tables 1 and 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638239/)

Study 1 results (deep talk)
- Follow-up questions predicted faster own response time (standardized beta = -0.23, b = -0.10, 95% CI [-0.16, -0.05], p < 0.001) and faster partner response time (beta = -0.24, b = -0.08, CI [-0.13, -0.04], p < 0.001) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Verbal validation predicted faster own response time (beta = -0.42, b = -0.20, CI [-0.25, -0.15], p < 0.001) and partner response time (beta = -0.31, b = -0.11, CI [-0.16, -0.07], p < 0.001) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Neither behavior predicted the listener's own reported positivity resonance (follow-up questions beta = 0.05, p = 0.417; validation beta = -0.00, p = 0.999). Both predicted the partner's reported resonance (questions beta = 0.22, b = 0.49, CI [0.23, 0.76], p < 0.001; validation beta = 0.33, b = 0.80, CI [0.53, 1.06], p < 0.001) and observer-coded resonance (questions beta = 0.13, b = 0.12, CI [0.01, 0.23], p = 0.03; validation beta = 0.20, b = 0.21, CI [0.10, 0.32], p < 0.001) — [West PMC Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638239/)
- Raw-variable models give the same picture with smaller betas (questions to partner resonance beta = 0.12*, validation 0.22***; questions to BIPR 0.12*, validation 0.28***) — [West SI Table S1.6](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- Intervention effect: the connectedness group asked more follow-up questions (beta = 0.15, b = 0.21, CI [0.04, 0.26], p = 0.009), "corresponding to a 23% increase"; raw means 2.30 (SD 3.21) vs 1.45 (SD 2.21) questions in 10 minutes. No effect on validation (beta = 0.07, p = 0.234; raw 7.49 vs 6.90) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Exploratory mediation: intervention -> more follow-up questions -> latent social connection, indirect effect beta = 0.04, b = 0.06, CI [0.012, 0.124], p = 0.031; direct effect p = 0.630; the reversed model (connection as mediator of questions) gave no indirect effect (p = 0.243) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- From the transcripts: people rated high on listening "often recalled specific details shared by their partners and referenced earlier content to progress the conversation with follow-up questions (e.g., 'What were you thinking when the professor made that comment?', 'What happened after you told her what was on your mind?')" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- The authors' own reading of the magnitude: "during an interaction in which a stranger asks a person two or more questions, compared to one or none, this additional question may make a meaningful impact in signaling interest to a partner" — [West peer review](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)

Study 2 results (small talk)
- Global listening predicted faster own response time (beta = -0.14, p = 0.014), faster partner response time (beta = -0.16, p = 0.003), observer-coded resonance (beta = 0.34, b = 0.20, CI [0.14, 0.26], p < 0.001) and experimenter-reported resonance (beta = 0.21, b = 0.64, CI [0.34, 0.95], p < 0.001) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Follow-up questions in small talk predicted only the partner's faster response time (beta = -0.18, p = 0.001); not own response time (beta = -0.01, p = 0.886), not observer-coded resonance (beta = -0.02, p = 0.763), not experimenter-reported resonance (beta = 0.06, p = 0.295) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Verbal validation in small talk predicted own faster response time (beta = -0.22, p < 0.001; partner gap p = 0.079), observer-coded resonance (beta = 0.20, p < 0.001) and experimenter-reported resonance (beta = 0.26, b = 1.41, CI [0.85, 1.98], p < 0.001) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- The 35-day interventions (all three active groups pooled vs passive control) did not change any listening measure (global beta = 0.00, p = 0.957; questions beta = 0.08, p = 0.152; validation beta = 0.05, p = 0.350); per-bin means 0.33 vs 0.29 questions, 1.13 vs 1.04 validations, 1.48 vs 1.47 global — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2); [West SI Table S2.3](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- Authors' interpretation of the small-talk difference: "follow-up questions in the context of 'small talk' were sometimes offered out of politeness or as a way to combat potentially sitting in awkward silence", whereas in the disclosure task questions "more likely ... reflect motivation to connect"; and "explicit verbal indicators may be most indicative of listening when accompanied by a range of nonverbal cues ... particularly in settings when there is greater ambiguity" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- On the listener's own feelings: "high-quality listening does not always translate to markers of social connection for listeners themselves"; the dyad-level state "may be most pronounced when both individuals mutually display high-quality listening" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)

Boundary conditions and what the paper does not show (from the paper and its reviewers)
- Partners were trained confederates or experimenters who "likely became increasingly experienced in their respective roles"; samples were mostly women from one university; the authors ask for replication "in naturalistic contexts with unfamiliar partners who are not trained" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- The reviewer (Graham Bodie) wrote: "Overall, these are TINY effects (when they occur), and the effects that are there are not fully consistent (especially between the two studies)"; the authors accepted that "effect sizes were small" and that other factors (personality, motivation, relational dynamics) explain most variance in listening behavior — [West peer review](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
- The reviewer also noted the study does not distinguish "asking a question" from the kind of question, nor explicit from implicit validation ("verbal person centeredness"); the authors answered only with the transcript examples quoted above — [West peer review](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
- Listening was observed and coded, not manipulated; the mediation is exploratory; the authors say causality needs "experimentally manipulating follow-up questions" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Face-to-face spoken conversation only; no text chat; no repeated interactions; no sales or persuasion element; response time was measured as speech gaps, not message latency — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)

### Inferences
- The signal West found is relevance anchored to the speaker's content (questions that pick up a specific detail, statements that reflect or react to what was said), not the quantity of questions; the exclusion rules (backchannels, "me too") show the coders were deliberately separating "I registered what you said" from "I made a noise" or "I turned it back to me".
- Validation was about four times more frequent than questions in both studies, and it was the more consistently linked behavior (both studies, all partner-side markers), so for a chat persona it is the safer default move; relevant questions add value mainly in disclosure-type exchanges.
- Faster turn-taking as a marker of connection is a spoken-conversation phenomenon; its chat analogue (reply latency) is untested here.

### Gaps
- No analysis of which follow-up questions worked (open vs closed, about feelings vs facts); only the two quoted examples.
- No data on text-based conversation, on repeated contact, or on a relationship with a commercial element.
- The participant's own feeling of connection was not linked to their listening, and in Study 2 the listener's own feelings were not measured at all.

## Paper 2. Yin, Jia and Wakslak (2024), "AI can help people feel heard, but an AI label diminishes this impact", PNAS 121(14): e2319112121, published 29 Mar 2024

### Takeaway
In a 2 x 2 experiment (N = 455 analysed) where people who had described a real difficult situation received one written response, responses actually written by Bing Chat (GPT-4) made them feel more heard than responses from paid online strangers (Δ = +0.57 on a 7-point scale), while telling them a response came from AI lowered feeling heard by about the same amount (Δ = -0.68), independently of the true source; the two "truth" cells were statistically indistinguishable. Third-party raters (N = 1,449) found the AI responses gave more emotional support and less practical advice, and the techniques that correlated with feeling heard were acknowledging feelings, taking the discloser's side, affirming their qualities and voicing understanding; practical advice, insight and the responder's own experiences did not correlate.

### Cited Findings

Design and samples
- Preregistered (AsPredicted qi5cg, plus addendum #138714), Prolific, US residents, native English; run in six batches so that each person's description and the reply were close in time — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Part 1 (disclosers): 540 recorded an audio description of "a complex situation that you are grappling with" (prompt mentions a challenging decision, an interpersonal conflict, or "a profound question or thought"); 39 excluded for speaking one sentence or less; 501 kept; 251 assigned to human response, 250 to AI response — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/); [Yin SI](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Part 2 (human responders): other Prolific participants, paid $1.00, told "Please respond to what they said in a way that makes them feel understood" and not to use generative AI; 15 excluded for admitting AI use; 233 human responses kept; median human response length 52-63 words per batch — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- AI responses: Bing Chat in GPT-4 "creative" mode, prompt: "A person you otherwise don't know described the following complex situation they have been dealing with. Please respond to what they said in a way that makes them feel understood. Keep your response to [median number of words of human responses in that batch] words." — so AI replies were length-matched to human ones — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Part 3: 456 disclosers returned (94.4%); 455 analysed; label randomized at that point (told the reply was from "another Prolific participant" or from "Bing Chat, an AI chatbot developed by Microsoft and OpenAI"); disclosers 221 women, 227 men, mean age 43.82 — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)

How feeling heard was measured
- Six items adapted from the Perceived Partner Responsiveness scale, reworded to the response rather than the partner "to avoid issues related to partner mind perception": "reading this response makes me feel understood / affirmed / validated / seen / accepted / cared for", 7-point agreement, alpha 0.97, single factor — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Related measures: response accuracy ("accurately captures what I mean", "correctly summarizes what I said", alpha 0.90); responder understanding ("knew exactly what I meant", "understood what I was thinking and feeling", alpha 0.92); connection to responder (close, connected, trust; alpha 0.96); exploratory state loneliness, emotions, mind perception of Bing Chat (18 items: agency alpha 0.87, experience alpha 0.95), attitude toward Bing Chat (-3 to +3) — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)

Main results
- Response effect (AI-written minus human-written): feeling heard 5.74 vs 5.17, Δ = 0.57, F(1,451) = 22.00, p < 0.001, eta² = 0.046; accuracy Δ = 0.68 (eta² = 0.060); understanding Δ = 0.72 (eta² = 0.062); connection Δ = 0.65 (eta² = 0.041) — [Yin Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Label effect (AI label minus human label): feeling heard 5.13 vs 5.81, Δ = -0.68, F(1,451) = 30.31, p < 0.001, eta² = 0.063; accuracy Δ = -0.31 (eta² = 0.015, p = 0.010); understanding Δ = -0.71 (eta² = 0.060); connection Δ = -0.94 (eta² = 0.079) — [Yin Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- No response x label interaction on any outcome (feeling heard F(1,453) = 0.006, p = 0.94); the effects are "independent and additive" — [Yin SI Table S1](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Cell means for feeling heard: AI response + human label 6.12; AI response + AI label 5.41; human response + human label 5.51; human response + AI label 4.82. The two truth cells differ by d = -0.08 (p = 0.561); AI label vs human label on the same AI response d = 0.52; on the same human response d = 0.51 — [Yin Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/); [Yin SI Table S2](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Connection cell means: 5.19 / 4.28 / 4.56 / 3.56 (same order) — the label costs connection most — [Yin Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)

What drives the label penalty
- Attitude toward Bing Chat moderated the label effect on all four outcomes (feeling heard interaction F(1,451) = 18.69, p < 0.001); Johnson-Neyman: the penalty on feeling heard is significant for attitudes from -3 up to +1.43 on the -3..+3 scale (sample mean 0.34, SD 1.29), so only people with clearly positive AI attitudes showed no penalty — [Yin SI Tables S3-S4](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Perceived agency and experience of the AI moderated only the connection outcome (F = 9.43, p = 0.002 and F = 7.98, p = 0.005); people who saw Bing Chat as having a mind "felt as connected to Bing Chat as to another human responder" — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- The authors: "It is crucial, however, to distinguish between feeling heard by AI and feeling connected to it. In our data, feelings of connection depended upon perceiving AI as having the mind to think and experience, a belief that might not shift as readily" — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Suspicion check (added mid-study, n = 238): suspicion did not depend on the true source (AI-label: 2.62 vs 2.45, p = 0.59; human-label: 3.93 vs 3.52, p = 0.35); people were more suspicious under the human label, which if anything weakens the measured penalty — [Yin SI](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)

What drives the response advantage
- Empathic accuracy: Bing Chat's ratings of the discloser's six emotions were closer to the discloser's own ratings than the human responder's for happiness (d = 0.14), sadness (d = 0.27), fear (d = 0.20) and disgust (d = 0.19); no difference for surprise and anger — [Yin SI Table S5](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Follow-up rating study: 1,449 Prolific raters, each response (250 AI, 232 human) rated by 2-4 people who were not told the source; emotional support (reassurance, concern, positive feedback) AI 5.76 vs human 4.64, t(480) = 11.09, d = 1.01; practical support (advice, suggested courses of action, offers of assistance) AI 3.28 vs human 4.31, d = 0.67 — [Yin Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- 14 responsiveness techniques (proportion of raters saying "yes", AI vs human, Cohen's d): repeat back key phrases/summarize 0.50 vs 0.35 (d = 0.55); voice understanding ("I understand", "I see") 0.58 vs 0.42 (d = 0.67); express understanding of why the event matters 0.57 vs 0.46 (d = 0.43); agree/take their side 0.45 vs 0.35 (d = 0.38); big picture/insight 0.32 vs 0.37 (d = 0.16, p = 0.076); reassure things will work out 0.42 vs 0.34 (d = 0.30); acknowledge feelings as justified 0.61 vs 0.46 (d = 0.64); draw on own experiences 0.20 vs 0.25 (humans more, d = 0.19, p = 0.036); acknowledge efforts 0.41 vs 0.22 (d = 0.72); affirm desired identity/positive qualities 0.34 vs 0.21 (d = 0.52); exclamations/judgments 0.25 vs 0.14 (d = 0.48); express caring 0.25 vs 0.13 (d = 0.57); offer support/comfort ("I am here for you") 0.26 vs 0.15 (d = 0.48); express emotions/empathy ("I'm sorry that happened") 0.49 vs 0.28 (d = 0.81) — [Yin Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Third-party raters also expected AI responses to make the discloser feel more heard (5.64 vs 5.13, d = 0.55) and judged them as higher effort (4.90 vs 4.30, d = 0.56); the human responders' 4.30/7 effort is why the authors call them "moderately invested online strangers" — [Yin SI Table S12](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)

Which features correlated with the recipient actually feeling heard (SI Table S6; r with the discloser's own rating, N = 455)
- Emotional support r = 0.19**; practical support r = -0.02 (ns) — [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Correlated: affirm desired identity 0.15**; agree/take their side 0.14**; acknowledge feelings as justified 0.13**; voice understanding 0.12*; exclamations/judgments 0.12**; express why it matters 0.11*; repeat back/summarize 0.10*; offer support/comfort 0.10*; acknowledge efforts 0.09* — [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Not correlated: big picture/insight 0.01; reassure things will work out 0.02; responder's own experiences 0.04; express emotions/empathy 0.07 (ns); express caring 0.09 (ns) — [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- None of the 16 label x feature interactions was significant (all F < 2.3, p > 0.13), so the features' links to feeling heard did not differ between AI-labelled and human-labelled readers — [Yin SI Table S9](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Authors' conclusion: "AI specifically provided emotional support - support that proved more effective at making people feel heard - while refraining from offering practical suggestions. ... humans could benefit from practicing self-discipline to temper their tendency to offer advice and instead offer more emotional support if they want to make others feel heard" — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)

Emotions (exploratory, small)
- AI-written responses raised hope (4.66 vs 4.34, eta² = 0.010) and lowered distress (1.87 vs 2.15) and discomfort (1.79 vs 2.02); the AI label raised creepiness (1.48 vs 1.29, p = 0.035) and ambivalence (2.68 vs 2.34, p = 0.031); 3 of 11 emotions affected, authors "urge caution" — [Yin SI Tables S10-S11](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf); [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)

Boundary conditions and what the paper does not show
- Scope: "we compared AI response to an anonymous, noninteracting stranger online"; the authors expect AI "will likely fall short" against friends, family or professionals with "greater capability and motivation" — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- No no-label condition, "making it unclear whether AI labeling led to lower ratings or human labeling led to higher ratings" — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- One written reply to one disclosure, about 55 words, read once; no back-and-forth, no relationship history, no repeated exposure; 2023 Bing Chat; US adults, mean age 44 — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- The technique-to-feeling-heard links are correlations across responses, coded by third parties, with r between 0.09 and 0.19; the authors present them as "an intriguing mechanism", not a causal test — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Disclosures only: nobody in the study asked for help, so the paper cannot say what happens when a person wants advice — [Yin SI survey wording](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Feeling heard" here is operationally "the reply shows it took in what I said and treats my feelings and my qualities as valid"; the strongest correlates are specific acknowledgments (feelings justified, effort, qualities, taking his side), not generic sympathy phrases or promises that it will be fine.
- The AI advantage came with the same word budget as humans (about 55 words), so the lever was selection of content, not length.
- The label penalty is a constant subtraction, not a change in what works: under the AI label the ordering of good and bad replies is the same, only the ceiling is lower, and the ceiling is lowest on closeness/trust.

### Gaps
- No test of advice-seeking disclosures, of multi-turn chats, of flirtatious or commercial contexts, or of whether the penalty fades with repeated contact or a persistent persona.
- The authors' "Reddit" quotes about ChatGPT responding "to my whole question" are anecdotes in the introduction, not data.

## Paper 3. Santoro, Broockman, Kalla and Porat (2025), "Listen for a change? A longitudinal field experiment on listening's potential to enhance persuasion", PNAS 122(8): e2421982122, published 20 Feb 2025

### Takeaway
In 1,485 roughly 10-minute Zoom conversations between trained ex-canvassers and Americans opposed to in-state tuition for undocumented immigrants, non-judgmental listening (at least three "tell me more" style inquiries, nods, "I hear that", paraphrase, no agreeing and no arguing) made people feel understood and valued (+0.79 SD), liked (+0.19 SD), respected (+0.28 SD) and less defensive (-0.23 SD), and made them talk three times as much, but added nothing to persuasion: a persuasive narrative moved attitudes by about 0.25-0.33 SD immediately and about 0.2 SD five weeks later with or without listening (difference d = 0.06-0.07 immediately, -0.02 at five weeks; upper 95% bound 0.11 SD).

### Cited Findings

Design and sample
- Preregistered field experiment; eight confederates who had been professional door-to-door canvassers, working about 8 hours a week for four months, had 1,526 one-on-one Zoom conversations; 1,485 completed the immediate survey and 1,406 the five-week follow-up (92% recontact, average 5.1 weeks later) — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/); [Santoro SI](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- Recruitment by Meta ads targeted at conservative congressional districts; only opponents of the policy were invited; sample at the immediate survey: mean age 49, 66% female, 87% White, 56% college-educated, 50% Republican — [Santoro SI](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- 2 x 2 between subjects: Listening (yes/no) x Persuasive appeal (a personal narrative about an undocumented immigrant, yes/no); allocation per 16 participants: 3 Listening Only, 3 Placebo, 5 Listening + Persuasion, 5 Persuasive Appeal Only; cell sizes at the immediate survey 267 / 283 / 469 / 466 — [Santoro SI](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf); [Santoro Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Every conversation opened the same way: the confederate read aloud "instructions", stated "I'm a 10 and strongly support allowing illegal immigrants to get in-state college tuition" and asked the participant for their 0-10 number (average 3.8); in the no-listening conditions the conversation then moved on (Placebo ended; Persuasion Only went straight to the story) — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/); [Santoro SI Fig. S2](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- Confederates were trained 10-15 hours each (initial 1.5 h session, a two-hour pilot with recorded feedback, weekly hour-long coaching for three months) and never told the hypotheses — [Santoro SI](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)

What "listening" consisted of (the training sheet, Fig. S4, and the scripts)
- Definition given to confederates: "Non-judgmental listening includes paying attention to what your counterpart is saying and actively and curiously inquiring about what they have to say, all the while withholding judgment" — [Santoro SI Fig. S4](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- "Affirmative Attention, Rather Than Judgment": nod your head; affirm with "I see", "I hear that", "hmm"; "Repeat back or paraphrase what they're saying ('it sounds like / it seems like / I hear you saying that ...')"; "Avoid disagreeing with them or criticizing their beliefs" — [Santoro SI Fig. S4](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- "Inquire About Their Opinion": "Tell me more", "Say more", "Could you elaborate?", "Could you tell me more about why you think that?", "What led you to having your opinion about undocumented immigrants?", "What are your concerns about making them eligible?"; "Please be sure to say three 'inquiries' (i.e., a question or a statement encouraging elaboration) over the course of the conversation" — [Santoro SI Fig. S4](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- "Things to Avoid": "Do NOT agree with them ... You can encourage feelings ('I hear that') without affirming their ideas (so avoid saying 'I agree with you' or 'Correct' about an opinion)"; "Do NOT try to argue with the points they make"; "Do NOT ask questions about their own story related to immigration"; "Do NOT check your phone or email" — [Santoro SI Fig. S4](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- In the Listening Only condition the confederate was also told not to share their own reasons; in the Persuasion Only condition not to engage if the participant raised their own reasoning; in Listening + Persuasion the listening came first and the story second — [Santoro SI](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- Confederates were explicitly told not to use what they heard to tailor the appeal: "We explicitly asked our confederates to avoid engaging in listening of this type in order to not confound any effects of listening with changes in the persuasive appeal made" — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- The manipulation was deliberately strong ("high-quality listening to minimal listening"), and participants "may have partially attributed the confederate's listening to the instructions, which they read aloud" — [Santoro footnote](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)

Manipulation checks and what listening did to the conversation
- "Partner listened" index (four items: "carefully listened to my opinions", "I felt really listened to", "asked me questions", "understood why I have my view"): Listening vs Placebo +1.03 SD; Listening + Persuasion vs Persuasion Only +1.20 SD (both p < 0.001); Persuasion Only did not differ from Placebo (-0.08) — [Santoro SI Table S8](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- Conversation content by condition (confederate words / participant words / minutes / confederate receptiveness score from the politeness package): Placebo 734 / 307 / 4.6 / 0.5; Persuasion Only 1,200 / 343 / 7.8 / 0.7; Listening Only 1,107 / 996 / 9.7 / 1.3; Listening + Persuasion 1,570 / 1,010 / 13.4 / 1.4 — [Santoro SI Table S9](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)

Persuasion outcomes (standardized indices; higher = more pro-immigrant)
- Immediately: Policy — Placebo -0.19, Listening -0.10, Persuasion Only 0.08, Listening + Persuasion 0.15; effects: Listening vs Placebo 0.09 (adjusted p = 0.062), Persuasion vs Placebo 0.26***, Listening + Persuasion vs Placebo 0.33***, Listening + Persuasion vs Persuasion Only 0.07 (ns), interaction -0.02. Prejudice: 0.08+, 0.25***, 0.30***, 0.06 (ns), -0.02 — [Santoro Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Five weeks later: Policy — Listening vs Placebo 0.01, Persuasion 0.21***, Listening + Persuasion 0.23***, Listening + Persuasion vs Persuasion Only 0.02; Prejudice — -0.01, 0.17***, 0.18***, 0.01 — [Santoro Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- In plain numbers: immediately after the conversation 33.6% supported the policy in Placebo, 34.5% in Listening Only, 45.9% in Persuasion Only, 48.8% in Listening + Persuasion; five weeks later the differences were 5-10 percentage points — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Primary preregistered comparison (Listening + Persuasion vs Persuasion Only): d = 0.06 and 0.07 immediately (p > 0.10), d = -0.02 at five weeks (p > 0.617); the long-term 95% CI "did not exceed d = 0.11"; the study had 80% power to detect d = 0.102, i.e. listening raising persuasion by about 40% — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Listening alone had at most a small immediate direct effect (d about 0.09, adjusted p = 0.062; pooled "any listening" main effect d = 0.07, adjusted p = 0.035) that "decayed to zero in the long term" — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Reweighting to the full baseline population changed nothing (Listening + Persuasion on policy 0.33 unweighted vs 0.34 weighted) — [Santoro SI Tables S21-S22](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)

- Felt defensive (defensive, guarded, open reversed): -0.23***; raw 1.56 / 1.49 / 1.64 / 1.46 on 1-5, i.e. "fairly low on average, at approximately 1.5 on a 5-point scale" — [Santoro Table 3 and footnote](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Reflected on attitudes: +0.07 (ns) on top of the story (Listening alone +0.54*** vs Placebo; story alone +0.42***); the SI shows a negative interaction (-0.47***): listening increased reflection only when there was no story — [Santoro Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/); [Santoro SI Table S13](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)

- All 17 confederates and research assistants predicted Listening + Persuasion would be the most persuasive condition — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Authors' summary: "improved interpersonal perceptions and reduced defensiveness do not appear to enhance persuasion"; narratives "communicated novel information" and that, not the listening, "is likely responsible" for the attitude change; practically, "adding listening to persuasive appeals may not be worth the added costs if persuasion is the goal" — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)

Boundary conditions and what the paper does not show
- Only one appeal type (a detailed personal narrative); "it is possible it may enhance the persuasiveness of facts or other persuasive methods" — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Participants had opted in to a video conversation with a stranger; video only, "text-based conversations" and in-person not tested; one contentious political topic; only opponents recruited; no objective learning measure — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- The untested, more plausible route: "a persuader could listen to understand a counterpart's needs or concerns, and then in turn tailor the persuasive message accordingly ... future research should investigate these questions" — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Listening may still matter for whether people enter or stay in a conversation ("Receive" in Zaller's terms) even if it does not change "Accept"; "failing to listen well may also be uncomfortable for the speaker, reducing their interest in having such conversations" — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Defensiveness was low to begin with, so "it is possible that lowering defensiveness would matter in more acrimonious conversations" — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Prior text-chat studies reviewed in the paper reach the same null: Chen et al. 2010 (an elaboration question raised perceived receptiveness, no policy change), Minson et al. (receptiveness raised trust, no vaccine-attitude change), Argyle et al. 2023 (AI-rephrased, more understanding messages improved perceptions and respect, no gun-policy change) — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)

### Inferences
- Listening is a strong lever on the relationship variables (understood and valued, respect, liking, defensiveness) and on how much the other person talks, and a near-zero lever on whether they accept the persuader's position; the content that was meant to persuade did the persuading.
- The listening that was tested was "listen, then deliver the same pitch regardless"; "listen, then say something that fits what you heard" was deliberately excluded, so the null does not cover a tailored offer.

### Gaps
- No text or asynchronous version, no repeated contact, no commercial or affectionate context, no measure of whether people would return to the same partner (the "interest in another conversation" item was about talking "with someone else").

## Key question 1. Which concrete listening behaviours were linked to connection, which were not, and could they become coach labels or scorecard measures?

### Takeaway
The behaviours with evidence are (a) follow-up questions that pick up something the speaker just said, (b) validation statements that reflect, acknowledge or react to the speaker's content before anything else, and (c) in brief small talk, an overall impression of attention + comprehension + non-judgment; the behaviours explicitly excluded or unlinked are backchannel noises, "me too" / "same" that turn the talk back to self, and in small talk the raw number of questions. A relevance-based coach label fits the evidence far better than a question count.

### Cited Findings
- Coded and linked (deep talk): relevant follow-up questions and verbal validation, each predicting partner-reported resonance (beta 0.22 and 0.33), observer-coded resonance (0.13 and 0.20) and both speakers' turn speed — [West PMC Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638239/)
- Coded and linked (small talk): verbal validation (partner resonance beta 0.26, observer 0.20) and global listening (0.21 and 0.34); not linked: follow-up questions (0.06 and -0.02) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Not counted as listening by the coders: "yeah/mhmms", "I agree, same, me too", questions about the task or procedure, questions that change topic — [West SI Section III](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- Counted as validation: paraphrase, reflecting, offering support, "I get that", "gotcha", "really", "right", "sure", "I've heard of that", and exclamations of opinion or interest "wow", "jeez", "that's great" — [West SI Section III](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- The transcript signature of good listeners: recalling specific details and "referenced earlier content to progress the conversation" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Independently, the Santoro listening package (three "tell me more" inquiries, "I hear that", paraphrase, no judgment) moved "felt understood and valued" by 0.79 SD and "partner listened" by 1.2 SD — [Santoro Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/); [Santoro SI Table S8](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- Independently, in written replies the features that correlated with feeling heard were acknowledging feelings as justified, taking the discloser's side, affirming their qualities, voicing understanding, repeating back; not advice, insight, reassurance, or the responder's own experiences — [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Nonverbal cues could not be coded reliably or validly in isolation (ICC 0.75 average, 0.37 single coder) — [West SI](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)

### Inferences
- PROPOSAL (coach label): replace or supplement the question-count band with a per-reply label "relevant follow-up question": a question that asks to learn more, understand or clarify something he said in his last message (or earlier), or asks how he feels about it, and does not change the subject. West's coder rules give the exact definition; the engine's reader already extracts "what is new" and "what she would want to ask", so the label can check whether the question targets one of those.
- PROPOSAL (coach label): "validation before share": does the reply contain at least one statement that reflects, paraphrases, supports or reacts to his content before Yuna shares anything of her own or asks anything. West's exclusion rules become the negative cases: a bare "haha", "omg", "same", "me too" is not validation.
- PROPOSAL (scorecard measures): (1) share of her replies after a substantive fan message that contain a relevant follow-up question or a validation statement (either, not both required); (2) share of replies whose validation names a specific detail of his (the "understood" coach dimension already does this; the papers support making it a tracked band); (3) the ratio of validation to questions (in West it was about 4:1 in both contexts, a plausible human-chatter-like band to check rather than a target).
- The current rule "no third question in a row" is consistent with the data in the sense that the papers show value in relevance, not volume; the 30-45% band itself has no support or refutation in these papers because they count questions per time, not per turn.

### Gaps
- No paper tested question quality types (open vs closed, feelings vs facts); the only evidence is West's two transcript examples.
- Everything is spoken (West, Santoro) or single written replies (Yin); no multi-turn chat coding exists in these sources.

## Key question 2. Disclosure versus request for help: acknowledgment versus suggestions, and what exactly made the AI responses feel more "heard"

### Takeaway
For a disclosure (someone describing a hard situation), what made people feel heard was emotional support built from specific acknowledgments (feelings justified, effort recognized, qualities affirmed, side taken, content repeated back); practical suggestions, insight-giving, reassurance that things will work out and the responder's own experiences did not raise feeling heard. None of the papers tested a person who asked for advice, so the "request for help" side is an inference only.

### Cited Findings
- Human responders gave more practical support (4.31 vs 3.28) and more of their own experiences (0.25 vs 0.20) and insights (0.37 vs 0.32); AI gave more emotional support (5.76 vs 4.64) — [Yin Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Feeling heard correlated with emotional support (r = 0.19) and not with practical support (r = -0.02); the two human-favoured techniques, own experiences (0.04) and insight (0.01), were unrelated — [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Reassurance ("things will work out", "express faith") was used more by AI (0.42 vs 0.34) but was unrelated to feeling heard (r = 0.02); generic empathy phrases ("I'm sorry that happened") had the biggest AI-human gap (d = 0.81) but r = 0.07, not significant — [Yin Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/); [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- The AI also read the discloser's emotions more accurately on four of six emotions — [Yin SI Table S5](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- The disclosers' prompt asked for "a complex situation that you are grappling with" and the responders' instruction was to make them "feel understood"; there was no advice-seeking condition — [Yin SI survey wording](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- West's coders excluded "me too" because it shifts the conversation to the self — [West peer review](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
- Santoro's listeners were told they could "encourage feelings ('I hear that') without affirming their ideas" and must not argue — [Santoro SI Fig. S4](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)

### Inferences
- The engine's "he is sharing something hard" mode (reflect his words, name the feeling, no story of her own, no unasked advice) matches the evidence closely; the papers add two refinements. PROPOSAL: in that mode, prefer the specific acknowledgments that correlated with feeling heard — his feeling is justified given what happened, the effort he has put in, a quality of his that the situation shows, and taking his side — over two moves that did not correlate: reassurance ("it'll be okay") and a generic "I'm so sorry". The current "comfort" and "reassure" moves would be worth splitting along this line.
- PROPOSAL: treat "repeat back a key phrase" as a first-class move in hard mode; it was the clearest marker of taking in the whole message (AI 0.50 vs human 0.35) and did correlate with feeling heard (r = 0.10).
- For a request for help, no evidence exists in these papers. The defensible inference is the engine's existing "answers him" criterion: if he asks what to do, a reply that gives nothing practical fails to answer him; Yin's null on practical support should not be read as "never give advice", only as "advice does not make someone feel heard".

### Gaps
- No study of advice requests, of the right mix of acknowledgment plus suggestion, or of timing (acknowledge first, suggest later).

## Key question 3. What the AI-label penalty implies for a persona the fans know is an AI

### Takeaway
The label penalty is a fixed subtraction that leaves the ranking of behaviours untouched: every technique kept the same link to feeling heard under the AI label as under the human label. Feeling heard and understood survive the label with a lower ceiling; closeness and trust take the biggest hit and depend on whether the person grants the AI a mind; fans with a positive attitude toward AI show little or no penalty.

### Cited Findings
- Label penalty sizes: feeling heard -0.68 (eta² 0.063), understanding -0.71 (0.060), accuracy -0.31 (0.015), connection -0.94 (0.079) — [Yin Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- No label x response interaction (the penalty is the same for good and poor replies) — [Yin SI Table S1](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- No label x technique interaction for any of the 16 features (all p > 0.13) — [Yin SI Table S9](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Even under the AI label, an AI-written reply scored 5.41/7 on feeling heard, which the authors call "moderately high" and equal to a labelled-human stranger's reply (5.51) — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Attitude toward the AI moderated the penalty on all outcomes; people with very positive attitudes "did not exhibit any negative AI label effects" — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Mind perception moderated only connection; those who saw agency and experience in the AI "felt as connected to Bing Chat as to another human responder" — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- The label added a little creepiness (1.48 vs 1.29) and ambivalence (2.68 vs 2.34), while the AI-written content itself lowered distress and discomfort — [Yin SI Tables S10-S11](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- The study had no "no label" condition and the label was shown at the moment of reading — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)

### Inferences
- Behaviours that keep their value under the label: reflecting his content, naming and validating his feeling, recognizing his effort and qualities, taking his side, answering what he actually said. Nothing in the data says any of these loses value because the reader knows it is an AI.
- What loses most: the fan's reported closeness and trust toward Yuna, which is the outcome most tied to whether he treats her as having a mind. This supports the warmth dial's rarity of declared feelings: declarations of closeness are claims about her inner life, exactly the thing the label makes him discount.
- PROPOSAL (measurement): keep "understood" and "answers him" as the coach's primary targets and expect them to reach human-chatter bands; set separate, lower expectations for closeness-type outcomes in the scorecard rather than treating a gap there as a dialogue failure.
- PROPOSAL (reader signal): since attitude toward AI is the strongest moderator, the Haiku reader could tag how the fan relates to her being an AI (hostile or mocking, neutral, curious, fully accepting); the rapport meter and the warmth dial could be read against that tag. This is a proposal built on a moderator analysis, not on a tested intervention.
- The ambient Fanvue label differs from Yin's explicit label at the moment of reading; the penalty might be larger (constant reminder) or smaller (habituation). Unknown.

### Gaps
- No data on label penalties in ongoing relationships, in flirtatious or sexual contexts, or where the AI is a named persona with a face; no data on whether disclosure of AI status up front changes later turns.

## Key question 4. Listening versus persuasion: what Santoro's null means for an engine that must also sell, and which outcomes to keep separate

### Takeaway
Santoro shows that listening reliably raises "understood and valued", respect, liking and willingness to talk, and reliably fails to raise acceptance of the persuader's position; persuasion came from the content of the appeal. For Yuna this argues for keeping rapport-type meters and readiness-to-pay as separate outcomes with separate drivers, and for not expecting listening-quality improvements to show up as sales.

### Cited Findings
- Listening + Persuasion vs Persuasion Only: d = 0.06-0.07 immediately, -0.02 at five weeks, upper bound 0.11; interaction terms null (adjusted p = 0.566) — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Same comparison on relationship outcomes: understood and valued +0.79, respect +0.28, liking +0.19, defensiveness -0.23 (all significant) — [Santoro Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Listening roughly tripled what the participant said (996-1,010 words vs 307-343) and lengthened conversations (9.7-13.4 min vs 4.6-7.8) — [Santoro SI Table S9](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- The story alone raised liking as much as listening alone (+0.36 vs +0.37 SD over Placebo) and raised learning by 1.39 SD — [Santoro Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Listening did not change interest in having another such conversation — [Santoro SI Tables S10-S11](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- Confederates were forbidden to tailor the appeal to what they heard; the authors flag "listen to understand ... then tailor" as the open question — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Three earlier text-chat studies found the same pattern: better perceptions of the counterpart, no attitude change — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Yin, in a different setting, found the same split: feeling heard and feeling connected are "crucial" to distinguish and have different moderators — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)

### Inferences
- PROPOSAL (outcome separation): score listening moves against rapport, "understood", engagement (his message volume) and doubt (defensiveness analogue), and score offers against readiness-to-pay; do not let a rapport gain feed readiness-to-pay by itself. Santoro's data are a direct warning against a model where "he feels heard" is treated as a step toward "he buys".
- PROPOSAL (what carries the sale): in Santoro the thing that moved people was a specific, detailed, emotional narrative with new information. The analogue is the content of Yuna's offer or steer (what is in it, why it is for him), which deserves its own quality criteria in the coach, separate from how well she listened before it.
- The tailored path (listen, then shape the offer to what he said) is exactly what Santoro excluded, so it remains open; the engine can use the reader's "what he is doing / what is new" to shape offers, but that is untested by these papers.
- Listening's measured effect on how much people talk (3x) is the most "engine-relevant" causal result in the three papers: a reliable way to raise engagement volume, which is a prerequisite for anything else even though it is not persuasion.

### Gaps
- No paper tests listening before a purchase request, an emotional appeal in an affectionate relationship, or the effect of listening on returning to the same partner; the persuasion topic (immigration policy, opponents only) is far from a fan deciding to buy.

## Key question 5. Deep talk versus small talk: does listening quality matter less in small talk, and what that means for Yuna's many small-talk turns

### Takeaway
Listening still mattered in small talk, but the behaviour that carried it changed: validation and the overall impression of attentiveness predicted connection, while follow-up questions did not (they only kept the partner's replies quick). Interventions that raised question-asking in deep talk did nothing in small talk. So small-talk turns are not a place to force questions; they are a place for brief, specific acknowledgment and visible attention.

### Cited Findings
- Small talk: global listening -> experimenter resonance beta 0.21, observer resonance 0.34; validation 0.26 and 0.20; follow-up questions 0.06 (p = 0.295) and -0.02 (p = 0.763) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Deep talk: follow-up questions -> partner resonance 0.22, observer 0.13; validation 0.33 and 0.20 — [West PMC Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638239/)
- The authors' explanation: small-talk questions may be "offered out of politeness or as a way to combat potentially sitting in awkward silence", while the disclosure task gave clear topics so questions "more likely ... reflect motivation to connect" — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- In small talk "the global listening behavior emerged as the more consistent predictor"; explicit verbal signals may count most "when accompanied by a range of nonverbal cues" in ambiguous settings — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- No intervention effect on any listening behaviour in small talk; a 23% rise in questions after the intervention in deep talk — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- The small talk itself was 5 minutes or shorter, on mundane topics (the rest of the day, weekend plans, the experimenter's apartment news) — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Validation remained about 3-4 times more frequent than questions in small talk (1.10 vs 0.32 per bin) — [West PMC Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638239/)

### Inferences
- PROPOSAL (mode-dependent scoring): make the value of a "curious question" move depend on the reader's judgment of the moment: high when he has just disclosed something personal (deep-talk analogue), low in small talk where a question that is not about something he said risks reading as filler. The existing question band could then be relaxed in disclosure moments and tightened in small-talk stretches rather than applied uniformly over the last ten replies.
- PROPOSAL (small-talk default): in small talk, the connection-carrying move is a specific reaction or acknowledgment ("that's great", "really", paraphrase of what he said) plus whatever moves the conversation; this is what West's validation code counted, and it worked in both contexts.
- The chat analogue of "global listening" (attention + comprehension + non-judgment, including soft hedges) has no direct measure; the coach's "fits the moment" is the nearest existing dimension.
- The fast-turn marker in small talk suggests that in brief exchanges a quick, short, on-point reply is itself part of the signal, but West measured speech gaps, so this is an analogy, not a finding.

### Gaps
- No text-chat small talk was studied; no data on how many small-talk turns a relationship can sustain before listening behaviours stop mattering; no data on sexual or flirtatious small talk.

## What Yuna could use (all items are PROPOSALS, none implemented)

### Takeaway
The three papers support shifting the engine's listening logic from counting questions to checking relevance and acknowledgment, splitting "comfort" into specific acknowledgments versus reassurance, treating rapport and sales as separate outcome tracks, and expecting the AI label to cap closeness rather than understanding.

### Cited Findings
- The behaviours and exclusions in West's coder sheet — [West SI Section III](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)
- The technique-by-feeling-heard correlations — [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- The listening training sheet and the mechanism table — [Santoro SI Fig. S4](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf); [Santoro Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)
- Label moderators — [Yin SI Tables S3-S4, S9](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)

### Inferences (the proposals)
1. PROPOSAL — Coach label "relevant follow-up question" (West definition): a question that seeks to learn more about, understand or clarify something he said, or asks about his feelings about it, and stays on his topic. Replace the per-ten-replies question band with: a question is good when it is relevant and the moment is disclosure-like; it is neutral-to-bad when it is generic ("how was your day?" after he just told her) or when it arrives in small talk as filler. Keep the "no third question in a row" veto; it does not conflict with anything in the papers.
2. PROPOSAL — Coach label "validation before share" with West's exclusions: at least one statement that reflects, paraphrases, supports, or reacts with interest to his content before Yuna's own share or her question; "haha", "same", "me too", "omg" alone do not count. This formalizes the existing "a share connects, no echo" rule and the "specific detail of his picked up" criterion.
3. PROPOSAL — Scorecard bands: share of replies (after a substantive fan message) with a relevant question or a validation; share of validations that name a specific detail; validation-to-question ratio (West observed roughly 4:1). Compare Yuna against the human chatters' values on these rather than on question frequency alone.
4. PROPOSAL — Hard-mode refinement (Yin): rank acknowledgment moves by what correlated with feeling heard: his feeling is justified; his effort; a quality of his; taking his side; repeating his key phrase. Demote reassurance ("it'll be fine") and generic sympathy ("I'm so sorry") to optional closers, since neither correlated. Keep "no story of her own" (own experiences r = 0.04) and "no unasked advice" (practical support r = -0.02).
5. PROPOSAL — Disclosure vs request split in the reader: tag whether his message discloses or asks; in disclosure, suggestions are a deduction unless asked; in a request, "answers him" requires something practical. (The second half is the engine's own logic, not a paper finding.)
6. PROPOSAL — Separate outcome tracks (Santoro): listening-type moves (react, curious question, get to know him, comfort) are scored against rapport, understood, engagement and doubt; offer-type moves (steer, offer, readiness check) against readiness-to-pay. No automatic transfer from the first group to the second. Judge the offer's content on its own quality (specific, about him, carrying something new), because in Santoro the content did the persuading.
7. PROPOSAL — Engagement expectation (Santoro): expect listening moves to raise his message length and count (participants said about 3x as many words when listened to) and use that as a measurable signal of listening quality in the scorecard, independent of sales.
8. PROPOSAL — Label-aware targets (Yin): keep "understood" and "answers him" as the dimensions expected to match human chatters; set lower expected bands for the fan's declared closeness and trust; keep declared feelings rare, since closeness is the outcome the label discounts most. Have the reader note the fan's stance toward her being an AI (mocking, neutral, curious, accepting) as a moderator the meters can use.
9. PROPOSAL — Small-talk mode (West Study 2): in stretches with no disclosure, lower the score of "curious question" and raise "react" with a specific acknowledgment plus a move forward; reserve follow-up questions for moments when he has given something to follow up on.
10. PROPOSAL — Non-judgment phrasing (West global code, Santoro training): include softening hedges ("maybe", "a bit", "it sounds like") and "I hear that" style acknowledgments as positive-regard markers when he states an opinion she does not share; Santoro's sheet shows one can acknowledge feelings without agreeing with ideas, which fits a persona that should not fake agreement.
11. PROPOSAL — Reply length is not the lever: Yin's AI advantage came at a matched ~55 words; the coach should not reward longer replies for "listening", only more specific ones.

### Gaps
- All proposals extrapolate from spoken conversations (West, Santoro) or single written replies (Yin) to multi-turn chat with a known-AI persona in a commercial, flirtatious context; none of the three papers covers that setting, and no adjacent source was consulted that does.

## What not to infer

### Takeaway
The papers do not license treating question frequency as connection, listening as a sales lever, emotional language as a way around the AI label, reassurance as feeling-heard, or spoken-conversation effect sizes as predictions for chat.

### Cited Findings
- Effects are small: West's intervention raised questions from 1.45 to 2.30 per 10 minutes; the mediation indirect effect was beta = 0.04; the reviewer called the effects "TINY" and inconsistent across studies — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2); [West peer review](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00342-2/MediaObjects/44271_2025_342_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
- Follow-up questions were unrelated to connection in small talk — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- The listener's own sense of connection was not predicted by their listening — [West](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2)
- Listening added nothing to persuasion (upper bound 0.11 SD at five weeks) and did not change interest in another conversation — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/); [Santoro SI Tables S10-S11](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- The label penalty is additive and the same for every technique; no technique escaped it — [Yin SI Tables S1, S9](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Reassurance (r = 0.02), "I'm sorry that happened" (r = 0.07 ns), and expressing caring (r = 0.09 ns) did not correlate with feeling heard — [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Technique correlations are r = 0.09-0.19 across single replies, third-party coded — [Yin SI Table S6](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2319112121/suppl_file/pnas.2319112121.sapp.pdf)
- Yin compared AI to "moderately invested online strangers" (effort 4.30/7) and expects AI to fall short of close others and professionals — [Yin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/)
- Santoro's confederates had 10-15 hours of training and weekly coaching; the authors call it "a generous test" of listening — [Santoro SI](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2421982122/suppl_file/pnas.2421982122.sapp.pdf)
- Defensiveness was about 1.5/5 in all conditions — [Santoro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873821/)

### Inferences
- Do not infer that more questions mean more connection; the evidence is for relevant questions in disclosure moments and for validation generally, and even then the effects are small.
- Do not infer that better listening will raise readiness to pay; the only causal test of listening on a persuasive outcome is null, and three earlier text-chat studies agree.
- Do not infer that a known-AI persona can offset the label by saying more emotional things; the label penalty did not interact with any response feature, and the label specifically raised creepiness and ambivalence a little.
- Do not infer that the fan's closeness or trust ratings should match a human chatter's; connection was the outcome the label hit hardest, and it depends on mind perception, not on reply quality.
- Do not infer that "it'll be okay", "I'm so sorry" or "I care about you" make him feel heard; the correlated moves are specific acknowledgments of his feelings, effort, qualities and side.
- Do not infer that listening lowers his doubt much when doubt is already low; Santoro's defensiveness floor effect means the -0.23 SD may understate or overstate what happens in a tense chat.
- Do not infer that Yuna's own "rapport" meter rises because she listened; West found the listener's own felt connection unchanged; what rose was the partner's and the observers'.
- Do not infer chat latency effects from West's turn-gap results; those are speech gaps in face-to-face talk.
- Do not infer that backchannel-style tokens ("haha", "omg", emoji) count as listening; West's coders excluded their spoken equivalents, and nonverbal cues could not even be coded reliably in isolation.
- Do not infer that these effect sizes transfer: all three settings differ from Fanvue in medium (spoken or single written reply), relationship (strangers, one-off), motive (no money, no flirtation) and population (US adults; West mostly young women).

### Gaps
- No source in this set covers text chat with a persistent AI persona, repeated interactions, sexual or romantic framing, or purchases; adjacent literature was not pulled in, per the brief's instruction to stay with the three papers unless a gap required it, and the gaps above are noted rather than filled.
