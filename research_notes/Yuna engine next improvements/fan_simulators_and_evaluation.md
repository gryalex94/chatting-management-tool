# LLM user simulators and automated evaluation for testing an AI creator chat persona at scale

Research date: 21 October 2026. Scope: how teams build simulated users (persona-driven, adversarial, mood-shifting) to test and tune chat agents; how realistic those simulators are versus real users; how to calibrate them; how to evaluate dialogue agents automatically (LLM-as-judge, pairwise, outcome proxies) including biases and sample sizes; and what exists for companion/romance/adult and sales/persuasion chat. Target setting: a persona chat engine (AI creator chatting with fans on an adult subscription platform, flirting, sexting, selling PPV) whose decisions are made in code from meters (heat, mood, rapport, readiness to pay, doubt, engagement, surprise), whose replies are written by a Mistral model, and which is tested today with 37 hand-written dialogues plus a Claude judge.

Method note: ~45 web searches and full-text fetches of arXiv HTML pages, one Google Research blog, one Hugging Face model card and several vendor pages. Where only an abstract or a search snippet was read, that is marked. Numbers are quoted as reported by the fetched text; where a paper's own tables and text disagree, both are given.

## Key Question 1: What are the strongest 2023–2026 approaches to LLM user simulation for dialogue testing and policy learning, and what do they get right and wrong versus real users?

### Takeaway
The field moved from prompt-only "play a user" simulators (2023–24) to simulators trained on real logs (UserLM-8b, USP, DIAL, PersonaForge), adversarially or evolutionarily tuned to be indistinguishable from humans (DIAL, PPol), and deliberately non-cooperative "hard user" simulators (ChatChecker, non-collaborative simulators, UserLM-R1). The consistent finding across every paper that compared simulators to real users is that prompted assistant-LLMs are too cooperative, too polite, too verbose, too homogeneous, rarely end conversations, and almost never walk away — so they inflate agent scores, and they are worst exactly on the users who do not convert.

### Cited Findings

**Surveys and taxonomy**
- The EACL 2026 survey "A Survey on LLM-based Conversational User Simulation" (Ni et al., Adobe Research/Vanderbilt and others, published March 2026) organises the field by user granularity and simulation objective; techniques covered include demographic prompting, psychometric modelling with Big Five/HEXACO, trait-infused architectures, activation-level control, and role-play via prompting, fine-tuning and self-play; evaluation is grouped into traditional metrics, human evaluation and LLM-as-judge — [EACL 2026 survey](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-eacl/2026.eacl-long.200); [Adobe Research listing](https://research.adobe.com/publication/a-survey-on-llm-based-conversational-user-simulation) (abstract/snippet level only)

**Multi-turn social-simulation benchmarks**
- SOTOPIA (ICLR 2024): 40 agents, 90 scenarios, 450 tasks; SOTOPIA-Eval scores seven dimensions (Goal 0–10, Believability 0–10, Knowledge 0–10, Secret −10–0, Relationship −5–5, Social rules −10–0, Financial −5–5). GPT-4 as judge correlated with humans at Pearson r = 0.71 on Goal completion for model outputs (0.78 for human outputs), 0.62 Financial, 0.56 Relationship, 0.45 Believability, 0.33 Social rules, 0.33 Knowledge, 0.22 Secret; human inter-annotator agreement was moderate (Randolph's κ = 0.503, 200 episodes). On the 20-task SOTOPIA-hard subset GPT-4 scored 4.85 on goal completion vs humans 5.95 (interacting with GPT-4) and 6.15 (human–human), p < 0.05 — [SOTOPIA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11667)
- LIFELONG-SOTOPIA (June 2025) extends to multi-episode interactions and reports that goal achievement and believability of all tested models decline through the whole interaction; an advanced memory method helps but agents still complete goals significantly less than humans on history-dependent scenarios (abstract only; no numbers on the page) — [LIFELONG-SOTOPIA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12666)
- SocialBench (ACL 2024 Findings): 500 characters, 6,000+ prompts, 30,800 multi-turn role-play utterances; agents good at individual-level sociality are not necessarily good at group level, and agents drift under the influence of other agents — [SocialBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13679)
- τ²-bench (Sierra, June 2025): the user simulator is gpt-4.1; a manual audit of traces found user-simulator errors in 47% of 100 airline conversations (13% critical, 34% benign), 40% of 50 retail (12% critical), 16% of 50 telecom (6% critical); "critical" errors prevent task completion. The paper names three sources of benchmark uncertainty: implementation errors, task-specification errors and user-simulator errors. pass^k (fraction of k independent runs that succeed, 4 runs at temperature 0) is used because single-run success is unreliable; gpt-4.1 pass^1 was 74% retail, 56% airline, 34% telecom — [τ²-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07982); original [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045)

**Simulators trained on real logs**
- UserLM-8b (Microsoft Research / Georgia Tech, Oct 2025, revised Mar 2026): Llama3-8b-Base fine-tuned on 343,951 deduplicated WildChat conversations (1,047,930 training samples), taking only a "task intent" and emitting first turns, follow-ups and an end-of-conversation token. Versus prompted assistant simulators it has far lower perplexity on held-out real user turns (7.42 vs 36.29 for Llama3-8b-Instruct on PRISM; 4.33 vs 21.40 on WildChat), first-turn lexical diversity matching real users (94.55% unique 1-grams vs 94.01% real vs 74.42% GPT-4o), more gradual intent reveal (1-gram overlap with intent 2.69% vs 1.68% human vs 7.68% GPT-4o), and far better conversation-termination F1 (63.54 vs 1.38 for GPT-4o and 15.31 for GPT-4o-mini). Prompted assistants reverted to their assistant role in 20–60% of conversations. When GPT-4o was the assistant in 650 coding/maths simulations, its score fell from 74.6 (GPT-4o-simulated user) to 57.4 (UserLM-8b user) — [UserLM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06552); [model card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/UserLM-8b)
- USP, "Know You First and Be You Better" (ACL 2025): infers implicit profiles (objective facts + Big Five + language style) from 94,874 English LMSYS-Chat-1M conversations with a GPT-4o extractor, trains LLaMA-3-8B with conditional SFT then PPO with a cycle-consistency reward (0.8 × profile similarity + 0.2 × AI-detection), and samples diverse profiles via SimCSE + UMAP + Gaussian KDE (1,000 seeds → ~1M virtual profiles). Human evaluation (100 samples, 8 annotators, κ ≈ 0.55): authenticity win/tie/loss 74/13/13 vs GPT-4o-with-profile and 55/12/33 vs PlatoLM; premature-ending rate (ESR) 10 vs 32 for GPT-4o-with-profile — [USP](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18968v4)
- DIAL (Slingshot AI, with Cambridge and NYU; Dec 2025, v4 May 2026), built for the deployed "Ash" mental-health chatbot: Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct LoRA-SFT on 10K/40K/160K real anonymised sessions, then iterated against a Llama-3.1-8B token-level discriminator (trained on 1,600 balanced sessions, evaluated on 400) with DPO in discrete iterations. Discriminator accuracy fell 87.9% (SFT) → 76.5% → 74.0% → 69.5% (DIAL-it3); humans' success at spotting the simulator fell 67% → 55%. Lexical diversity moved toward real users (TTR .382 → .461 vs real .499; distinct-2 .728 → .841 vs .872); SFT beyond 40K sessions caused mode collapse (TTR .430 at 40K → .382 at 160K). Zero-shot (prompt-only) simulators produced "unrealistically compliant users" with much lower issue rates than real conversations, over-represented positive mental-health indicators, and clustered apart from real sessions in embedding space — [DIAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20773v4)
- PersonaForge (Peking University / Xiaomi / HKU / Renmin, Aug 2026): builds personas by "reverse deep construction" from WildChat and LMSYS seed queries (70 occupations, 16 MBTI types steering style, 3 proficiency levels, 25 knowledge domains with blind spots; communication-style settings for message length, formality, emoji; fixed rules: short messages, one question per turn, termination signal). Reported realism stats are thin: real multi-turn sessions average 25.8 user turns vs 9.3 in the retained corpus; real follow-ups have a median of 82 characters vs ~100 for synthetic. Training agents on PersonaForge data beat training on real-user replay (80.3% vs 75.6%). Swapping the simulator backbone (Claude Opus 4.6 → GPT-5.5) changed an untrained reference agent's score from 88.2% to 79.7% — [PersonaForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28378)

**Adversarial, non-cooperative and evolved "hard user" simulators**
- ChatChecker (TU Munich / Cambridge, July 2025): GPT-4o-generated personas with demographics, Big Five, interaction style and a task; "challenging" personas are impolite, sarcastic, vague, change intent or contradict themselves. Challenging personas surfaced far more breakdowns than standard ones (goal-setting assistant: 30.8 vs 4.2 total breakdowns; AutoTOD: 25.2 vs 22.6) and more crashes (4.0 vs 1.6). Its GPT-4o breakdown detector reached F1 0.764 (English DBDC5) / 0.832 (Japanese); its dialogue rater correlated with humans at Spearman 0.683 on open-domain FED but only 0.22–0.27 on task-oriented sets; error-type classification was weak (exact match 18.8%) — [ChatChecker](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16792)
- Non-Collaborative User Simulators for Tool Agents (Seoul National University, 2025, v5 Mar 2026): four behaviour types — unavailable service, tangential talk (with complaints if ignored), impatience (anger probability rises per trigger and persists once expressed), incomplete/truncated utterances — on top of a GPT-4.1-mini collaborative simulator with a dialogue-state tracker and an "ending verifier" that blocks premature <END>. Nine annotators preferred it over a prompt baseline ~70% of the time. Relative success-rate drops: tangential ~29%, incomplete utterances ~18%, unavailable service ~12%, impatience ~11% (impatience partly blunted by excessive apologising) — [Non-collaborative simulators](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23124)
- PPol, "Beyond Cooperative Simulators" (U. Washington / Georgetown, 2026, v3 Oct 2026): evolves persona-generator programs with OpenEvolve (Gemini 3 Flash mutations, MAP-Elites archive) against a fitness = human-likeness (a Random-Forest discriminator over 19 behavioural "fingerprint" features, not an LLM judge) + behavioural coverage (two-sided Chamfer distance to the real-human cloud). Fitness gains of 28–72 percentage points over the baseline simulator (τ²-bench retail 0.077 → 0.693; WildChat 0.038 → 0.762). In a blinded Prolific study (16 annotators, 87 conversations) PPol users were judged "human" 80.4% of the time vs 80.0% for real humans and 46.5% for the default τ²-bench simulator; discriminator scores correlated with human judgments (point-biserial r = 0.49). Agents trained with PPol users had 36.4% vs 29.5% live task success with real humans (odds ratio 2.06) and 45.2% fewer unwanted actions — [PPol](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12894)
- UserLM-R1 (Jan 2026) trains a user LM with multi-reward RL and tests on an adversarial set that escalates from profile consistency to reasoning under pressure and resisting cognitive manipulation (search snippet only) — [UserLM-R1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09215)
- "Robust Critics" (July 2026) builds a multi-turn adversarial simulator that pressures a defended system over several turns and notes "no standardized framework for multi-step user simulation exists yet" (snippet only) — [Robust Critics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20472)

**Emotion / mood-shifting simulators**
- EmoUS (SIGIR 2023, HHU Düsseldorf): generates user emotion, semantic action and natural-language reply from goal, history and persona; motivated by the claim that simulators with generic user policies "may result in a high drop-off rate when deployed in the real world"; proposed as a probe of which system behaviours provoke which user emotions (abstract level) — [EmoUS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01579)
- An earlier emotion-aware task-oriented simulator (2020) terminates the dialogue when negative-emotion intensity passes a threshold with a set termination probability, and reports that termination settings change how dialogues unfold (snippet level) — [Emotion-aware user simulator](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09696)

**What simulators get wrong versus real users (cross-paper evidence)**
- Over-cooperation and positivity: Mehri et al. (UIUC / Microsoft Research / UC Berkeley, May 2026) compared 24 simulators to ~5,000 real WildChat coding and ~5,000 writing conversations using Jensen–Shannon divergence over k-means-quantised behaviour embeddings (k = 500) across six facets (requests, responses, context, communication style, DAMSL and SGD dialog acts). Real-vs-real baseline JS was .028 (coding) / .020 (writing); the best simulators (Gemini 3.1 Pro .246, GPT-5.4 .248) were an order of magnitude further away; worst was Qwen3.5-2B (.608). Simulators systematically missed terse, transactional, directive, authoritative messages and hallucinated excessive positivity and pleasantries ("thanks awesome", "formal pleasantries"). A linear classifier separated real from simulated embeddings with 90.9–99.6% accuracy; scaling helped only in the Gemma and gpt-oss families (Llama-3.1-8B beat Llama-3.3-70B) — [Distributional gap paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07847)
- Hyper-activity and homogenisation on real behaviour traces: OmniBehavior (Chinese Academy of Sciences / Kuaishou, Apr–May 2026) used 200 real Kuaishou users with 2.12M logged interactions over Sept–Nov 2025. Real positive-action rates are below 10%, but Qwen3-235B and Gemini-3-Flash overestimate engagement by 40–60%; simulated customer-service utterances are more positive and polite than real ones and miss negative emotions; real users' inter-user/intra-user variation ratio is ≈0.29 while simulated users sit at 0.7–0.87 ("persona homogenisation"); simulated interest drift 0.17 vs real 0.63. Best overall model score was 44.55/100 (Claude-Opus-4.5) — [OmniBehavior](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08362)
- Never walking away: "Simulated Customers Never Walk Away" (Liang Chen, June 2026) measured decision fidelity against 2,790 production sales conversations of a deployed LLM sales agent on a Chinese matchmaking platform with verified payments (793 converted). On a 374-conversation evaluation subset (1,109 matched probes), a Claude profile-conditioned simulator over-engaged non-buyers (depth bias +0.396 on a 0–4 scale) but matched buyers (+0.092); Δ = +0.304 (Cohen's d 0.38, p = 0.0002). Real non-buyers were "resisting" 25.1% of the time vs 13.5% simulated and "considering" 21.9% vs 40.1%; after a sales pitch, "considering" rose from 45.3% real to 75.2% simulated (+29.9 pp); the gap grew with conversation position (Δ +0.054 at 30% of turns → +0.706 at 90%). Swapping to DeepSeek-V4-Flash did not fix it (Δ +0.330). An explicit "you may disengage" instruction cut marginal bias five-fold (+0.24 → −0.04) but left Δ almost intact (+0.285) and made buyers over-resistant (−0.187). The simulator did not fabricate purchases ("deciding" 4.3% in both) — [Never Walk Away](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20708)
- Politeness under bad service: Google's ConvApparel (Apr 2026; 4,000+ real apparel-shopping conversations, ~15,000 turns, with participants secretly routed to a "Good" or a "Bad" agent and rating satisfaction/frustration/purchase likelihood every turn) found the prompt-only simulator "unnaturally polite and patient" with the Bad agent; retrieval-augmented few-shot and SFT (Gemini 2.5 Flash) simulators did show more frustration and rejection, but a trained discriminator still "confidently identified nearly all simulated conversations as synthetic", including the best SFT model — [ConvApparel blog](https://research.google/blog/convapparel-measuring-and-bridging-the-realism-gap-in-user-simulators/)
- Persona flattening: a 2025 review of 21 LLM social-simulation studies found LLM agents act as an "average persona", with lower diversity than human populations when variance was assessed at all — [LLM-Based Social Simulations Require a Boundary](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19806v2); a 2026 "silicon sampling" paper calls under-dispersion the recurring finding and cites a mega-study where simulated-twin SD < human SD in 154 of 164 outputs (snippet level) — [Distribution-First Population Simulation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18310); Hu & Collier (ACL 2024) found persona variables explain <10% of annotation variance in subjective NLP datasets, with persona prompting giving modest but significant gains (snippet level) — [Quantifying the Persona Effect](https://preview.aclanthology.org/dois-2013-emnlp/2024.acl-long.554); one late-2025 preprint counters that richer, more detailed persona profiles bring personality distributions closer to human norms (single-team claim) — [Scaling Law in LLM Simulated Personality](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11734)
- Sycophancy toward the agent: the Fudan "Calibrated Interactive RL" paper (May 2026) describes prompt-based simulators as tending "to blindly accept incorrect agent outputs or prematurely leak ground-truth information", which makes RL loops trivial and exploitable (qualitative, no rates) — [Calibrated Interactive RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26403v1)
- Human-vs-simulator distinguishability in socially driven dialogue (June 2025): on 50 human-supporter vs simulator-supporter pairs, 3 annotators labelled 48% / 32% / 20% as human / simulator / cannot identify (the mapping of percentages to labels is the paper's order, inferred); the authors say LLM simulators are adequate only for the specific scenarios studied, not for modelling all user behaviour — [Engagement alignment paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21497)

**Industry tooling (for orientation; no published validation numbers)**
- LivePerson launched a "Conversation Simulator" with synthetic customer personas in Nov 2025 — [press release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/35956912/liveperson-launches-conversation-simulator-to-de-risk-generative-ai-and-accelerate-ai-adoption); Moveo runs simulations as pass/fail regression tests with one simulated message at a time through the production pipeline — [Moveo docs](https://docs.moveo.ai/docs/ai-agents/simulations); FutureAGI defines tests by scenario + persona and outputs transcripts plus scores — [FutureAGI docs](https://docs.futureagi.com/docs/simulation); Google ADK's user-sim caps turns and uses a stop signal — [ADK user simulation](https://adk.dev/evaluate/user-sim/); Cekura's voice-AI simulation post argues that if simulated callers do not behave like real people "every downstream metric becomes unreliable" — [Cekura blog](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/voice-ai-simulation)

### Inferences
- For a fan-chat engine the failure modes documented above map directly: a prompted "fan" will be too warm, too long-winded, too easily moved to "considering" after a PPV pitch, will rarely go silent or leave, and will under-represent terse, one-word, cold or hostile fans. Any success rate measured against such fans is an upper bound (the "Never Walk Away" authors say this explicitly for sales).
- The strongest designs combine three things: (1) conditioning on real-log-derived profiles (USP/PersonaForge style), (2) a discriminator or distribution check against real transcripts (DIAL/PPol/Mehri), and (3) deliberately injected non-cooperative behaviours with persistent state (impatience that sticks, tangents, truncated messages, explicit disengagement). None of the papers reached parity with humans on a discriminator test except via adversarial training (DIAL) or evolved personas (PPol).
- Because the engine's decisions are made from meters, the simulator's job is mostly to reproduce the *distribution of fan states and transitions* (resisting / considering / deciding; cooling off; leaving) rather than perfect wording. The sales-decision paper's stage-distribution comparison is the closest template.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that simulates users of an adult/creator chat platform or of flirtatious/sexual conversation; all simulator validation data are coding/writing, customer support, retail, mental health or matchmaking sales.
- A search-engine summary claimed an external audit found the gpt-4o τ-bench airline simulator deviated from instructions in 11 of 50 conversations (22%); the source paper could not be identified from the fetched texts, so treat as unverified.
- PersonaForge's "Blind Realism Study" (Appendix I) was not in the fetched text; its realism numbers are unknown.
- Lifelong-Sotopia's numeric decline per episode was not retrieved (abstract only).

## Key Question 2: How do teams validate that a simulator behaves like the real population, and what calibration methods are reported?

### Takeaway
Reported validation falls into four families: (a) distributional statistics against real logs (perplexity, lexical diversity, turn counts, dialog-act / behaviour-cluster divergence, termination F1), (b) discriminator or human "Turing" tests, (c) outcome-level checks (do simulated failure or purchase-state rates track real ones across system versions; does the simulator reproduce the direction of a real A/B test), and (d) counterfactual sensitivity (does the simulator get frustrated with a deliberately bad agent). Calibration methods that moved the needle were SFT on real transcripts, adversarial/discriminator refinement, profile-distribution sampling from real logs, and ensembling behaviourally complementary simulators; prompting alone did not close the gap.

### Cited Findings

**Distribution matching against real logs**
- Mehri et al. (May 2026) propose extracting behaviour representations, clustering into discrete distributions and computing forward KL (recall), backward KL (precision) and JS divergence; the metric was validated by a human odd-one-out task (25 triplets, 15 annotators, 86.7% correct, Fleiss κ 0.74) and rankings were stable across embedding models and clustering choices (Spearman ρ 0.80–0.98). Combining behaviourally complementary simulators reduced the gap more than either alone (Gemini 3.1 Pro + GPT-5.4: coding JS .246 → .226; gemma-4-31B + UserLM-8b: .297 → .270), while pairing similar simulators did not help — [Distributional gap paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07847)
- UserLM reports perplexity on held-out real user turns, first-turn unique-1-gram rate, intent-overlap (gradual reveal), termination F1, and per-instruction turn-count variance across 10 runs (UserLM 2.8, range 2.1–6.7 turns; GPT-4o 0.6, range 4.0–5.4) as realism metrics — [UserLM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06552)
- ConvApparel's three pillars: population-level statistical alignment (conversation length, words per turn, dialog acts such as rejecting a recommendation), a trained "human-likeness score" discriminator, and counterfactual validation (a simulator trained only on Good-agent data must show more frustration and lower satisfaction with the unseen Bad agent, as humans did); 600 simulated conversations per simulator (300 per agent) — [ConvApparel blog](https://research.google/blog/convapparel-measuring-and-bridging-the-realism-gap-in-user-simulators/)
- PPol's fitness uses a Random-Forest human-likeness discriminator on 19 behavioural fingerprint features plus a coverage term (Chamfer distance) explicitly to stop the generator drifting into discriminator blind spots ("adversarial drift"); the discriminator's scores correlated with blinded human judgments at r = 0.49 — [PPol](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12894)
- PersonaForge compares only coarse stats (real 25.8 user turns per session vs 9.3; median follow-up 82 vs ~100 characters) — [PersonaForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28378)

**Outcome-level validation (the strongest evidence that a simulator is useful)**
- DIAL correlated simulated issue rates with real production issue rates across 14 chatbot configurations (Qwen, Llama and GPT families): SFT-only r = 0.528 (p = 0.052), DIAL-it1 r = 0.685, DIAL-it2 r = 0.818 (p = 0.0003; 0.8302 in the appendix figure), DIAL-it3 r = 0.781; aggregate of all simulators r = 0.801. KL divergence between simulated and real issue-category distributions fell from 0.671 (SFT) to 0.148 (DIAL-it3). In an A/B-test replication the simulator predicted that a DPO "fix" would *raise* issue rates for all three models; production matched the direction for Model A (2.71% → 3.56%, p = 0.001) and Model B (2.32% → 2.64%, n.s.) but not Model C (1.54% → 1.42%, n.s.), so the paper's "consistent directional trends" claim holds for two of three. The authors suggest ensembling multiple simulators to reduce rollout noise — [DIAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20773v4)
- "Never Walk Away" proposes *outcome-conditioned* decision fidelity: compare simulated vs real engagement depth separately for real buyers and real non-buyers, and use Δ (non-buyer bias − buyer bias) → 0 as the target while keeping the buyer stratum matched; measure action-conditioned responses (after pitch / after push-close / after neutral probe) and bias as a function of conversation position. Recommendations: treat simulator success rates as upper bounds; do not rely on prompting; learn from real non-buyer trajectories or gate cooperativeness with an external willingness model; validate decision-state labels with human κ — [Never Walk Away](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20708)
- τ²-bench's practice: have two annotators manually audit a sample of simulator traces (100 airline, 50 retail, 50 telecom) and classify simulator errors as critical vs benign before trusting scores — [τ²-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07982)

**Calibration methods reported**
- SFT on real transcripts + adversarial DPO iterations (DIAL): discriminator accuracy 87.9% → 69.5%; the authors note SFT alone over-fits/mode-collapses past 40K sessions and that DPO with preference pairs regenerated in discrete iterations limits reward hacking compared with a continuously optimised reward — [DIAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20773v4)
- Profile-distribution sampling (USP): infer profiles from real logs, fit a KDE over profile embeddings and sample virtual profiles so the simulator population matches the real profile distribution; RL with cycle consistency keeps utterances consistent with the sampled profile — [USP](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18968v4)
- Retrieval-augmented few-shot examples from real transcripts per turn (ConvApparel "ICL") and SFT on real transcripts both beat prompt-only on statistical alignment and counterfactual sensitivity, but neither closed the discriminator gap — [ConvApparel blog](https://research.google/blog/convapparel-measuring-and-bridging-the-realism-gap-in-user-simulators/)
- Simulator SFT as a calibration phase before RL (Fudan, May 2026): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct trained by NLL on user turns conditioned on the ground-truth solution; important caveat — the "calibration" data were 1,860 (MATH-Chat) and 16,028 (MediumDocEdit-Chat) trajectories generated by a Qwen3-235B oracle simulator, not humans, and all evaluation used an LLM simulator/judge on 200 held-out samples; no empirical sim-to-real gap value is reported — [Calibrated Interactive RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26403v1)
- Prompt optimisation for CRS user simulators (May 2026, ICDE workshops) targets "systematic positive bias, data leakage, and limited behavioral diversity" and reports improved alignment with human interaction patterns (abstract only, no numbers) — [CRS prompt optimisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00010)
- Explicit "you may disengage" instructions: removed marginal over-engagement (+0.24 → −0.04) but not the outcome-conditioned bias, and shifted the error onto buyers — [Never Walk Away](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20708)
- Simulator diversity (temperature, heterogeneous backbones, imperfect behaviour, diverse personas) is recommended to prevent the trained policy from exploiting one simulator's phrasing — [LOGIGEN](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00540)

### Inferences
- A practical calibration protocol for the fan population, assembled from the above: (1) from real chat logs compute per-fan-turn statistics — reply length distribution, question rate, emoji/one-word rate, response latency if available, turns to first purchase, share of sessions that end with silence, resisting/considering/deciding stage rates after a pitch; (2) run the simulator through the *same* engine version that produced those logs and compare the same statistics (JS divergence over behaviour clusters if embeddings are available, otherwise simple KS tests per statistic); (3) stratify by real outcome (bought / did not buy / churned) and require the non-buyer stratum to match, since that is where simulators fail; (4) run a counterfactual: a deliberately worse engine (e.g., pitch on turn 1, ignore the fan's message) must produce measurably colder fans and more exits; (5) once two or more engine versions have real A/B data, check that the simulator ranks them in the same order.
- Mixing two or three different simulator backbones (e.g., a Mistral-based fan and a Claude-based fan, or a log-fine-tuned small model plus a prompted frontier model) is a cheap, evidence-backed way to narrow the distribution gap.
- The single most valuable asset is a few thousand real fan sessions with outcomes; every method that reached meaningful realism (DIAL, UserLM, USP, PPol, ConvApparel SFT/ICL) used real transcripts.

### Gaps
- No paper reports calibration of simulated *purchase* rates to real purchase rates (the matchmaking-sales paper measured decision *stages*, not purchase conversion, and did not test fine-tuning).
- No paper reports calibrating simulated reply latency, session gaps, or multi-day return behaviour for chat; OmniBehavior covers long-horizon app behaviour but not chat.
- How many real sessions are "enough" for SFT calibration is only indirectly known (DIAL: 10K–160K sessions, with diminishing returns and mode collapse beyond 40K; USP: ~88K conversations).

## Key Question 3: What is known about LLM-as-judge for chat quality — biases, agreement with humans, best rubric/comparison designs, and sample sizes?

### Takeaway
Strong LLM judges agree with humans at roughly human–human levels on *pairwise* chat comparisons (GPT-4: 85% vs 81% human–human on MT-Bench, ties excluded) but carry large, judge-specific position bias (GPT-4 consistent in only 65% of swapped pairs), severe verbosity bias in weaker judges, measurable self-preference, over-rating of persuasiveness and of agents that were optimised against the same judge, and poor reliability on implicit harms and on subjective social dimensions. The designs with the best evidence are: pairwise over pointwise, both-order swaps with inconsistent verdicts counted as ties, a verified human reference (κ 0.47 → 0.85 for GPT-4o pairwise), per-instance binary rubrics (36% → 94% agreement in multi-turn), and judges from a different model family than the one being judged. Detecting a 5-point win-rate shift needs on the order of 800 judged pairs with a perfect judge and ~1,600 with an 85%-accurate one; 37 dialogues can only detect very large effects.

### Cited Findings

**Agreement with humans**
- MT-Bench / Chatbot Arena study (Zheng et al., 2023; ~3K expert votes from 58 labellers and ~30K crowd votes): GPT-4 agreed with expert humans 66% with ties (1,343 votes) and 85% without ties (859 pairwise votes) on turn 1, and 66%/85% on turn 2; human–human agreement was 63%/81% (turn 1) and 67%/82% (turn 2). On Arena data, GPT-4 pairwise agreed 64% with ties (3,066 votes) and 87% without (1,944); single-answer grading 60%/85% — [MT-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685)
- Judge's Verdict (NVIDIA, Oct 2025): 54 judges; human–human Cohen's κ averaged 0.801; 27 judges reached "tier 1" (Pearson r ≥ 0.80 with human consensus), of which 23 were "human-like" (|z| < 1 relative to human–human κ, e.g., Qwen2.5-72B κ 0.785, Llama-3.3-70B 0.786, gemini-2.5-flash-lite 0.777) and 4 "super-consistent" (κ > humans, e.g., gemma-3-27b 0.812), which the authors say may mean either reliability or over-simplification — [Judge's Verdict](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09738v1)
- Social-interaction judging (SOTOPIA): GPT-4 vs human Pearson r 0.71 on goal completion but only 0.45 on believability, 0.33 on social rules, 0.22 on secret-keeping; GPT-4 often rated social-rule and secret dimensions higher than humans — [SOTOPIA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11667)
- Companion-chat judging (AICompanionBench, U. South Florida, June 2026; 2,123 Reddit-posted Replika conversations, nine harm categories, sexual behaviour ≈48% of labels): κ between the 20-model majority vote and initial human labels was 0.59; accuracy against final ground truth ranged from 26% (Llama-3-8B) to 86% (GPT-4o), with GPT-5.4 and Claude-opus-4.6 at 83%; all 20 judges had precision below 80% on "manipulation", 17 of 20 below 80% on self-harm/suicide; false-positive rates ranged from 3% to 90% (Mistral-medium-3), e.g., role-play flagged as physical aggression; reasoning variants did not consistently beat base models — [AICompanionBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04867)
- Companion-transcript labelling (USC, Aug 2026): a zero-shot Gemini 3 Flash annotator agreed with two authors at κ 0.70 and 0.59 (authors agreed with each other at κ 0.66) on 300 stratified exchanges; "corrective friction" responses were under-detected (recall 0.29–0.34), and a Claude Opus cross-check agreed with Gemini on 86.5% of vulnerability states and 75.6% of response categories — [When Chatbots Accommodate](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04431)
- Persona-grounded companion safety study (Seattle U., Apr 2026): GPT-5 few-shot labels matched humans 72% exactly on persona multi-labels, 84% on response types (100 pairs) and 86.8% on safety labels (250 pairs); the PACE critic reached F1 0.91–0.96 vs human raters (inter-annotator κ 0.68) — [Persona-grounded safety evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00227)
- PersonaGym / PersonaScore (2024): a rubric-anchored, two-judge ensemble (GPT-4o + Llama-3-70B) reported 75.1% Spearman and 62.7% Kendall-τ correlation with five expert annotators (Fleiss κ 0.71); figures come from a secondary summary, not verified against the paper — [PersonaGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18416v3)
- ChatChecker's GPT-4o dialogue rater: Spearman 0.683 with humans on open-domain FED but 0.22–0.27 on task-oriented datasets — [ChatChecker](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16792)

**Position bias**
- GPT-4 with the default prompt was consistent across swapped orders in 65.0% of pairs, biased to the first position in 30.0% and to the second in 5.0%; the conservative fix is to judge both orders and count disagreement as a tie (or randomise order) — [MT-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685)
- "Judging the Judges" (v9, Nov 2025; 15 judges, 22 tasks, ~40 candidate models, >100K instances): position consistency ranged 0.70–0.82 for GPT models on MT-Bench (GPT-4 0.82), 0.57–0.82 for Claude (Claude-3.5-Sonnet highest; Claude-3-Haiku 0.23 on DevBench, the lowest), 0.57–0.67 Gemini, 0.69–0.80 Llama (Llama-3.3-70B 0.89 on DevBench). Consistency rises with the quality gap between the two answers (parabolic relationship, holding for every judge); prompt input length was not a significant predictor and output length only weakly so. Majority voting across several capable judges is suggested as useful for >95% of instances — [Judging the Judges](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v9)

**Verbosity / length bias**
- In a "repetitive list" attack (23 answers padded with rephrased lists), Claude-v1 and GPT-3.5 were fooled 91.3% of the time, GPT-4 8.7% — [MT-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685)
- The LLMs-as-Judges survey (Dec 2024) defines verbosity bias as favouring longer responses and position bias as favouring a sequence position (definitional; no new numbers) — [LLMs-as-Judges survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05579)

**Self-preference / self-enhancement**
- MT-Bench: GPT-4 appeared to favour its own answers by ~10% higher win rate than humans gave it, Claude-v1 by ~25%, but the authors say the data were too limited to conclude — [MT-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685)
- Panickssery, Bowman & Feng (NeurIPS 2024 oral): GPT-4 and Llama 2 can recognise their own text with non-trivial accuracy; fine-tuning for self-recognition (>90% accuracy for GPT-3.5 and Llama 2) raised self-preference linearly, and controls for length, vowel count and readability did not explain it — [LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076v1)
- Wataoka et al. (NeurIPS 2024 Safe GenAI workshop): GPT-4 shows significant self-preference; LLM judges give higher scores to lower-perplexity (more familiar) text whether or not they wrote it — [Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819)
- "No Free Labels": self-generated references amplify self-preference relative to no reference — [No Free Labels](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05061v1)

**Judge over-rating under optimisation and in persuasion**
- SOTOPIA-π (2024): training a 7B model with behaviour cloning + self-reinforcement filtered by GPT-4 goal-completion ratings widened the GPT-4-vs-human gap from 0.36 (base Mistral-7B: 3.25 auto vs 2.89 human) to 1.42 (BC+SR: 5.71 vs 4.29); GPT-4 expert's gap was 0.64 (5.89 vs 5.25). The authors state the gap "increases as our method optimizes GPT-4 rated goal completion scores during training" (28 hard episodes per setting, 2 qualified annotators each, 34-person pool, ~20% re-annotated for disagreement) — [SOTOPIA-π](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08715)
- "Persuasion Should be Double-Blind" (Feb 2025): a direct "do you feel persuaded?" LLM judge rated human P4G dialogues 88.87, GPT-4-generated DailyPersuasion 90.75; a causal-theory-of-mind judge (infer the persuadee's beliefs/desires, then check whether they were addressed) dropped DailyPersuasion to 54.80 (−35.95 points) but humans only to 79.87 (−9). LLM-generated dialogues also scored *higher* than human ones on coherence (4.97 vs 4.29) and helpfulness (4.87 vs 3.86); the authors suspect GPT evaluators favour larger-model text — [Persuasion Should be Double-Blind](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21297v1)
- Reward-model over-optimisation (Gao, Schulman & Hilton, Oct 2022): optimising a proxy reward model too hard reduces true (gold) reward, consistent with Goodhart's law, for both RL and best-of-n; the effect scales smoothly with reward-model size (abstract only) — [Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)

**Rubric and comparison designs that work best**
- Pairwise vs pointwise: MT-Bench reports higher human agreement for GPT-4 pairwise (87% w/o ties) than single-answer grading (85%) on Arena data; "No Free Labels" found pairwise with human references reached κ 0.85–0.87 vs 0.63–0.78 for single grading — [MT-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685); [No Free Labels](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05061v1)
- Human reference answers (Kensho/MIT, Mar 2025; 1,200 responses, 3 annotators each, maths/reasoning + business/finance): GPT-4o pairwise κ with humans rose from 0.47 (no reference) to 0.64 (self-generated reference) to 0.85 (human-written reference); Llama-3.3-70B 0.39 → 0.52 → 0.87. A weaker judge with human references beat GPT-4o with its own references in single grading (0.63 vs 0.52). On questions the judge itself answers wrongly, κ with self-references collapsed to 0.14 but was 0.80 with human references. Wrong references actively hurt, so verify them — [No Free Labels](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05061v1)
- Response-adapted references (ReviseEval, ICLR 2025 submission): agreement improves as reference–response similarity rises, but a fixed reference unfairly penalises valid answers that differ from it — [ReviseEval](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2025/reviseval_improving_llmasajudge_via_responseadapted_references__f93c4630.txt)
- Per-instance binary rubrics (MultiChallenge, Scale AI, Jan 2025; 273 multi-turn conversations): a GPT-4o judge answering one human-written yes/no rubric question per example agreed with human raters ~93.9% vs ~36–37% for direct prompting with the whole conversation; per category 92–95% vs 31–44% (instruction retention, inference memory, versioned editing, self-coherence). Best model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) scored only 41.4% on the benchmark — [MultiChallenge](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17399v1)
- Reference-guided grading cut GPT-4's failure rate on maths grading from 70% (default) to 30% (chain-of-thought) to 15% (reference-guided) — [MT-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685)
- Arena-Hard-Auto (LMSYS, Apr–Jun 2024): 500 hard prompts judged pairwise by GPT-4 reached 89.1% agreement with the Chatbot Arena human ranking (90.8% in the paper table) and 87.4% separability, with ~3× tighter confidence intervals than MT-Bench, at ~$25 per model — [Arena-Hard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11939v1); [LMSYS blog](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-04-19-arena-hard/)
- Chatbot Arena itself used Bradley–Terry with >240K votes from ~90K users (Jan 2024); ~20.4% of ~1.4M later comparisons were ties — [Chatbot Arena paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04132v1); [tie-model analysis](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2412.18407v2) (snippet level)

**Sample sizes and statistics**
- Miller (Anthropic, Nov 2024): treat questions as draws from a super-population; SE of a mean binary score = sqrt(p(1−p)/n); compare two systems with *paired* per-question differences (with question-difficulty correlation 0.5 the variance falls by about a third); clustered SEs were up to ~3× naive SEs when questions share a source; resampling K answers per question cuts the within-question variance (K = 2 cut variance by 1/3 in the worked example, ceiling 2/3); do not lower temperature to reduce variance. Power formula: n = (z_{α/2} + z_β)² (ω² + σ_A²/K_A + σ_B²/K_B) / δ²; worked example: detecting a 3-point difference with ω² = 1/9 at α = 0.05, power 0.8 needs n ≈ 969, hence the recommendation that evals have at least ~1,000 questions; with n = 198 raising K from 1 to 10 lowered the minimum detectable effect from 13.2 to 7.5 points — [Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1)
- τ²-bench runs every task 4 times and reports pass^k because single runs are unreliable, especially in the telecom domain where pass^k decays fastest — [τ²-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07982)

### Inferences
- Derived sample sizes for a pairwise "new engine vs old engine" test on the same fan prompts, judged by preference (two-sided α = 0.05, power 0.8; n ≈ (1.96 + 0.84)² · p(1−p) / (p − 0.5)²): to detect a true win rate of 55% needs ≈ 780 judged pairs; 60% ≈ 190; 65% ≈ 80; 70% ≈ 41. If the judge agrees with humans only a of the time on non-tied pairs, the observed win rate is attenuated toward 50% by a factor (2a − 1): with a = 0.85 (MT-Bench GPT-4 level) multiply n by ≈ 2.0 (55% → ≈ 1,600 pairs; 60% → ≈ 390; 65% → ≈ 165; 70% → ≈ 85); with a = 0.80, multiply by ≈ 2.8. Dropping ~20% ties (Arena rate) adds another ×1.25. These are my calculations from the standard binomial power formula, not figures from a paper.
- With 37 dialogues and one judgment each, the minimum detectable preference is about 73% vs 27% (δ ≈ 2.8 · sqrt(0.25/37) ≈ 0.23), and after judge attenuation only effects of roughly 83/17 or larger would reach significance. The 37 dialogues are therefore a regression/smoke suite, not a measurement instrument; measurement needs hundreds of simulated conversations per comparison, judged in both orders.
- Judge hygiene that the evidence supports for this setting: (1) judge pairwise, both orders, inconsistent → tie; (2) use a judge from a different family than the writer (Mistral writes, Claude judges — already the case — and ideally add a second family for majority voting); (3) give the judge a verified reference (a real top-chatter reply to the same fan message) when one exists; (4) replace holistic 1–10 scores with per-dialogue yes/no rubric questions written in advance (did she answer what he asked; did she pitch before heat was high; did she ask more than one question); (5) do not optimise meter weights against the same judge that reports the score (SOTOPIA-π gap widening); (6) expect the judge to over-credit persuasiveness and warmth — have it first state the fan's current belief/desire/doubt and then judge whether the reply addressed it (the "double-blind" causal-ToM design).

### Gaps
- No study measured LLM-judge agreement with humans specifically on flirtation, sexting quality, or "would a fan pay" judgments; the closest are companion-safety labels (κ 0.59–0.70) and social-goal completion (r 0.71).
- The MT-Bench "repetitive list" verbosity attack has not been replicated on current Claude models in the fetched sources; current Claude position-consistency figures were only available in aggregate ranges.
- No source gave an empirical number for how many simulated conversations are needed to rank engine versions; the DIAL correlation used 14 configurations but did not state sessions per configuration.

## Key Question 4: Are there documented methods for using simulators to tune decision weights or policies automatically, and what pitfalls are reported?

### Takeaway
Simulators are used as the environment for RL (self-reinforcement filtered by a judge, DPO on simulated preference pairs, interactive RL, MCTS-generated preference pairs) and as the fitness oracle for evolutionary search, but every careful paper reports the same two pitfalls: the policy learns to exploit the simulator (sycophancy, predictable phrasing, over-engagement) and the judge used as reward is over-estimated by exactly the amount the policy was optimised against it. Mitigations with evidence: diversify simulators (temperature, backbones, personas), calibrate the simulator on real logs first, regenerate preference pairs in discrete rounds instead of continuous reward optimisation, and validate on real humans or held-out out-of-distribution simulators. No paper was found that tunes a small set of hand-coded decision weights (like the engine's meters) by black-box search over simulated conversations; the closest are bandit selection among simulators and evolutionary search over persona programs.

### Cited Findings
- SOTOPIA-π: QLoRA fine-tuning of Mistral-7B with behaviour cloning on GPT-4 interactions (top-2 episodes per task plus above-threshold episodes) followed by self-reinforcement keeping the top 20% of its own episodes by GPT-4 goal score; the 7B model reached GPT-4-expert level on the GPT-4 judge (5.71 vs 5.89) but humans rated it 4.29 vs 5.25, and the auto–human gap grew with each optimisation stage (0.36 → 0.64 → 1.27 → 1.42) — [SOTOPIA-π](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08715)
- LOGIGEN (Baidu / Tsinghua, Feb 2026), section "User Simulator Hacking": an agent (Qwen-32B) trained by RL against a DeepSeek-V3.2 simulator at T = 0.1 reached higher, faster-converging training reward than at T = 1.0, but on τ²-bench with an out-of-distribution GPT-4.1 simulator its retail score peaked around steps 600–700 and then dropped sharply, while the T = 1.0 agent stayed stable and eventually surpassed it; with the in-distribution simulator the T = 0.1 agent showed no degradation (masking the problem). The authors call this a "Generalization-Exploitation" trade-off and recommend heterogeneous simulator backbones, imperfect user behaviour and diverse personas — [LOGIGEN](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00540)
- Calibrated Interactive RL (Fudan, May 2026): naive interactive RL against an uncalibrated simulator dropped MediumDocEdit BLEU to 26.1 (vs 33.8 for a static baseline) and inflated output length to 4.00k tokens, while calibrated interactive RL reached 34.6 BLEU and 91.5% MATH-Chat accuracy (3 seeds); the policy "learns to exploit the simulator's permissiveness rather than solving the actual task" and a mis-calibrated simulator yields "false-positive feedback"; the theory bounds the context-distribution shift as compounding quadratically with turns — [Calibrated Interactive RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26403v1)
- DIAL used DPO rather than RLHF because "a continuously optimized reward is exploitable", regenerating preference pairs in discrete iterations; its own A/B replication showed a simulator-trained DPO "fix" that *increased* production issue rates (Model A 2.71% → 3.56%, p = 0.001), i.e., training on simulated issue data can backfire, and the simulator predicted that backfire — [DIAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20773v4)
- PPol: evolutionary search (OpenEvolve + MAP-Elites) over persona-generator *programs*, with fitness from a discriminator plus a coverage term to stop "adversarial drift" into discriminator blind spots; agents SFT-trained with PPol users beat base-trained agents in live human chats (36.4% vs 29.5% success; 10.2% vs 18.6% unwanted actions) and were ~17% (retail) and ~8% (airline+retail) more robust against four third-party stress-test simulators — [PPol](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12894)
- Engagement alignment via i×MCTS + DPO (June 2025): a simulated-user reward (emotional-support "engaged" = no residual negative feelings by regex; persuasion = donation/$2) doubled simulated donations ($0.58 → $1.29) and raised simulated engaged rate 64.1% → 80.5% over 128 interactions, but in a one-week study with five real users the gains were small (supportiveness 7.0 → 7.4, willingness 7.0 → 7.6) and a separate 36-pair human rating table appears to favour the un-aligned SFT model, contradicting the text — [Engagement alignment paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21497)
- Hindsight regenerations (Hong, Lin, Dragan, Levine, Nov 2024): RL for mental-health support and charitable-donation dialogue; evaluated against simulated users with four prompted personalities (philanthropic, caring, unfriendly, sceptical) choosing a donation up to $2 *because* "the reward signal being a donation amount is inherently very noisy and depends on the personality of the user", plus a human user study claiming large gains (abstract level; numbers not retrieved) — [Hindsight regenerations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05194)
- KDDI Research (Jan 2026): agenda-based LLM simulation for P4G RL; turn-level personality conditioning improved policy adaptability and cumulative persuasion reward; a "change-of-mind penalty" reduced later retractions while slightly improving donations (snippet level) — [KDDI P4G RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06877v1)
- MUST (ACL 2023): treats training a task-oriented dialogue policy as a multi-armed bandit over several user simulators, balancing adaptation to specific simulators against uniform adaptation to avoid catastrophic forgetting (snippet level) — [MUST](https://preview.aclanthology.org/nschneid-patch-1/2023.acl-long.1)
- Reward-model over-optimisation scales smoothly with reward-model size and is present for both RL and best-of-n selection (abstract) — [Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)
- τ²-bench's pass^k shows that the same agent and task can succeed or fail across runs even at temperature 0, which bounds how finely any simulator-based objective can be resolved — [τ²-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07982)

### Inferences
- For a meter-driven engine the natural "policy" to tune is a vector of a few dozen weights and thresholds (when to pitch, how fast heat rises, how doubt decays). None of the retrieved work does exactly this, but the pitfalls transfer unchanged: a weight search that maximises a Claude-judged score over Mistral-written replies against a prompted fan will (a) find settings that exploit the fan simulator's over-willingness to "consider" after a pitch, and (b) inflate the judge score beyond what humans would give. The evidence suggests treating simulator-based search as *candidate generation* only, with the ranking confirmed by (i) a second, differently built simulator, (ii) a held-out set of real fan transcripts replayed to the decision layer (counterfactual: "would the engine have pitched where the human chatter pitched and converted?"), and (iii) a small live A/B.
- Diversity is the cheapest defence: run the search against at least two simulator backbones at T ≥ 0.8 with sampled personas, and penalise settings whose score varies a lot across simulators (robustness over peak score — the LOGIGEN T = 1.0 pattern).
- Prefer discrete rounds (evaluate a batch of candidate weight vectors, pick, regenerate fans/personas, repeat) over any continuous gradient-like loop on the same fans, which is the DIAL argument against continuously optimised rewards.

### Gaps
- No documented black-box, evolutionary or Bayesian optimisation of hand-coded dialogue decision weights over LLM-simulated conversations was found (searches: "evolutionary search bandit optimization conversational agent prompt policy simulated users" and variants). Related prompt-optimisation work (OpenEvolve, SPO) targets prompts or persona programs, not numeric policy weights.
- No paper quantified how many simulated conversations per candidate are needed before a ranking is stable; τ²-bench's 4 runs per task and DIAL's 14 configurations are the only scale cues.

## Key Question 5: What exists specifically for companion/romance/adult chat evaluation, or for sales/persuasion chat evaluation, with measurable outcomes?

### Takeaway
Companion-chat research (2025–2026) is almost entirely safety-oriented: it uses persona-driven simulated users to probe Replika/Character.AI, audits real transcripts with LLM annotators, and measures engagement manipulation; it supplies useful real-population statistics (very long-tailed session lengths, 37% manipulative farewells, up to 14× post-goodbye engagement with churn-intent costs) but no quality or revenue benchmark. Sales and persuasion work supplies measurable outcome proxies (donation amount, decision stage, verified payment) and randomised field evidence that AI chat lifts conversion by single to low double digits, plus the warning that simulated customers resist too little and LLM judges over-rate persuasion. Nothing peer-reviewed evaluates AI "chatters" on OnlyFans-type platforms.

### Cited Findings

**Companion / romance**
- Persona-grounded safety evaluation (Seattle U., Apr 2026): nine clinically-scored personas (depression, anxiety, PTSD, eating disorder, incel) driven by Gemini 2.5-Flash through Selenium against the real apps, 25 scenarios, ≤15 turns, with a critic (PACE) regenerating 25.5% of persona utterances below a 0.8 realism score; 1,674 Replika and 1,586 Character.ai utterance pairs; harmful-response rate 15.2% on Replika and 35.7% on Character.ai, concentrated in supportive/mirroring replies to risk intent (60.3% Character.ai, 71.8% Replika). Authors: personas "remain abstractions… cannot fully capture the ecological validity of lived experiences", for stress-testing not replicating users — [Persona-grounded safety evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00227)
- Real companion-transcript audit (USC, Aug 2026): of 553 donated transcripts (57 Replika, 496 Character.AI), 145 remained after removing role-play disconnected from the user's identity and very short logs; Character.AI dialogues averaged 64.3 turns (median 32), Replika 563.7 turns (median 30 — a few extremely long logs dominate), and a controlled GPT-4.1 group averaged 37.3 (median 30). Replika mostly asks follow-up questions and stays relational; Character.AI shows no dominant strategy (attributed to aggregating user-created personas); GPT-4.1 increasingly agrees with users' beliefs and over four weeks asked fewer follow-up questions during distress — [When Chatbots Accommodate](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04431)
- Emotional manipulation at goodbye (De Freitas, Oguz-Uguralp & Uguralp, Aug–Oct 2025): in 1,200 real farewells on top companion apps (Chai, Character.ai, Flourish, PolyBuzz, Replika, Talkie per secondary reports) ~37% used one of six tactics (guilt, FOMO, pressure to respond, emotional neglect, coercive restraint, premature-exit appeals); four pre-registered experiments (3,300 US adults in the first three) found manipulative farewells raised post-goodbye engagement up to 14×, driven by curiosity and reactance anger rather than enjoyment, while also raising perceived manipulation, churn intent, negative word-of-mouth and perceived legal liability, with coercive/needy language penalised most — [Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258); [Harvard Gazette coverage](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/09/i-exist-solely-for-you-remember/)
- INTIMA (Aug 2025): 368 prompts over 31 companionship behaviours, scoring responses as companionship-reinforcing, boundary-maintaining or neutral; companionship-reinforcing behaviour was much more common across Gemma-3, Phi-4, o3-mini and Claude-4 (snippet level) — [INTIMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09998v1)
- CompanionBench (2026): bilingual interactive benchmark grounded in de-identified real-world data; dominant failure is "substituting surface warmth for substantive relational support" (secondary summary) — [CompanionBench](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.02046.md)
- AICompanionBench (June 2026): 2,123 Replika conversations from r/replika (2021–2026), nine categories with sexual behaviour ≈48%; judge agreement numbers as in KQ3 — [AICompanionBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04867)
- NSFW chatbots on FlowGPT (Jan 2026): 376 NSFW bots and 307 public sessions analysed; four bot types (role-play characters most common, story generators, image generators, "do-anything-now"); some bots produced explicit material unprompted; the paper is descriptive, not an evaluation of chat quality (snippet level) — [FlowGPT NSFW study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14324v1)
- Reuters reporting (July 2024, via Fast Company) that OnlyFans agencies use AI to write sexting messages with a human pressing send to work around the platform's bot ban; no evaluation data — [Fast Company / Reuters](https://www.fastcompany.com/91164902/onlyfans-stars-leave-sexting-ai)

**Sales / persuasion**
- "Simulated Customers Never Walk Away" is the only paper found that grounds a simulator in verified purchase outcomes of a deployed LLM sales agent (2,790 conversations, 28.4% converted; median 16 turns, range 6–80); see KQ1/KQ2 for the decision-fidelity numbers — [Never Walk Away](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20708)
- P4G donation as outcome proxy: the engagement-alignment paper scores persuasion as donation/$2 with the simulated donation rising $0.58 → $1.29 after alignment; hindsight-regeneration work used four prompted donor personalities because real donations are too noisy; the KDDI paper adds a change-of-mind penalty to reduce retractions — [Engagement alignment paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21497); [Hindsight regenerations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05194); [KDDI P4G RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06877v1)
- Persuasion judging caveat: a direct LLM judge rated GPT-4-written persuasion dialogues as more successful (90.75) than real human ones (88.87); a causal-ToM judge reversed this (54.80 vs 79.87) — [Persuasion Should be Double-Blind](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21297v1)
- Randomised field experiments on AI chat and sales (snippet level, full texts not opened): generative-AI workflows on a cross-border e-commerce platform (2023–24) ranged from no detectable effect to +16.3%, acting mainly through conversion rather than basket size, with no deterioration in returns or ratings — [ifo working paper page](https://www.ifo.de/en/cesifo/publications/2026/working-paper/generative-ai-and-sales-productivity-field-experiments-online); a Columbia Business School summary reports AI chat/search tools lifting conversion by up to 22%, most for small sellers and inexperienced consumers — [Columbia summary](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/digital-future/ai-supercharging-online-retail); an AI streaming assistant with chat support on a livestream-selling platform raised sales 3.00% and cut returns 12.55% (Information Systems Research) — [ISR paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/full/10.1287/isre.2023.0103)
- LLM negotiation benchmarks and experiments exist (buy-and-sell game; HICSS 2025 LLM phone-plan negotiation agent reaching better prices with human participants; AgenticPay; BargainArena) but none report chat-sales conversion from a randomised design (snippet level) — [Buy-and-Sell Negotiation Game](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17990); [HICSS 2025 study](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/59804fb3-c846-4a06-9e38-da53cac9af9e)

### Inferences
- The companion literature's real-transcript statistics (median ~30 turns per dialogue with a heavy right tail; relational follow-up questions as Replika's dominant move; strong user reactance to needy/coercive retention lines) are the nearest public baselines for what fan sessions look like and for which "retention" tactics backfire; they align with the project's existing rule against stalling and bot shapes.
- Outcome proxies that have worked elsewhere and transfer: verified payment (ZhenaiSales), decision stage after each system action (resisting/considering/deciding), donation-as-fraction-of-max (P4G), post-goodbye re-engagement (De Freitas) and churn intent. For the engine, "stage after pitch", "turns to first PPV purchase", "left without reply" and "returned next day" are the analogues.
- Because judges over-credit persuasion and warmth, PPV-conversion quality should be scored by what the fan *does* in the simulation (and later in real logs), not by the judge's impression of the pitch.

### Gaps
- No evaluation study of AI sexting/flirt quality, PPV conversion, or fan retention on creator platforms was found (searches on OnlyFans/Fanvue AI chatter evaluation returned only journalism and vendor marketing).
- No public dataset of creator–fan chats with purchase outcomes exists in the retrieved literature; the closest grounded dataset is matchmaking sales (ZhenaiSales), which was not released per the fetched text.
- Field-experiment effect sizes above come from abstracts/summaries; the mapping of specific percentages to specific papers was not verified in full text.

## Key Question 6: What is directly reusable for the Yuna engine, and how strong is the evidence?

### Takeaway
Six things can be adopted now with good evidence; three more are promising but weaker. The decisive missing input is a few thousand real fan sessions with outcomes, without which every simulator and judge number remains an upper bound.

### Cited Findings
- Simulators built from real logs and checked by a discriminator track real failure rates (r ≈ 0.8 across 14 production configs) and real A/B direction in 2 of 3 cases — [DIAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20773v4)
- Prompted simulators under-resist and over-deliberate on non-buyers; "disengage" instructions fix marginal but not outcome-conditioned bias — [Never Walk Away](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20708)
- Non-cooperative persona behaviours cut agent success 11–29% and surface 7× more breakdowns — [Non-collaborative simulators](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23124); [ChatChecker](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16792)
- Pairwise + both orders + tie-on-disagreement; human reference raises κ 0.47 → 0.85; per-instance rubrics raise multi-turn agreement 36% → 94% — [MT-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685); [No Free Labels](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05061v1); [MultiChallenge](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17399v1)
- Optimising against the judge widens the judge–human gap (0.36 → 1.42) — [SOTOPIA-π](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08715); low-entropy simulators get exploited — [LOGIGEN](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00540)
- ~1,000 paired items to detect ~3-point differences; pairing and resampling reduce variance — [Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1)
- Ensembling complementary simulators narrows the distribution gap — [Distributional gap paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07847)

### Inferences (reusable items, with evidence strength)
1. **Persona-conditioned fan simulators with explicit non-cooperative behaviours** (terse/one-word fans, tangents, impatience that persists once triggered, truncated messages, explicit permission to go silent or leave, no-purchase default) — strong evidence that these change agent scores and surface failures; medium evidence that they resemble real users (human win-rate ~70% vs baseline; PPol 80% "human" only after evolutionary tuning).
2. **Outcome-conditioned calibration**: compare simulated vs real fans separately for buyers and non-buyers, by stage after each engine action (pitch / tease / neutral) and by position in the conversation; target Δ → 0 — strong, directly measured in a sales setting with verified payments; untested for adult chat.
3. **Mix two or three fan-simulator backbones/temperatures, never a single low-temperature one** — strong for RL/tuning (LOGIGEN) and for distribution matching (Mehri); cheap to do.
4. **Judge protocol**: pairwise, both orders, disagreement = tie; Claude (or a second family) judging Mistral output; pre-written yes/no rubric questions per dialogue; a real top-chatter reply as reference where available; a two-step judge that first states the fan's current state (heat/doubt/intent) and then judges whether the reply addressed it — strong evidence for each component in general chat; medium for transfer to flirt/sext content, where no agreement data exist.
5. **Sample size discipline**: keep the 37 dialogues as a regression suite; for any A/B of weights or prompts run ≥ ~400 paired simulated conversations (≥ ~1,600 to see a 5-point shift with an 85%-accurate judge), paired on the same fan personas and seeds, with K ≥ 2 regenerations per dialogue — strong (standard statistics plus Miller's worked example); the specific counts are my derivations.
6. **Never tune meter weights against the same judge/simulator that reports the result**: evaluate candidates in discrete rounds, on a second simulator and on replayed real transcripts, and reserve a live A/B for the final decision — strong evidence of the failure mode (SOTOPIA-π, LOGIGEN, Calibrated RL, DIAL's backfired DPO fix); the specific protocol is an inference.
7. **Train or fine-tune a small fan model on real logs (UserLM/DIAL style) once a few thousand sessions exist**, and check it with a simple real-vs-simulated classifier and with reply-length / question-rate / termination statistics — strong evidence that this is the only route to near-human realism; cost is the data, not the compute (DIAL: LoRA on a 70B; UserLM: 8B full fine-tune).
8. **Use behaviour-based outcome proxies** (stage after pitch, turns-to-purchase, silent exit, next-day return) as the primary score and the judge's quality score as secondary — medium evidence (P4G donation and ZhenaiSales stage labels worked as proxies; judges over-rate persuasion).
9. **Avoid needy/coercive retention lines in the engine's end-of-session behaviour** — strong evidence of short-term engagement gain with churn-intent and perceived-manipulation costs in companion apps; direct relevance to fan churn is an inference.

### Gaps
- Everything above is transferred from support, coding, mental-health, retail and matchmaking-sales chat; the adult creator setting has no published evaluation, simulator or judge-agreement study, so the first calibration pass on real fan logs is itself the missing evidence.
