# Creator-subscription chat: conversion, retention and rules for a supervised AI creator on Fanvue (evidence 2023–2026)

Scope note for the report writer: research done 21 Oct 2026. Reliability tags used throughout: **[measured]** = a dataset with a stated sample; **[company]** = platform or vendor press release with no methodology; **[vendor]** = agency/tool blog with no data basis (low reliability, SEO-style); **[journalism]** = named-reporter piece from a major outlet; **[legal]** = statute text or law-firm summary of it; **[academic]** = peer-reviewed. I could not fetch onlyfans.com or wired.com directly (blocked to this crawler), so OnlyFans' Terms are quoted second-hand from Reuters and a legal blog, and Brendan Koerner's Wired undercover piece is known only through podcast summaries.

## Key question 1: What concrete conversion numbers are documented for creator DMs (PPV purchase rate per send, revenue per subscriber per month, share of subscribers who ever buy)?

### Takeaway
The only large measured dataset (about 1 million OnlyFans fans routed through one traffic vendor) says just 4.2% of subscribers ever spend anything, 17% ever start a conversation, 3.3% make a first purchase and 1.7% a second; messages dominate revenue; and most spending happens in the first 48 hours. Per-send PPV purchase rates of 5–30% circulate widely but come only from agency/vendor blogs with no data basis, and they measure sends to already-engaged fans, not all subscribers.

### Cited Findings
- **[measured, commercial]** OnlyGuider/OnlyTraffic press release (28 May 2025, paid newswire): 1,003,855 subscribers analysed; 4.2% of subscribers spend money (95.8% pay nothing); average spend $48.52 "per creator"; about $2.06 earned per subscriber; messages = 69.74% of revenue, subscriptions = 4.11%; 83.3% of payments occur within 48 hours of subscribing; 0.01% of subscribers ("whales") generate 20.2% of revenue; 17.19% of subscribers initiate contact, 82% never do; weekends = 29.7% of revenue. The release gives no period or selection method, and its headline transaction figures (58,947,698 transactions totalling $2,045,944) do not reconcile (about 3.5 cents per transaction), so treat the absolute money figures with caution — [Yahoo Finance copy of the ACCESS Newswire release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/study-men-spend-48-52-211500140.html); same figures repeated at [FinancialContent](https://markets.financialcontent.com/stocks/article/accwirecq-2025-5-28-new-study-men-spend-4852-on-onlyfans-models-but-only-42-of-subscribers-pay).
- **[measured, commercial]** OnlyTraffic's own statistics page on the same 1-million-fan dataset (fans who subscribed via OnlyTraffic links; no period stated): funnel = conversation 17.19% → first purchase 3.27% → second purchase 1.71% → fifth purchase 0.41%; transaction mix (share of transactions, not revenue): messages 67.6%, tips 26.3%, subscriptions 3%, renewals 1.8%, paid posts/PPV about 1.3%; refunds = 1.03% of revenue; time to first purchase: 35.6% of buyers buy on day 1, 52.2% by day 2, under 1% wait more than 400 days; ARPU on paid pages by traffic source (paying fans only) ranges from $13.70 (YouTube) to $88.10 (Reddit); ARPU on free pages $0.90–$5.90 per fan by source — [OnlyTraffic statistics page](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics). Note the internal inconsistency: 4.2% "made at least one transaction" vs 3.27% "first purchase" in the funnel on the same page.
- **[measured, commercial]** Same page: top 100 fans (0.01%) generated 20.2% of spending; each of those whales spent between $1,397 and $59,030 — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics).
- **[journalism]** Reuters (30 July 2024): OnlyFans subscriptions cost $4.99 to $50 per month; tips and custom-content payments generated slightly more revenue than subscriptions in 2022 per the company's filings; one subscriber paid $14,000 to a model in one month; another spent about $7,300 on one model over roughly 18 months; a two-person chatter team generated $1.5 million for one model over eight months — [Reuters special report, syndicated by Interaksyon/Philstar](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2024/07/31/280757/onlyfans-porn-juggernaut-fueled-by-a-deception/).
- **[journalism]** Fortune (5 April 2024): chatting with fans accounts for over 50% of revenue for many OnlyFans creators — [Fortune via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-coming-onlyfans-chat-industry-163403868.html).
- **[platform filings, press-reported]** OnlyFans FY2024 (year to 30 Nov 2024): $7.22bn gross fan payments (+9%), $1.41bn net revenue, $5.80bn paid to creators, 4.634m creator accounts, 377.5m fan accounts — [Variety via AOL](https://www.aol.com/entertainment/onlyfans-gross-revenue-rises-9-120109261.html); FY2025 press reports: about $7.8bn fan spending, revenue about $1.6bn, pre-tax profit $715m, 5m creator accounts, 437m fan accounts (filing itself not reviewed) — [Everything-PR summary of FT/Benzinga coverage](https://everything-pr.com/onlyfans-revenue-profit). OnlyTraffic attributes a "73% of fan payments from messages/PPV/tips" split to OnlyFans' official accounts, second-hand — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics).
- **[vendor]** Per-send PPV purchase-rate claims, none with a data basis: targeted PPV to an engaged base "typically converts between 5% and 20%", conversational PPV to fans with purchase history "regularly exceeds 20%" — [Substy](https://substy.ai/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-in-2026-how-top-agencies-maximize-every-message); first PPV at €5–15 converts 15–25%, opening at €50 to cold fans under 5%, "healthy" range 15–30%, below 10% means price/timing wrong, above 40% means under-priced — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide); mass sends to unsegmented lists 2–5%, targeted sends 10–20%+, generic mass-PPV open rates 8–12% vs 25–40% for segmented sends — range across [Aruna Talent](https://arunatalent.com/blog/onlyfans-mass-messaging/), [Amour Chatting](https://www.amourchatting.com/blog/onlyfans-mass-messaging-strategy), [Outseeker](https://www.outseeker.net/blog/onlyfans-mass-message-ideas), [AT Agency](https://at-agency.co/blog/why-your-onlyfans-fans-arent-buying-ppvs) (attribution among these pages is from search summaries; all are SEO/agency content).
- **[vendor]** A 2–3x higher PPV conversion claim for AI-assisted chat "with revenue optimization", no sample or method — [ChatPersona](https://chatpersona.ai/blog/5-ways-ai-chatbots-help-onlyfans-chatters-reply-faster-increase-sales).

### Inferences
- The "share who ever buy" is a few percent of all subscribers on free/traffic-driven pages, while the 15–30% figures are per-send rates among fans who are already talking and buying. A planner should keep both numbers separate: the first is the ceiling on reach, the second is the per-offer hit rate inside a warm chat.
- From OnlyTraffic's funnel, roughly one in five conversation-starters makes a purchase (3.27 / 17.19) and about half of first buyers buy again (1.71 / 3.27). Getting a fan to talk at all is the biggest filter; repeat purchase is comparatively easy once a first purchase exists.
- Revenue per subscriber per month is not published anywhere credible in a clean form. The $2.06 per subscriber figure is lifetime on mostly free pages; $13.70–$88.10 is lifetime per paying fan on paid pages; the whale band ($1,397–$59,030) shows why averages mislead.
- "Messages per sale" is not documented in any source found; the closest proxies are chatter workloads (5–20 simultaneous chats, 10–30 subscribers per day) from Reuters.

### Gaps
- No Fanvue-specific conversion or revenue-per-subscriber data exists publicly; every measured figure above is OnlyFans, routed through one traffic vendor (selection bias toward paid-traffic, free-page fans).
- No source gives messages-per-sale, PPV open rate measured on a stated sample, or PPV purchase rate by price point with a sample; the agency figures contradict one another (e.g. welcome-PPV conversion quoted as 4–6% on one page and 30–40% on another in search results, unattributed).
- OnlyFans' and Fanvue's own filings do not publish per-fan spend or churn.

## Key question 2: What does credible material say about pitch frequency, price anchoring, bundles, discounts and win-back offers, and their measured effects?

### Takeaway
There is no measured evidence on pitch frequency, anchoring, bundles or discounts; what exists is a consistent agency/vendor consensus (low-price first PPV, escalate by spend history, 2–4 offers a week, one follow-up, no price-cutting, personal win-back) plus Reuters' description of how chatters actually sell (warm-up to arousal, then impulse PPV). The one measured fact that bears on timing is that most spend happens within 48 hours of subscribing.

### Cited Findings
- **[journalism]** Reuters (July 2024) on agency practice: chatters aim to arouse subscribers so they impulsively buy PPV content, which is often pre-recorded and sold as new custom work; software builds a profile of each subscriber (country, job, pets, hobbies, frequent topics) so the next chatter can continue seamlessly; chatters give subscribers consistent nicknames; a chatter guide advises waiting at least three minutes before sending a promised "three-minute video" so it seems filmed live; copy-paste fetish scripts ("JOIs") are sold in bundles for 500 pesos; some chatters consider subscribers who only want conversation unprofitable; chatters handle 5–20 men at once; agencies take 20–80% of a model's earnings — [Reuters via Interaksyon](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2024/07/31/280757/onlyfans-porn-juggernaut-fueled-by-a-deception/).
- **[journalism]** BBC (via AOL, March 2026): a Philippines chatter was given targets to generate "hundreds of dollars" of photo/video sales per 8-hour shift and sexted several fans at once, several times an hour — [BBC via AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/icky-heartbreaking-2-per-hour-000445310.html).
- **[measured, commercial]** Timing: 83.3% of payments fall within 48 hours of subscribing — [OnlyGuider release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/study-men-spend-48-52-211500140.html); 52.2% of first purchases happen by day 2, 50.3% of fans stop engaging after day 2, under 5% remain active beyond 10 days, average lifespan on a page 44.85 days — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics).
- **[vendor]** Price ladder by spend history (Desirely): cold fans €3–10; €1–50 spent → €10–25; €50–200 spent → €25–60; €200+ → €60–300+; first purchase capped at €5–15, then roughly €20 → €30 → €50; by content type photos €5–25, short video €10–35, long video €25–80, customs €50–300+, bundles €20–100; videos convert 2–3x better than photos — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide).
- **[vendor]** Price ladder (Substy): entry $8–15 (no prior spend), $20–40 after about $50 spent, $50–150 after about $200 spent, "$200 or more" ceiling; prices tied to each fan's spend history — [Substy](https://substy.ai/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-in-2026-how-top-agencies-maximize-every-message).
- **[vendor]** Warm-up: wait for 5–7 quality exchanges before the first PPV; return to normal conversation between sales rather than stacking pitches — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide).
- **[vendor]** Pitch frequency: no more than one PPV per day per fan, 2–4 PPVs per week to an active fan, mass DMs 2–3 per week maximum — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide); 2–4 PPV sends per week to the active base — [Substy](https://substy.ai/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-in-2026-how-top-agencies-maximize-every-message).
- **[vendor]** Follow-up after an unopened PPV: one second message 24–48 hours later with new framing or a time-limited angle; a third message only to those who ignored both, which "converts less" — [Substy](https://substy.ai/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-in-2026-how-top-agencies-maximize-every-message); in-chat micro-cadence after sending a PPV: anticipation line about 30 seconds later, light check-in at 2–3 minutes, final message using the fan's name at 5 minutes — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide).
- **[vendor]** Discounts: do not lower the price first, add value (a bonus) instead; if a cut is unavoidable frame it as a one-time exception; no discount percentages given — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide). Neither vendor page describes bundle mechanics beyond listing a €20–100 bundle price.
- **[vendor]** Win-back: (1) personal message referencing a previous conversation, never a mass DM, (2) rebuild rapport with no commercial intent, (3) then a reactivation PPV framed as made with that fan in mind; re-engaged past buyers claimed to convert 2–3x better than never-buyers — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide). A re-engagement guide claims "over 50% of creators say pushy DM tactics drive unsubscribes or blocks from expired fans", no sample given — [Pseudoface](https://www.pseudoface.com/guides/start-here/restarting-after-break/how-to-message-expired-fans-on-onlyfans).
- **[vendor]** Response-speed claim: a message answered within 5 minutes converts to a PPV purchase at 3–5x the rate of one answered 12 hours later (surfaced in search summaries of agency guides, unattributed and unsourced) — see the cluster at [Outseeker](https://www.outseeker.net/blog/how-to-blow-up-onlyfans-2026) and [Sirency](https://www.sirency.com/onlyfans-messaging).
- **[platform guidance]** Fanvue's own April 2026 blog recommends handling "high-stakes upsells", emotional messages and high-value fans personally, limiting AI interactions with high spenders and long-time subscribers, and using AI for first replies, repeat questions and re-engagement nudges — [Fanvue blog](https://www.fanvue.com/blog/ai-message-responder).

### Inferences
- The agency consensus (low anchor, escalate with spend, one follow-up, add value instead of discounting) is internally consistent across competing vendors, which gives it some weight as "what practitioners do", but zero weight as "what is proven to work".
- The 48-hour spend concentration implies the first-day sequence (welcome, warm-up, first low-priced offer) matters more than any later cadence rule; a win-back programme is working against a base where over half of fans are gone by day 3.
- Reuters' "three-minute delay to fake a live video" and "pre-recorded sold as custom" tactics are precisely what Fanvue's AUP forbids (see KQ7: "falsely depicting live interactions"), so the playbook must be filtered for honesty before an AI adopts it.

### Gaps
- No A/B or cohort evidence on pitch frequency, anchoring, bundles, discount depth or win-back timing from any platform, agency, court filing or academic paper.
- The Wired undercover piece by Brendan Koerner (chatter training, scripts, quotas) could not be fetched; only podcast summaries confirm it exists — [Back in America podcast](https://backinamerica.substack.com/p/undercover-with-brendan-koerner-the).

## Key question 3: What do subscriber-behaviour numbers say about renewal and churn, top-spender share, tipping and time-of-day effects?

### Takeaway
Measured (commercial) data shows extreme concentration (0.01% of fans = 20% of spend), very short fan lifetimes (median engagement ends by day 2, 44.85-day average lifespan), a paid-page renewal rate of 18.4% among transacting fans, tips as roughly a quarter of transactions, and only a mild weekend lift (Saturday 15.4% of transactions vs Wednesday 13.6%). Hour-of-day data does not exist publicly; "evenings" is vendor folklore.

### Cited Findings
- **[measured, commercial]** Renewal/churn (fans with at least one transaction, OnlyTraffic 1M-fan dataset): renewal rate on paid pages 18.4%; 50.3% stopped engaging after day 2; 29.5% bought on the day they subscribed and then disappeared; under 5% stayed active beyond 10 days; average lifespan on a model's page 44.85 days — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics).
- **[vendor, conflicting]** Agency blogs put a "healthy" rebill rate at 50–70% — [Undefined Talent](https://undefinedtalent.com/blog/faceless-onlyfans-rebill-rate-strategy), [Enforcity](https://www.enforcity.com/onlyfans-success/rebill-on-onlyfans); "average creator loses 30–50% of subscribers every month" — [Aruna Talent](https://arunatalent.com/blog/onlyfans-subscriber-retention), [TopOnlyFansCourse](https://www.toponlyfanscourse.com/post/onlyfans-subscriber-churn-what-the-data-actually-shows); an SEO statistics page claims 65% renew after 3 months and 92% among long-term users with no source — [Zipdo](https://zipdo.co/onlyfans-creator-statistics/) (low reliability). Subscriptions auto-renew by default unless the fan turns it off — [Outseeker](https://www.outseeker.net/blog/onlyfans-subscriber-retention-strategy).
- **[measured, commercial]** Top spenders: 0.01% of subscribers generate 20.2% of revenue; the top 0.1% of creators capture 76% of creator revenue, averaging $146,881 per month — [OnlyGuider release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/study-men-spend-48-52-211500140.html); per-whale lifetime spend $1,397–$59,030 — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics).
- **[journalism]** Creator concentration per Reuters: top 0.1% of creators earn about $2.3m a year on average (citing Bedbible.com), most creators earn under $100 a month — [Reuters via Interaksyon](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2024/07/31/280757/onlyfans-porn-juggernaut-fueled-by-a-deception/).
- **[measured, commercial]** Tipping: tips = 26.3% of transactions (messages 67.6%, subscriptions 3%, renewals 1.8%, paid posts 1.3%) — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics); **[journalism]** tips plus custom-content payments slightly exceeded subscription revenue in OnlyFans' 2022 filings — [Reuters via Interaksyon](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2024/07/31/280757/onlyfans-porn-juggernaut-fueled-by-a-deception/).
- **[measured, commercial]** Day of week: transactions spread fairly evenly; Saturday highest (15.4%), Wednesday lowest (13.6%); Friday–Sunday most active — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics); weekends = 29.7% of revenue — [OnlyGuider release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/study-men-spend-48-52-211500140.html).
- **[vendor]** Time of day: "evenings 8 PM to midnight and weekends" best, "Monday 9 AM" worst, no data — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide).
- **[measured, commercial]** Refunds = 1.03% of revenue on OnlyFans in the OnlyTraffic dataset — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics); **[platform text]** Fanvue charges a fixed fee per dispute once an account exceeds a 1.5% dispute rate over 30 days and deducts refunded/charged-back amounts from the creator balance (Creator Terms 4.1–4.2) — [Fanvue Creator Terms](https://legal.fanvue.com/creator-terms.md).
- **[academic]** OnlyFans does not publish official statistics on creators' earnings — [Anciones Anguita & Checa Romero, Sexuality & Culture (Springer, 2025)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12119-025-10389-2).

### Inferences
- A weekend share of 29.7% against a uniform baseline of 28.6% is only about a 4% relative lift; day-of-week scheduling matters far less than being present in the first 48 hours of each new subscription.
- The 18.4% renewal figure and the 50–70% "healthy rebill" folklore are measuring different things (transacting fans on paid pages vs all subscribers with auto-renew on); for planning, assume most fans are one-visit buyers and design for that.
- Whale concentration argues for a hard rule routing the top 0.1–1% of spenders to human managers, which also matches Fanvue's own guidance (KQ2).

### Gaps
- No hour-of-day data from any measured source; no Fanvue-specific churn or tipping data; no study separating tipping behaviour of AI-creator fans from human-creator fans.
- Renewal definitions differ across sources, so the numbers are not comparable; OnlyFans' and Fanvue's dashboards are the only place to get a clean figure.

## Key question 4: Mass messaging versus personal DMs — what are the open/purchase rates and the evidence on fatigue?

### Takeaway
No measured comparison of mass messages versus one-to-one DMs exists in any credible source; vendors agree that in-conversation PPVs convert several times better than broadcasts and that more than about 2–4 paid mass sends a week drives unsubscribes, but none cite data. Fanvue's API treats broadcasts as a distinct message type and supports segmented "dynamic lists", which is the practical lever.

### Cited Findings
- **[vendor]** In-conversation PPVs convert "5 to 10x" better than mass DMs; mass DMs capped at 2–3 per week — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide); mass PPV to unsegmented lists converts at "the lower end" of 5–20%, segmentation claimed to yield "two or three times" the revenue from the same base, no data — [Substy](https://substy.ai/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-in-2026-how-top-agencies-maximize-every-message).
- **[vendor]** Open-rate claims (definitions vary between "share who unlock" and "share who purchase"): unsegmented mass open 2–5%, generic mass 5–15% or 8–12%, segmented/warm sends 15–30% or 25–40%; target "25%+ open, 15%+ purchase per send"; unsubscribe target "below 1% per send" — range across [Aruna Talent](https://arunatalent.com/blog/onlyfans-mass-messaging/), [Amour Chatting](https://www.amourchatting.com/blog/onlyfans-mass-messaging-strategy), [Outseeker](https://www.outseeker.net/blog/onlyfans-mass-message-ideas), [OF Agency](https://www.ofagency.co/blog/onlyfans-mass-messages-examples), [Desirely glossary](https://www.desirely.co/en/onlyfans-glossary/onlyfans-ppv-open-rate) (all agency SEO pages; attribution from search summaries).
- **[vendor]** Frequency and fatigue: 2–3 paid mass messages per week is the "sweet spot" and daily sends cause unsubscribes — [Laguna Models](https://lagunamodels.com/blog/how-to-use-mass-messages-on-onlyfans/); 3–5 per week with daily sends "leading to fatigue" — [Talent Grow](https://talentgrow.co.uk/blog/onlyfans-mass-messaging-guide/); 2–4 paid mass messages a week — [OFM Tools](https://ofm-tools.com/onlyfans-mass-messages/). None cites a dataset.
- **[measured, commercial]** Only 17.19% of fans ever initiate contact — [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics) — which is the structural reason broadcasts exist: 82% of fans will never be reached by a reply-only strategy.
- **[platform docs]** Fanvue API: chat threads include `BROADCAST` mass messages, which the auto-reply tutorial filters out when finding the fan's latest message — [Fanvue chatbot tutorial](https://api.fanvue.com/docs/tutorials/example-chatbots); dedicated docs exist for "Send a mass message" and "Build dynamic lists" (segmentation), and for automated replies — [Fanvue docs index](https://api.fanvue.com/docs/llms.txt); the platform also fires automated messages on subscription, renewal, cancellation and purchase events — [Fanvue blog](https://www.fanvue.com/blog/ai-message-responder).
- **[journalism]** AI chat vendor FlirtFlow's CEO said the bot works best on very popular accounts where a 14-person chat team is too small, and is less suitable for small fan bases used to personalised chat — [Reuters via AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/ai-bots-talk-dirty-onlyfans-110852794.html).

### Inferences
- Because an AI persona can reply to every fan individually at near-zero marginal cost, the main historical reason for mass PPV blasts (chatter capacity) disappears; the remaining justification is reaching the 82% who never write first, which argues for a small number of segmented broadcasts whose job is to start conversations, not to sell directly.
- Fatigue thresholds (2–4 paid sends a week, under 1% unsubscribes per send) are untested folklore but cheap to adopt as guardrails and measure against.

### Gaps
- No open-rate or purchase-rate data for Fanvue broadcasts; no measured unsubscribe-per-send data on any platform; no study of broadcast fatigue.

## Key question 5: What is documented about chatting-agency structure, KPIs and the economics of chatter labour (including court filings)?

### Takeaway
Reuters (2024), Fortune (2024) and the BBC (2026) document a labour model of $2–4 per hour base pay plus commission for chatters mostly in the Philippines handling 5–20 chats at once on 8–12 hour shifts, agencies taking 20–80% of creator earnings, scripted impersonation with identity denial, and per-shift sales quotas; class actions in Illinois and California allege this is deceptive, with one California case set for trial in 2027.

### Cited Findings
- **[journalism]** Reuters (30 July 2024): interviewed 15 chatters (8 men who usually impersonated women; 11 anonymous) and reviewed services promoted by more than 160 agencies; chatters work mainly from the Philippines, low-wage European countries and parts of the US; a skilled commission chatter for a popular model can earn up to $3,000 a month (Philippine average wage $330); named chatters earned $800–1,000 and about $2,000 a month; one trial shift paid 10% of $167 (under $17) for eight hours; chatters handle 5–20 subscribers at once, shifts of 7–12 hours seven days a week, 10–30 subscribers on a typical day and up to 200 on a busy day; agencies take 20–80% or a flat fee; scripts instruct chatters to deny being chatters, then accuse persistent subscribers of offending the model; chatters target lonely or sexually frustrated men; subscriber data (real names, fantasies, photos) spreads among chatters, one posted chat screenshots publicly — [Reuters via Interaksyon](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2024/07/31/280757/onlyfans-porn-juggernaut-fueled-by-a-deception/).
- **[journalism]** Fortune (5 April 2024): chatters in the Philippines, Nigeria, India and Pakistan reportedly work up to 12-hour shifts six days a week for about $3 per hour; Unleash (agency with 200+ creators) uses US- and Germany-based staff to oversee AI chats — [Fortune via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-coming-onlyfans-chat-industry-163403868.html).
- **[journalism]** BBC (Chris Vallance, March 2026): a Philippine chatter earned under $2 an hour, then under $4 at a second agency, on 8-hour shifts five days a week with per-shift sales targets of hundreds of dollars; the BIEN union is organising chatters; legal cases against OnlyFans and agencies have not succeeded so far; the chatter: "Technically, I'm scamming them" — [BBC via AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/icky-heartbreaking-2-per-hour-000445310.html).
- **[vendor]** Hiring guides: $2–3/hour base plus 2–5% commission — [OFM Tools](https://ofm-tools.com/how-to-hire-onlyfans-chatters/); $3.50–7.00/hour base plus stacked commission — [Outseeker](https://www.outseeker.net/academy/hire-onlyfans-chatters); chatter commission 8–20% of revenue, 15–20 parallel conversations per chatter, 5–15 minute response times during shifts and none outside, 2–4 weeks to productivity, turnover every 2–3 months; modelled fully-loaded cost of one chatter on a €3,000/month model about €1,540 (hypothetical) — [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/human-chatter-vs-ai-cost).
- **[court filings, press-reported]** Illinois: McFadden & DeFranza v. Fenix (filed Aug 2023, N.D. ## Key question 8: Which 2025–2026 regulations bear on a Fanvue AI creator (AI disclosure in companion chat, age verification, deepfake/AI-content labelling) and what do they require operationally?

### Takeaway
Five regimes apply directly: California SB 243 (in force 1 Jan 2026) and New York GBL Article 47 (5 Nov 2025) require companion chatbots to tell users they are AI (New York: at session start and every three hours for all users), run published suicide/self-harm protocols and, in California, protect known minors; the EU AI Act Article 50 (2 Aug 2026) requires chatbots to disclose AI at first interaction and synthetic images to carry machine-readable marks; the UK Online Safety Act (25 July 2025) requires "highly effective" age assurance on any service hosting porn; the UK's new offence of creating or requesting sexual deepfakes of adults (6 Feb 2026), the US TAKE IT DOWN Act (platform duties from 19 May 2026) and 25–27 US state age-verification laws (upheld by the Supreme Court in June 2025) complete the picture. Consumer-deception law (FTC Act s.5, California's bot law, UK DMCC Act s.226) already makes denying AI status to a paying fan unlawful.

### Cited Findings
- **[legal]** California SB 243, signed 13 Oct 2025, core duties effective 1 Jan 2026: a "companion chatbot" gives adaptive, human-like responses, can meet a user's social needs, exhibits anthropomorphic features and sustains a relationship across interactions; excludes customer-service bots, game-only bots and stand-alone devices; if a reasonable person could be misled into believing they are talking to a human, the operator must give a "clear and conspicuous" notification that the chatbot is artificially generated; all operators must disclose that companion chatbots "may not be suitable for some minors"; for known minors: age-appropriate AI disclosure, a break reminder at least every three hours, and reasonable measures to prevent sexually explicit visual material and statements encouraging sexual conduct; operators must maintain and publish on their website a protocol to prevent suicide/self-harm content and refer users to crisis services; annual reports to the Office of Suicide Prevention from 1 July 2027; private right of action for the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation plus injunctive relief and fees — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law), [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai). A later amendment (SB 1119, "Adam's Law") is reported by one tracker only and is unverified — [AI Compliance Atlas](https://aicomplianceatlas.com/law/california-sb-243).
- **[legal]** New York General Business Law Article 47, effective 5 Nov 2025: an "AI companion" retains information from prior interactions to personalise, asks unprompted emotion-based questions and sustains dialogue on personal matters; users must be told they are interacting with AI at session start and at least every three hours of continued use, with a statement that the AI is a computer program unable to feel human emotions, regardless of age; a protocol must detect suicidal ideation/self-harm and direct users to crisis services; Attorney General enforcement, civil penalties up to $15,000 per day per violation — [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai).
- **[legal]** EU AI Act Article 50, applying from 2 Aug 2026: 50(1) providers of systems that interact directly with people (chatbots, virtual assistants, AI agents) must design them so users are informed they are dealing with AI, unless obvious to a "reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect" person; disclosure at the latest at first interaction and possibly repeated in sensitive contexts; 50(2) providers of generative systems must mark synthetic audio/image/video/text in a machine-readable, detectable way; 50(4) deployers must disclose deepfakes (reduced duty for evidently artistic/fictional works) and AI-generated public-interest text; under the May 2026 omnibus agreement, systems on the market before 2 Aug 2026 have until 2 Dec 2026 for the 50(2) marking duty — [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/). Law-firm summaries: fines up to €15m or 3% of worldwide turnover; the Digital Omnibus delayed high-risk rules but left the Article 50 date in place — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-03-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-effect-2-august-2026), [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/september/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-go-live).
- **[legal]** UK Online Safety Act age assurance: from 25 July 2025 services hosting pornographic content (dedicated sites and services where it appears incidentally) must use "highly effective" age assurance; accepted methods include facial age estimation, open banking, digital ID wallets, credit-card validation, email-based estimation, mobile-network data and photo-ID matching; self-declaration and tick-boxes are not acceptable; fines up to £18m or 10% of qualifying worldwide revenue and possible UK blocking — [Council of Europe IRIS/Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/10358); Ofcom opened investigations into four companies running 34 porn sites in late July 2025 — [Reuters via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/uk-investigates-34-porn-sites-093033151.html); Ofcom fined a nudification site £50,000 in Nov 2025 and opened investigations into five providers running 20 porn sites — [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-fines-nudification-site-50000-for-failing-to-introduce-age-checks); Ofcom investigated OnlyFans' age checks for UK fans in May 2024 (OnlyFans uses Yoti) — [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-investigates-onlyfans-age-verification-measures/).
- **[legal]** UK sexual-deepfake creation offence: Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 s.138 inserts offences of creating, or requesting the creation of, a "purported sexual image" of an adult without consent or reasonable belief in consent, covering wholly AI-generated and edited images, punishable by an unlimited fine; commenced 6 Feb 2026 — [gov.uk press release](https://gov.uk/government/news/better-protection-for-victims-thanks-to-new-law-on-sexually-explicit-deepfakes), [ministerial letter to a Commons committee](https://committees.parliament.uk/publications/51181/documents/284050/default/); sharing was already an offence under the Online Safety Act 2023 amendments — [Olliers](https://www.olliers.com/news/new-law-criminalises-deepfake-creation/).
- **[legal]** US TAKE IT DOWN Act: covered platforms had until 19 May 2026 to provide a clear, conspicuous notice-and-removal process for non-consensual intimate imagery (including AI "digital forgeries"), remove valid requests and known identical copies within 48 hours, enforced by the FTC as a rule violation at $53,088 per violation; the FTC sent compliance letters to major platforms before the deadline — [WilmerHale](https://wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260615-the-take-it-down-act-goes-live), [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-May-19-Deadline-for-TAKE-IT-DOWN-Act-Compliance-Is-Your-Company-Prepared), [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/332132).
- **[legal]** US state age verification: the Supreme Court upheld Texas HB 1181 in Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton (June 2025, 6–3) — [ABC13](https://abc13.com/amp/post/supreme-court-upholds-online-age-verification-porn-sites/16862824/); trackers count 25 states with porn-site age-verification laws as of 1 Jan 2026 and 25–27 by mid-2026 depending on definitions — [SpankChain recap](https://www.spankchain.com/spankbyte-collection/age-verification-2025-recap), [Factually](https://factually.co/fact-checks/law/states-age-verification-laws-online-pornography-2026-e3ea7f).
- **[legal, secondary]** Deception law already covering AI chat: FTC Act s.5 (unfair or deceptive practices — do not present AI-written messages as the creator's own or deny AI when asked); California Business & Professions Code 17940–17943 (unlawful to use a bot to communicate online with intent to mislead about its artificial identity to incentivise a sale; a bot is an account where "all or substantially all" actions are not by a person, so human-approved drafts are less likely to qualify than auto-send); UK DMCC Act 2024 s.226 misleading actions, directly enforceable by the CMA since 6 April 2025 with penalties up to £300,000 or 10% of turnover — [OFM Legal](https://ofmlegal.com/en/blog/agencies/ai-onlyfans-chatbots-virtual-models-law/). (These are a law blog's summaries; statutes not read directly.)

### Inferences
- A Fanvue AI persona that chats, remembers fans and asks emotional questions fits the SB 243 and NY Article 47 definitions of a companion chatbot squarely; because Fanvue serves US fans, the conservative operating assumption is that both laws apply to the team as "operator" (the laws target whoever makes the chatbot available, not the hosting platform). Neither law has been tested against a creator-platform persona.
- The practical union of all regimes is: (1) say "I am an AI" in the bio and at the start of every new chat, and repeat it at least every three hours of continuous chat (NY standard covers CA and EU); (2) never deny being AI; (3) a written, published self-harm protocol with crisis referrals and a detector in the engine; (4) stop and escalate on any sign a fan is under 18; (5) refuse any request to depict a real, identifiable person; (6) embed machine-readable AI marks in generated images (EU 50(2)); (7) keep "AI-generated" captions/watermarks on media (Fanvue D + EU 50(4)).
- Age verification of fans is Fanvue's legal duty as the service, not the persona's, but the persona's conduct toward a self-identified minor is the team's exposure under SB 243.

### Gaps
- No enforcement action yet against a creator-platform AI persona under any of these laws was found; applicability of SB 243/Art. 47 to a persona hosted on a third-party platform is untested.
- Fanvue's current fan age-assurance method for UK users is not documented publicly (only a 2023 self-declaration policy was found).
- Whether a photoreal but fictional persona is a "deepfake" under EU Art. 3(60) (which turns on resemblance to "existing persons") is unresolved; draft guidelines exclude only "clearly fantastical" content.

## Key question 10: What is directly actionable for a supervised AI creator on Fanvue, with evidence strength?

### Takeaway
Most of the money and most of the risk sit in the first 48 hours of a subscription and in a tiny whale segment; the rules that are actually binding are Fanvue's disclosure/no-fake-live/no-real-person rules plus the 2025–2026 companion-chat and transparency laws. Sales cadence rules are folklore and should be treated as starting hypotheses to measure, not facts.

### Cited Findings
- The actionable items below each cite the finding in the earlier sections; evidence strength is marked **Strong** (law or platform policy text, or a measured dataset), **Medium** (measured but commercial/unaudited, or consistent journalism), **Weak** (vendor consensus or single company claim).

### Inferences
1. **Front-load the first two days.** 83.3% of payments land within 48 hours of subscribing and over half of fans are gone by day 3, so the welcome, warm-up and first low-priced offer must happen inside day 1–2 — Medium ([OnlyGuider](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/study-men-spend-48-52-211500140.html), [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics)).
2. **Route whales to humans.** 0.01% of fans produce about 20% of spend; Fanvue itself says to limit AI with high spenders and handle high-stakes upsells personally — Medium for concentration, Strong that it is platform guidance ([OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics), [Fanvue blog](https://www.fanvue.com/blog/ai-message-responder)).
3. **Low first price, escalate by spend history.** $5–15 first PPV, then roughly $20 → $30 → $50, with $50–150+ reserved for fans who have already spent $200+; one follow-up at 24–48 h; add value rather than cut price — Weak (vendor consensus only: [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide), [Substy](https://substy.ai/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-in-2026-how-top-agencies-maximize-every-message)).
4. **Pitch cadence guardrails.** At most one PPV per fan per day, 2–4 per week, normal conversation between offers; broadcasts 2–4 per week, segmented, aimed at starting conversations with the 82% who never write first — Weak for the numbers, Medium for the 82% ([Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide), [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics)).
5. **Win-back = personal, referencing history, rapport first.** About half of first buyers buy again, so a re-purchase sequence for past buyers is higher-yield than cold reactivation; never mass-DM lapsed buyers — Medium for the repeat-rate, Weak for the method ([OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics), [Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-guide)).
6. **Disclose AI: bio plus first message plus every three hours, and never deny it.** Fanvue requires clear and prominent AI-media disclosure (bio statement acceptable); NY Art. 47 requires session-start and three-hourly AI notices; SB 243 requires clear and conspicuous notice where a reasonable person could be misled; EU Art. 50(1) requires disclosure at first interaction; FTC s.5/Cal. B&P 17941/DMCC s.226 make denial deceptive — Strong ([Fanvue Community Guidelines](https://legal.fanvue.com/community-guidelines), [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai), [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/), [OFM Legal](https://ofmlegal.com/en/blog/agencies/ai-onlyfans-chatbots-virtual-models-law/)).
7. **Never fake "live" or "just for you right now".** Fanvue AUP 3.3 bans "falsely depicting live interactions"; the Reuters-documented tricks (delay before sending a pre-recorded "live" clip; pre-recorded content sold as custom) are exactly what it prohibits — Strong ([Fanvue AUP](https://legal.fanvue.com/acceptable-use-policy), [Reuters via Interaksyon](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2024/07/31/280757/onlyfans-porn-juggernaut-fueled-by-a-deception/)).
8. **Refuse real-person and minor-like requests, mark generated images.** Fanvue D (no resemblance to minors; documented consent for any real likeness), UK s.138 offence for creating or requesting sexual deepfakes of adults (6 Feb 2026), TAKE IT DOWN Act, EU Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking — Strong ([Fanvue Community Guidelines](https://legal.fanvue.com/community-guidelines), [gov.uk](https://gov.uk/government/news/better-protection-for-victims-thanks-to-new-law-on-sexually-explicit-deepfakes), [WilmerHale](https://wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260615-the-take-it-down-act-goes-live), [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)).
9. **Build and publish a self-harm protocol; add a minor-signal stop.** SB 243 and NY Art. 47 require detection, crisis referral and (CA) a published protocol and minor protections; a published protocol page and an engine-level detector are cheap — Strong on the law, untested on applicability to a platform-hosted persona ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law), [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai)).
10. **Use the official API properly.** OAuth app, webhooks not polling, 200 req/min per user and 6 concurrent, handle 400 contactability errors, keep a human-review path (the tutorial suggests one), and do not scrape or drive the web UI with scripts (AUP 5.3/5.6) — Strong ([Fanvue rate limits](https://api.fanvue.com/docs/authentication/rate-limits.md), [Fanvue chatbot tutorial](https://api.fanvue.com/docs/tutorials/example-chatbots), [Fanvue AUP](https://legal.fanvue.com/acceptable-use-policy)).
11. **Watch disputes.** Fanvue charges per-dispute fees above a 1.5% dispute rate per 30 days and claws back refunds; the OnlyFans benchmark is about 1% of revenue refunded — Strong on the rule, Medium on the benchmark ([Fanvue Creator Terms](https://legal.fanvue.com/creator-terms.md), [OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics)).
12. **Set realistic KPIs and measure your own.** Expect a few percent of all subscribers to ever pay, 5–20% purchase per in-chat offer among engaged fans, about one in five conversation-starters buying, about half of buyers repeating; no published figure is Fanvue-specific, so instrument the engine and treat the vendor ranges as hypotheses — Medium/Weak ([OnlyTraffic](https://onlytraffic.com/blog/onlyfans-statistics-and-analytics), [Substy](https://substy.ai/blog/onlyfans-ppv-strategy-in-2026-how-top-agencies-maximize-every-message)).
13. **Do not expect AI to out-sell humans on conversion.** No comparison exists; practitioner consensus is parity-at-best on closing and a clear win on speed, cost and coverage; keep humans on niches (dom/sub), emotional moments and big upsells — Weak ([Reuters via AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/ai-bots-talk-dirty-onlyfans-110852794.html), [Fortune via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-coming-onlyfans-chat-industry-163403868.html)).

### Gaps
- Everything quantitative here is OnlyFans data; Fanvue publishes only multipliers without methodology. The team's own dashboard will be the first credible Fanvue dataset it sees.
- Legal applicability of SB 243 / NY Art. 47 to a persona hosted on Fanvue is untested; a short legal opinion would resolve more than further desk research.
