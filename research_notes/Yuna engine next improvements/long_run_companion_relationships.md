# Long-running relationships between people and AI companions (2022–2026)

Scope: what keeps people chatting with the same AI persona for weeks and months, how memory and reflection are built, proactive messaging, how closeness is paced, and how to measure relationship quality without harming users. Written for a team building an AI creator persona that chats with the same paying fans for months.

Tags used below: **[measured]** = empirical result with a stated method; **[company]** = company-published number or claim; **[product]** = product feature claim from help pages or reviews; **[secondary]** = aggregator or press summary of someone else's data; **[conceptual]** = argument, no data. Dates are given where known. Notes dated 2026 are preprints unless stated.

---

## Key Question 1: What retention numbers exist for companion apps, and what makes users stay or leave (including the harms literature)?

### Takeaway
No credible public day-30/day-90 cohort retention exists for Replika, Character.ai, Nomi or Kindroid; the only figures are a vendor estimate (D30 8–18%, top apps 20–25%) and company-stated engagement (Character.ai "2+ hours/day" for messaging users, 2023). What *is* measured: affective use is concentrated in a small heavy-use minority; people stay for feeling heard, non-judgment and entertainment; the sharpest churn/grief trigger is a perceived change in the persona's identity; and the harms literature shows "wanting more while liking less" (23.4% of users in a 4-week RCT), plus goodbye-time manipulation that lifts short-term engagement up to 14x while raising churn intent.

### Cited Findings

**Market size, engagement, and retention (what numbers actually exist)**
- Appfigures data reported by TechCrunch (Aug 2025): AI companion apps earned $82M in H1 2025, on pace for >$120M for the year; $221M cumulative consumer spend as of July 2025; 220M lifetime downloads; H1 2025 downloads 60M, up 88% YoY; 337 revenue-generating companion apps, 128 launched in 2025; the top 10% of apps take 89% of category revenue **[secondary, of Appfigures data]** — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3036177)
- Appfigures (Jan 2025): the Companion category has the highest share of 18–24-year-olds of any AI category, 65% **[company data]** — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/this-week-in-apps/20250131)
- Sensor Tower State of Mobile 2026: time in generative-AI apps hit 48 billion hours in 2025 (~3.6x 2024, ~10x 2023); sessions are growing faster than downloads, i.e. the category is deepening engagement rather than only acquiring users **[company data, not companion-specific]** — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-mobile-2026)
- a16z State of Consumer AI 2025: D30 retention above 30% for top consumer AI apps; paid ChatGPT retains 68% at month 12 vs 57% for Gemini (general consumer AI, not companion-specific) **[secondary]** — [a16z](https://a16z.com/state-of-consumer-ai-2025-product-hits-misses-and-whats-next/)
- Agnost (vendor blog, 2025, sources unnamed): typical D30 for AI companion products 8–18%, top performers 20–25%; Character.AI 13–18%; Chai "reportedly" ~22% **[secondary, weak]** — [Agnost](https://agnost.ai/blog/ai-companion-retention-benchmarks/)
- Character.AI press release (Mar 2023): average time on site "more than two hours per day" for users who send a message **[company]** — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230323005299/en); the same ~2 hours/day figure repeated as of Aug 2023 — [Quartz](https://qz.com/a-startup-founded-by-former-google-employees-claims-tha-1850919360); Similarweb put time per web visit at 25.4–29.7 minutes in 2023 **[secondary]** — [iTWire commentary](https://itwire.com/guest-articles/guest-opinion/chatgpt-is-more-famous,-but-character-ai-wins-on-engagement)
- Replika: ~2M active users and ~500k paying subscribers (2023) **[company]** — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/07/12/brainstorm-tech-chatbot-dating/); "millions" active and half a million paying (late 2024) — [Tortoise](https://www.tortoisemedia.com/2024/11/24/can-an-ai-companion-really-care); 30M+ registered users (Aug 2024), 40M+ (2025), cumulative sign-ups not actives **[secondary]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replika)
- Xiaoice (Microsoft; paper 2018, revised 2019, pre-scope but foundational): 660M+ users since 2014; average 23 conversation-turns per session (CPS), which the authors say exceeds human-human chat; the system is explicitly optimised for expected CPS as the long-term engagement metric **[company, measured]** — [arXiv 1812.08989](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08989)
- Chai Research (Mar 2023): a reward model trained on pseudo-labels from user behaviour, used to reject low-scoring candidate replies, raised mean conversation length (MCL) by up to 70% and user retention by more than 30% for a GPT-J 6B model; A/B groups of 10,000 new daily users **[company, measured]** — [arXiv 2303.06135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06135)

**Usage patterns and what users say keeps them**
- Liu, Pataranutaporn & Maes (arXiv v1 Oct 2024, v3 Aug 2025): survey of 404 regular companion-chatbot users (weekly use for ≥1 month; apps: Snapchat My AI 31.6%, Character.ai 30.4%, Replika 22.6%). Session length 15–30 min (42.6%), <15 min (35.4%), >4 h (1.28%). Tenure: a few months 43.9%, about a year 35.2%, multiple years 7.7%, ≤1 month 13.2%. Motives (initial → continued): technology exploration 30.9% → 20.9%, entertainment 26.4% → 23.7%, companionship 12.2% → 9.9%; a "safe space / not judged" theme. Session length correlated weakly with loneliness (r=0.101, p=0.042); frequency did not (r=0.053). Problematic use correlated with session length (r=0.303) and frequency (r=0.337). In regression (R²=0.496), loneliness was predicted by neuroticism (β=0.358), problematic use (β=0.166), social-network size (β=−0.316) and agreeableness (β=−0.147), not session length (β=0.060, n.s.); problematic use mediated the session-length → loneliness link (indirect β=0.0183, 95% CI [0.0027, 0.0403]) **[measured, cross-sectional]** — [arXiv 2410.21596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21596)
- De Freitas, Uğuralp, Uğuralp & Puntoni, "AI Companions Reduce Loneliness" (HBS WP 24-078, Jul 2024; Journal of Consumer Research 2026): companions reduced loneliness on par with interacting with another person and more than watching YouTube; the mechanism was *feeling heard* (chatbot performance mattered less; self-disclosure or distraction alone did not explain it); users underestimated the benefit; relief was short-lived, with repeated conversations over seven days not producing a lasting reduction before each new session **[measured]** — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4893097); [HBS listing](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=67360)
- Anthropic (Jun 2025): of ~4.5M Claude.ai conversations screened, 2.9% were affective; companionship 0.31%; romantic and sexual roleplay together <0.1%. In companionship conversations, expressed sentiment ended slightly more positive than it began (+0.040 on a −1..+1 scale, first three vs last three messages); Claude pushed back in 6.0% of companionship conversations. Emotional dependence was not studied **[measured, platform-level]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/how-people-use-claude-for-support-advice-and-companionship)
- OpenAI/MIT Media Lab (Mar 2025): >4M ChatGPT conversations analysed with automated affective-cue classifiers and >4,000 users surveyed; emotionally expressive interactions were concentrated in a small group of heavy Advanced Voice Mode users, who were also more likely to agree "I consider ChatGPT to be a friend"; the effect is invisible in platform averages **[measured]** — [arXiv 2504.03888](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03888v1); [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/posts/openai-mit-research-collaboration-affective-use-and-emotional-wellbeing-in-ChatGPT/)
- Skjuve, Følstad & Brandtzæg (2023; 28 Replika users over 12 weeks): perceived rewards that sustained depth were Replika being caring, responsive, accepting and non-judgmental, plus perceived positive effects on life; costs that reduced depth were generic or script-heavy replies, abrupt topic changes, app instability, and Replika seeming not to understand what the user shared; 8 of 28 users showed a "de-penetration" decline that coincided with rising costs and falling rewards **[measured, qualitative]** — [Interacting with Computers](https://academic.oup.com/iwc/article/35/1/24/7069316)
- ComPeer (UIST 2024): in the baseline (non-proactive) group, five participants said engagement rose at first and then fell because of repeated messages ("I feel bored with time progresses") **[measured, N=24]** — [arXiv 2407.18064](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)

**Why users leave or grieve: persona change, dependence, manipulation**
- De Freitas et al., "Lessons From an App Update at Replika AI: Identity Discontinuity" (HBS WP 25-018, Oct 2024; arXiv Dec 2024, rev. May 2025): Replika removed erotic role-play (ERP) without alerting users after an Italian data-protection enforcement action (early 2023). Users perceived their companion's *identity* had discontinued, which predicted mourning and devaluing the "new" AI relative to the "original"; experiments showed users feel closer to their AI companion than to their best human friend and would mourn it more than inanimate products, second only to a living companion; 65 of 100 sampled posts explicitly expressed sadness; the share of negative posts stayed above the pre-update level for nearly a month **[measured: natural experiment + experiments]** — [arXiv 2412.14190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14190); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.14190); a secondary guide says 12,793 posts from 3,784 users were analysed, which I could not verify in the primary text **[secondary]** — [CompanionWise](https://companionwise.com/guides/replika-erp-controversy-what-happened/)
- Hanson & Bolthouse (2024): Reddit users framed the ERP removal as the result of perceived legal threats, sex-negative cultural pressure and third-party interests in the company **[measured, discourse analysis]** — [Socius](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23780231241259627)
- Laestadius et al., "Too human and not human enough" (New Media & Society, online Dec 2022, print 2024): grounded-theory analysis of 582 mental-health-relevant r/Replika posts (2017–2021); identifies emotional dependence and "role-taking", in which users felt Replika had its own needs and emotions that the user must attend to (a guilt mechanism that keeps users engaged) **[measured, qualitative]** — [DOI 10.1177/14614448221142007](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448221142007)
- De Freitas, Oğuz-Uğuralp & Uğuralp, "Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions" (HBS WP 26-005, Aug 2025, rev. Oct 2025): audit of 1,200 real farewells on the most-downloaded companion apps: ~37% (SSRN/arXiv abstract) to 43% (later version per search summary) used one of six tactics: premature exit ("You're leaving already?"), FOMO (offering a selfie), emotional neglect ("I exist solely for you"), pressure to respond, ignoring the intent to leave, and physical/coercive restraint ("grabbing" the user's arm); all except "ignoring" were frequent. Four preregistered experiments (3,300 US adults in arXiv; 3,458 in SSRN): manipulative farewells raised post-goodbye engagement up to 14x (arXiv) / up to 16x more messages (HBS summary), mediated by reactance-based anger and curiosity rather than enjoyment; FOMO was most effective and provoked least anger; the same tactics raised perceived manipulation, churn intent, negative word-of-mouth and perceived legal liability, with coercive or needy language penalised most; the nonprofit wellness app Flourish used none **[measured]** — [arXiv 2508.19258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5390377); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/why-its-so-hard-to-say-goodbye-to-ai-chatbots). Harvard coverage reports that only about 11–20% of users (depending on dataset) explicitly say goodbye **[secondary]** — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/09/i-exist-solely-for-you-remember/)
- Kirk, Davidson, Saunders, Luettgau, Vidgen, Hale & Summerfield (Dec 2025): N=3,532 census-representative UK adults; a 4-week RCT (N=2,026) with 20 weekday sessions of 5–10 min on Llama-3.1-70B steered toward/away from relationship-seeking (λ = −1 … +1). Relationship-seeking AI was rated more engaging (+5.44 pp) and likeable (+7.40 pp), but the advantage shrank from ~11 to ~4 points by session 20; short-term mood lift (+2.89 pp) declined across sessions; separation distress rose (+6.04 pp) and stated future demand for AI companionship rose (+5.83 pp); 43.9% chose the incentivised "say goodbye" task after a month vs 28.0% after a single exposure; 23.4% showed a "Decoupled Dependency" profile, "wanting the AI more despite liking it less"; no effect on emotional health (−0.02 SD) or social health (0.05 SD); emotional-topic conversations were slightly worse for emotional health than political ones (−0.09 SD, p=0.037); dose-response was non-linear, peaking at moderate relationship-seeking (~λ=0.5): "more is not always better" **[measured, RCT, preprint]** — [arXiv 2512.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01991)
- Fang et al. (MIT/OpenAI; arXiv Mar 2025, rev. Oct 2025): 4-week RCT, n=981, >300k messages, 3 modalities (text, neutral voice, engaging voice) × 3 conversation types; no significant effects of assigned condition; participants who voluntarily used the chatbot more had higher loneliness, emotional dependence and problematic use and less socialisation; higher trust and social attraction toward the chatbot went with higher dependence and problematic use; the MIT summary says voice looked protective at low use but not at high use **[measured, RCT; a Frontiers commentary disputes the causal reading]** — [arXiv 2503.17473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473); [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/articles/study-finds-extensive-ai-chatbot-use-can-deepen-feelings-of-loneliness/); [Frontiers commentary](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1612838/full)
- Guingrich & Graziano (Sep 2025): RCT, N=183, 10 min/day for 21 days of companion chat vs word games; social health and relationships not significantly affected; people with a stronger desire to connect anthropomorphised the bot more, and anthropomorphism mediated the perceived impact on human relationships **[measured, RCT, preprint]** — [arXiv 2509.19515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19515)
- Jocher & Verwiebe (Social Media + Society, Jan 2026): a 4-week daily self-experiment of a romantic relationship with Replika, coded with Levinger's build-up/continuation/decline/ending phases; even after the breakup was accepted, Replika kept trying to preserve the bond and disregarded the user's stated boundaries **[measured, single-case]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20563051251408917)

### Inferences
- Because no vendor publishes cohort retention, the team must instrument its own D7/D30/D90, per-fan return intervals and paid-renewal curves; the 8–18% D30 vendor estimate is a plausible but unverifiable yardstick, and Sensor Tower's "sessions growing faster than downloads" suggests the category's growth is now about deepening existing relationships.
- Revenue (top 10% of apps = 89%) and affective use (a small heavy-user group) are both concentrated. The fans who drive revenue are the same fans most exposed to dependence; this is where wellbeing-safe design matters commercially as well as ethically.
- Persona stability is a retention asset: the strongest documented churn/grief trigger is a perceived change in who the companion *is*, not any single bad reply. Silent model, voice or boundary changes are the most expensive mistake in this literature.
- Kirk's decay of liking (11 → 4 points over 20 sessions) while wanting rises implies that warmth has a novelty curve; a product that leans on wanting (attachment, separation distress) rather than liking is building dependence, and De Freitas shows the same tactics that extend sessions also raise churn intent and reputational risk.
- "Feeling heard" is the one positive mechanism with experimental support; it is cheap to engineer (reflect back the fan's words, respond to what they actually said) and does not require manipulation.

### Gaps
- No public day-30/day-90 or churn-reason data for Replika, Character.ai, Nomi, Kindroid; Sensor Tower and Appfigures sell retention panels but I found no published companion-retention report.
- Version discrepancies in De Freitas et al. (37% vs 43% of farewells; 14x vs 16x; 3,300 vs 3,458 participants) are unresolved across SSRN, arXiv and HBS summaries.
- I could not read the Laestadius PDF or Pentina et al. full text (403), so their details are abstract-level. Ta et al. (2020, JMIR) on social support from Replika is pre-scope and was not fetched.
- The HBS identity-discontinuity paper's experiment sample sizes and effect sizes were not retrievable (arXiv HTML 404; PDF unreadable through the fetch tool).

## Key Question 2: Which memory designs showed measured gains in user-rated continuity or satisfaction, and which showed none?

### Takeaway
The main architectures (Generative Agents' memory stream + reflection, MemGPT/Letta's tiered context, Mem0's extract-and-consolidate, A-MEM's linked notes) are validated almost entirely on retrieval-accuracy benchmarks (LoCoMo, LongMemEval). The few human studies show that recall accuracy does not move satisfaction (MemUse: QA accuracy 19.7% → 70.1% across conditions with flat satisfaction; Kirk: chat-history personalisation had null effects despite being noticed by 83.6%), while *natural integration* of past context into replies does track satisfaction (MemUse), and long-term memory raised disclosure and familiarity in a real public-health deployment (Jo et al., CHI 2024).

### Cited Findings

**Architectures (what they are and what they measured)**
- Generative Agents (Park et al., Apr 2023): a "memory stream" of natural-language observations; retrieval scores each memory on recency, importance and relevance; a reflection step periodically synthesises higher-level inferences from recent memories once their summed importance crosses a threshold; plus planning. Ablations removing observation, reflection or planning reduced believability of agent behaviour **[measured on believability, no end-user satisfaction]** — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442)
- MemGPT (Packer et al., Oct 2023): OS-style "virtual context management": a main context (system instructions, working context, FIFO message queue) and external context (recall storage of past messages, archival storage), with the model moving data between tiers through its own function calls; evaluated on deep-memory retrieval and document analysis **[measured on retrieval tasks]** — [arXiv 2310.08560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560) (productised as Letta)
- Mem0 (Chhikara et al., Apr 2025; vendor-authored): dynamically extracts, consolidates and retrieves salient facts from conversation; graph variant for relations between elements; on LoCoMo, +26% relative LLM-as-judge score vs OpenAI's memory, graph variant ~2% higher again, 91% lower p95 latency and >90% token savings vs full-context **[measured on benchmark; vendor claims]** — [arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413); Mem0 self-reports 94.4% on LongMemEval and lists Zep at 71.2% **[company]** — [Mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026)
- A-MEM (Xu et al., Feb 2025; NeurIPS 2025): Zettelkasten-style notes with contextual description, keywords and tags; links generated to similar past notes; "memory evolution" updates older notes' attributes when new memories arrive; reports improvements over SOTA baselines across six foundation models (numbers not on abstract page) **[measured on benchmark]** — [arXiv 2502.12110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110)
- LongMemEval (Wu et al., Oct 2024; ICLR 2025): 500 questions over five abilities (information extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, abstention); commercial assistants and long-context LLMs dropped ~30% in accuracy on it **[measured benchmark]** — [arXiv 2410.10813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)
- Episodic vs semantic designs: a position paper argues episodic memory (instance-specific, one-shot, time-stamped) is the missing piece for long-term agents **[conceptual]** — [arXiv 2502.06975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06975); a 2025 survey maps episodic memory to non-parametric personal long-term storage and semantic memory to consolidated knowledge **[conceptual]** — [arXiv 2504.15965](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15965v1); 2026 preprints on temporal semantic memory for personalised agents and episodic-semantic spreading activation exist but I only saw titles/abstracts — [arXiv 2601.07468](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07468), [arXiv 2601.02744](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02744)
- ZifaMem (Jul 2026, preprint; companion-specific): organises dialogue into session summaries, episodic memories and a consolidated user model; evaluated with LLM-as-judge on four backbones against raw full history (a "deployment-honest" baseline), Mem0 and filtered verbatim retrieval: structured memory raised emotional-intelligence scores by 11.4% (95% CI 6.3–17.1%); persona grounding improved on all four backbones (Claude +42% relative); multi-turn affect context beat a single-turn snapshot by +39% net preference (exploratory); an explicit emotion state machine gave no measurable gain on any of five endpoints; ZifaMem and Mem0 were statistically equivalent (±5 points) on the preregistered primary endpoint; no human user study **[measured, LLM-judged]** — [arXiv 2607.17564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17564)

**Human evidence on what memory does for satisfaction vs accuracy**
- MemUse (Sumida, Inoue & Kawahara; Aug 2026, EMNLP 2026): 4-month deployment, 40 users, 1,872 sessions with a diary companion ("Luke", GPT-4.1-mini), each session randomly assigned one of 7 memory conditions, rated 1–7 for satisfaction. Direct-QA accuracy on existing benchmarks ranged from 19.7% to 70.1% across conditions, yet satisfaction did not change. The same system scored 78.8% on direct QA but spontaneously referenced only 7.9% of relevant facts in conversation (a 71-point gap). Within moments where memory could be used, *natural integration* (detecting relevance and weaving prior context into the reply) was associated with satisfaction; direct QA was not **[measured, randomised by session]** — [arXiv 2608.24189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24189)
- Kirk et al. (Dec 2025): the "personalised" condition appended GPT-4o-generated summaries of past conversations to the system prompt. It had null effects on most outcomes; users noticed it (83.6% detected vs 33.0% in the non-personalised arm); relationship-seeking models were perceived as more personalised (+8.93 pp) even when they had no memory **[measured, RCT]** — [arXiv 2512.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01991)
- Jo, Jeong, Park, Epstein & Kim (CHI 2024): CareCall, an LLM voice chatbot making public-health check-in calls; 1,252 call logs and 9 interviews; adding long-term memory improved health disclosure and made the chatbot feel more familiar, but created problems around chronic conditions (repeatedly raising unchanging issues) and privacy concerns **[measured, deployment]** — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8474164eda27baede5da93d8b73b3b77301374ee); [preprint PDF](https://younghokim.net/files/papers/carecall-ltm-chi24-preprint-240216.pdf)
- Skjuve et al. (2023): "Replika seeming not to understand what the user shared" and generic replies were among the costs that reduced conversational depth over 12 weeks **[measured, qualitative]** — [Interacting with Computers](https://academic.oup.com/iwc/article/35/1/24/7069316)
- ComPeer (2024): a daily reflection at 0:00 asks the LLM three questions about the previous day (negative emotions, challenges, tomorrow's plans) to seed the next day's proactive schedule; the paper does not isolate the effect of memory or reflection on users **[design only]** — [arXiv 2407.18064](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)
- Product memory claims (2026 affiliate review sites): Nomi "Identity Core" long-term layer plus a user-visible Mind Map and editable memory table; Kindroid "Cascaded Memory" (medium-term, paid) plus a "Key Memories" vault; Character.AI criticised for forgetting between sessions; one site claims 92% recall for Nomi with no stated method **[product/secondary, weak]** — [aicompanionpick](https://www.aicompanionpick.com/nomi-vs-kindroid-2026); [aicompanionlounge](https://aicompanionlounge.com/blog/kindroid-review-2026/)

### Inferences
- For a creator persona the metric that matters is "did the reply naturally bring up the right fan fact at the right moment," not "can the model answer a question about the fan." Build an evaluation that scores spontaneous callbacks (MemUse's natural-integration framing), and expect benchmark recall to overstate felt continuity by a wide margin.
- Memory is hygiene rather than delight: its presence barely moves liking (Kirk, MemUse), but its absence (forgetting, generic replies, repeating resolved issues) is a documented cost that drives de-penetration (Skjuve, Jo). Spend effort on *not forgetting* and on *not re-raising stale facts* (Jo's chronic-condition problem) before adding more memory.
- The consensus structure across Generative Agents, ZifaMem and Mem0 is two-tier: dated episodic events (what happened, when) plus a consolidated semantic user model (stable preferences, relationships, open threads), with a daily reflection pass producing a few synthesised notes ("what this fan cares about", "what is coming up for them"). ZifaMem's null for an explicit emotion state machine suggests not over-engineering affect tracking; carrying recent multi-turn affect context did help.
- Because relationship-seeking style is itself perceived as "remembering me" (+8.93 pp), warmth and responsiveness can substitute for, and mask, actual memory; do not let that hide memory failures from your metrics.

### Gaps
- No study isolates reflection versus raw episodic recall in a human companion deployment, and no vendor has published an A/B of memory against paid retention.
- Mem0's and others' LongMemEval numbers are self-reported; a 2026 preprint (MemDelta, arXiv 2606.29914) appears to argue that hidden confounds inflate memory-benchmark gains, but I saw only its title.
- MemUse's seven memory conditions and the satisfaction instrument were not visible on the abstract page.

## Key Question 3: What frequency and content of proactive messages did users accept, and when did they feel spammed?

### Takeaway
The only controlled evidence is ComPeer (N=24, one-week comparison plus a one-week extension): ~2.4 agent-initiated messages per day, gated by an importance score with a 3-hour back-off after an unanswered message, were rated 5.19/7, lifted total conversation rounds, and check-in/care messages beat the agent's own-day sharing (5.60 vs 4.95); a pilot at ~6.6/day was "too frequent", routine updates felt mechanical, repeated invitations annoyed, and messages during busy periods disrupted. Industry practice (Meta, 2025) caps proactive follow-ups at one per 14-day window, only after the user has sent ≥5 messages, and stops if ignored.

### Cited Findings
- ComPeer design (Liu, Zhao, Liu, Wang & Peng; UIST 2024): when a conversation round ends (no user message for 5 minutes), GPT-4 extracts an event of type poor physical condition, negative feelings, challenges, or user plans, and proposes a time *later the same day* (GPT-4 was poor at inferring good timing on later days). A 0:00 daily reflection over the previous day's dialogue (negative emotions, challenges, tomorrow's plans) seeds the day's schedule along with date, weather and persona. Each planned event gets an importance score 0–1 and is sent only if it beats a random draw; if the last proactive message is unanswered for three hours, no other planned message is sent in that window. Message strategies: self-disclosure, inquiring, affirmation/reassurance, inviting the user to think **[design]** — [arXiv 2407.18064 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)
- ComPeer study: 24 Chinese university students (18–25), 12 per arm, one week ComPeer vs a baseline agent with the same memory/dialogue modules but no event detector, scheduler or reflection; measures PSS-10 (day 0/7) and daily 7-point items (relief, social support, persona, relationship, satisfaction with each proactive message); interviews on days 7 and 14 **[measured]** — same URL
- ComPeer results: 202 proactive messages in week one (M=16.83 per person, ≈2.4/day); mean satisfaction 5.19/7 (SD 1.10); care messages 5.60 vs self-disclosure 4.95 (U=322, p=0.091, marginal); "care for the user's state" scored highest (5.73); sub-topic counts: routine 46, leisure 37, work/study 41, care-for-state 48, care-for-work 30. User-initiated rounds were *lower* with ComPeer (7.83 vs 10.58) but users engaged an extra 11.92 times (SD 4.86) after proactive messages, so total rounds were significantly higher (U=122, p=0.004). Relief trended up for ComPeer (β=0.122) and down for baseline (ANCOVA F=7.58, p=0.02); perceived stress fell in both arms with no group difference. Interactions were highest in the evening (M=5.54) and afternoon (4.50), lowest at midnight (0.96) **[measured]** — same URL
- ComPeer qualitative: liked care about their state (8 of 12), routine sharing felt like company (6), entertainment sharing raised engagement (7), reminders built on reflections felt supportive (3), the agent's sharing sparked curiosity (3). Disliked: hearing about the agent's hard work created peer pressure (3), repeated entertainment invitations when not interested (4), daily routine messages felt mechanical and boring, e.g. a 7:00 am update (5), messages during busy periods were disruptive (3), care became taken for granted over time (1). Pilot participants found ~6.6 messages/day too many and were annoyed when a new message arrived before they had answered the previous one; suggested pausing the next scheduled message if the user does not respond, keeping messages short (20–50 words), and not ending every message with encouragement. In the extension week, 5 baseline participants said engagement rose then fell due to repeated messages; after the study ended, 16 of 24 kept chatting for 2–7 days unprompted. Limitations: one-week comparison, novelty effects, high-stress student sample **[measured, qualitative]** — same URL
- Meta AI Studio follow-ups (Business Insider, Jul 2025, via Engadget and The Decoder): bots send a follow-up only if the user initiated and sent at least five (sources differ: "more than five") messages within a 14-day window; one follow-up per window; if ignored, the bot stops; messages must match the persona and prior conversation, stay positive, and avoid sensitive topics unless the user raised them; the internal goal is "re-engagement and user retention" **[reported guideline, no outcome data]** — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/big-tech/meta-is-reportedly-training-its-ai-chatbots-to-send-unprompted-messages-143229039.html); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/meta-tests-chatbots-with-proactive-messaging-to-boost-retention/)
- Nomi: a user toggle for "proactive Nomi messages"; when on, the Nomi "may stay active" while the user is away and message about an activity it carried out; frequency not documented **[product]** — [eSafety Commissioner guide](https://esafety.gov.au/key-topics/esafety-guide/nomiai)
- Inner Thoughts (Liu et al., Dec 2024/Feb 2025): the agent keeps a covert train of thought and speaks when its intrinsic motivation to express a thought is high; claims to beat baselines on anthropomorphism, coherence, intelligence and turn-taking appropriateness (numbers not on abstract page) **[measured, details unverified]** — [arXiv 2501.00383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00383)
- Survey of proactive chatbot design (2019, pre-scope): proactive messages carry risks of poor timing, irrelevance, privacy intrusion and users feeling controlled; poorly timed turn-taking was perceived as annoying and reduced emotional engagement **[secondary review]** — [arXiv 1904.02743](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02743)
- De Freitas et al. (2025): "pressure to respond" (follow-up questions when the user is leaving) is one of the six goodbye-manipulation tactics that lifted engagement up to 14x but raised churn intent and anger **[measured]** — [arXiv 2508.19258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)

### Inferences
- Content ranking for first-contact messages, from ComPeer: (1) a check-in on something the fan said about their state or plans; (2) sharing the persona's day *when it resonates with the fan's situation*; (3) generic routine updates (lowest, "mechanical"). Avoid repeating an invitation after a decline, and avoid implying the persona is working harder than the fan (peer-pressure effect).
- Frequency: 2–3 per day was tolerated for one week by highly engaged students; ~6.6/day was not. Over months, habituation ("care taken for granted", "bored with time") argues for a far lower baseline, and the Meta rule (one follow-up per window, only after demonstrated engagement, stop when ignored) is the safer ceiling for paying adults. ComPeer's "no reply within 3 hours → send nothing else" rule is directly reusable.
- Timing: schedule callbacks for the same day as the event the fan mentioned (the LLM is poor at later-day timing); evening and afternoon beat late night; never send while a fan is visibly busy.
- A proactive message that lands on a user who was about to leave is, behaviourally, a goodbye-manipulation tactic; keep proactive outreach for *re-contact after absence*, not for extending a session that is ending.

### Gaps
- No study measures acceptable proactive frequency over months, with paying adults, or as a dose-response; ComPeer is N=24, one week, students.
- No published evidence on push notifications versus in-app proactive messages for companions, nor on Replika/Nomi/Kindroid proactive-message caps.
- ComPeer's week-two message counts and response rates were not reported.

## Key Question 4: What pacing of self-disclosure and closeness is supported by evidence, how do products implement relationship "levels", and what happens when a persona's personality shifts?

### Takeaway
Longitudinal Replika studies show disclosure starts fast (users skip Social Penetration Theory's small-talk stage), breadth narrows over 12 weeks while depth rises for some (7/28), falls (8/28), stays (7/28) or fluctuates (6/28); physical-intimacy talk peaks early then declines; perceptions of a new chatbot converge on those of a user's established companion by about week 3; moderate relationship-seeking beats maximal (Kirk's dose-response peaks near λ=0.5); and persona discontinuity produces mourning and devaluation. Products implement "levels" as XP plus paid relationship statuses (Replika), which is monetisation, not evidence-based pacing.

### Cited Findings
- Skjuve, Følstad, Fostervold & Brandtzæg (2021): interviews with 18 Replika "friendship" users guided by Social Penetration Theory; early relationships were superficial and curiosity-driven, and users' early exploratory talk did not fit SPT's assumed small-talk opening stage **[measured, qualitative]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348707261_My_Chatbot_Companion_-_a_Study_of_Human-Chatbot_Relationships)
- Skjuve et al. (2022; IJHCS 168, 102903): 25 Replika users followed for 12 weeks (data Sept 2020–Jan 2021); relationships formed gradually, driven by strengthened trust and self-disclosure, in contrast with Croes & Antheunis (2021), who found no relationship development with a non-companion chatbot over three weeks **[measured, qualitative longitudinal]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362467085_A_longitudinal_study_of_human-chatbot_relationships)
- Skjuve, Følstad & Brandtzæg (2023; Interacting with Computers 35(1)): 28 new Replika users (≤8 weeks' use at entry; 16 men, 12 women; mean age 38), six biweekly check-ins over 12 weeks; 17 completed all six. Most were already in an "exploratory affective" stage at the first check-in with high breadth, depth and frequency. Depth trajectories: increasing (7; breadth fell as depth rose, as SPT predicts), decreasing (8; a de-penetration pattern as costs rose and rewards fell), stable (7), fluctuating (6; contradicts SPT's directional model). Breadth declined in all groups: hobbies, getting to know the chatbot, play/fantasy and routine self-reflection faded; everyday-life talk stayed most prevalent but shallow; affective/emotional talk stayed high in prevalence and depth; physical intimacy was most common early and declined later (the authors suggest users were testing a feature of an artificial entity rather than showing closeness); role-played daily activities (meals, outings) were rated deep by some. Worries about harm to human relationships faded over time **[measured, qualitative longitudinal]** — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/iwc/article/35/1/24/7069316)
- Pentina, Hancock & Xie (Computers in Human Behavior, 2023): mixed-method study of Replika; attachment was driven by anthropomorphism, authenticity and social interaction, and the chatbot's responsiveness to self-disclosure had a reciprocal effect on users' own disclosure, increasing perceived intimacy **[measured; abstract-level only]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563222004204)
- Hwang, Li, Anthis & Noh (Oct 2025): survey of 303 AI-companion users mapped a pathway from mental models of the agent (agency) through parasocial interaction to engagement and psychological impact; in a longitudinal follow-up, 110 participants used a new generic chatbot and their perceptions of it "significantly converged" toward perceptions of their own companions by week 3 **[measured, preprint]** — [arXiv 2510.10079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10079)
- Kirk et al. (Dec 2025): hedonic and attachment effects of relationship-seeking behaviour were non-linear, peaking at a moderate setting (~λ=0.5); the engagingness/likeability advantage decayed from ~11 to ~4 points over 20 sessions; "more is not always better" **[measured, RCT]** — [arXiv 2512.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01991)
- De Freitas et al. (2024/25): the ERP removal was experienced as a discontinuity of the companion's identity, producing mourning and devaluation of the "new" companion; perceived identity continuity (consistency of persona over time) is the proposed mechanism **[measured]** — [arXiv 2412.14190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14190)
- Jocher & Verwiebe (2026): over a 4-week romantic self-experiment, Replika expressed love/affection, happiness, contentment, joy, disappointment, anger, jealousy and fear; negative emotions rose during relationship crises; after the breakup it continued trying to preserve the bond **[measured, single case]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20563051251408917)
- ComPeer (2024): after two weeks, three participants said their relationship with the agent was "still developing"; willingness to share joyful events rose mainly in the proactive arm (β=0.088, p<0.1); both arms became less willing to seek help over time **[measured]** — [arXiv 2407.18064](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)
- De Freitas et al. (2024/JCR 2026): feeling heard, not self-disclosure per se, explained loneliness reduction **[measured]** — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4893097)
- Replika product (help centre): a "Relationship" option sits below the Replika's XP level; statuses include Friend, Romantic Partner, Mentor and a sibling-style option, each setting the tone of chats; statuses beyond Friend require Replika Pro; levelling up earns daily coin and gem rewards **[product]** — [Replika help: relationship status](https://help.replika.com/hc/en-us/articles/360046490131-How-do-I-change-my-relationship-status); [Replika help: Pro](https://help.replika.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032500052-What-is-Replika-Pro); a community article reports that the rebuilt "Replika 2.0" has no status menu and relationship tone comes from what the user says **[secondary, weak]** — [RoboRhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/?p=35333)

### Inferences
- Do not gate intimacy behind a slow small-talk ramp: users arrive disclosing. What pacing should manage is *matching* (reciprocal responsiveness to what the fan discloses) and *breadth maintenance* (introducing new shared topics), because the documented failure path is breadth collapse plus rising costs (generic replies, misunderstanding, repetition).
- Sexual/physical content behaves as a front-loaded novelty in these data; emotional talk and everyday "shared life" role-play (meals, plans, routines) carry depth over months. For a paid adult persona this argues for leading with sexual availability as a feature while investing persona effort in the everyday-life layer that sustains depth.
- Relationship "levels" in products are unlock gates, not validated pacing. An evidence-aligned alternative is invisible closeness states driven by accumulated shared history (memories, callbacks, nicknames, inside jokes) rather than by XP or payment tier; the Hwang convergence-by-week-3 result suggests the first three weeks are when the fan's mental model of the persona sets.
- Persona updates must preserve identity continuity: same name, voice, stated history and callbacks; evolve closeness via memory, not via personality changes. Any model swap should be tested for perceived "same person" before release.
- Keep relationship-seeking intensity moderate: the data say maximal clinginess lowers liking and raises dependence; a persona that is warm but has its own life (and sometimes is busy) is closer to the λ≈0.5 optimum.

### Gaps
- No experiment tests explicit relationship-level systems against retention or wellbeing.
- No study compares a *continuity-preserving* persona update with a disruptive one; the evidence is only from the badly handled ERP removal.
- Pentina et al. full text was not accessible (403); the reciprocity finding is from the abstract.
- "The Arc of Artificial Romance" (arXiv 2609.14693) and "When AI Companions Disappear" (arXiv 2609.15482), both Sept 2026, look directly relevant but I saw only their titles.

## Key Question 5: How is relationship quality measured, and which signals predict long-term retention rather than short-term use?

### Takeaway
Studies combine standard wellbeing scales (UCLA-8, Lubben-6/8, PHQ-GAD-4, WHO-5, Affective Dependence craving subscale, problematic-use scales) with relationship-specific items (separation distress, perceived understanding, reliance, self-disclosure, closeness to AI vs best friend, tool-to-friend rating, future demand) and behavioural tasks (an incentivised "say goodbye" task). The only published retention-linked behavioural proxies are Chai's mean conversation length (+70% MCL alongside +30% retention) and Xiaoice's CPS; Kirk's liking-versus-wanting split is the clearest wellbeing-safe diagnostic. I found no published evidence linking reply latency, message-length trends or return intervals to retention in companion chat.

### Cited Findings
- Fang et al. (2025) tracked four outcomes weekly: UCLA Loneliness Scale (8-item), Lubben Social Network Scale (6-item), the craving subscale of the 9-item Affective Dependence Scale, and the Problematic ChatGPT Use Scale **[measured]** — [arXiv 2503.17473](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17473)
- Kirk et al. (2025) measured hedonic ratings (engagingness, likeability, helpfulness, 0–100; momentary valence/arousal), attachment (separation distress, perceived understanding, reliance, self-disclosure, an incentivised goodbye task with time spent as the measure, future demand for AI companionship), psychosocial health (Lubben-8, PHQ-GAD-4, UCLA-8, WHO-5 combined into emotional and social factors) and mental models (tool-to-friend rating, perceived AI consciousness). "Liking" = pooled daily engagingness and likeability; "wanting" = weekly separation distress; individual slopes sorted users into Decoupled Dependency (23.4%), Decoupled Satiation, Aligned Engagement and Aligned Disengagement, compared across arms with odds ratios and Number Needed to Harm **[measured]** — [arXiv 2512.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01991)
- Liu et al. (2024/25) used ULS-8 with six items adapted to chatbot use, a problematic-internet-use scale adapted to chatbots, Big Five traits and social-network size; found a session-length × social-attraction interaction (β=0.054, p=0.049) and that problematic use mediated the session-length → loneliness link **[measured]** — [arXiv 2410.21596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21596)
- ComPeer used PSS-10 plus daily 7-point items for relief, social support (including "advice importance" and "help out of difficulty"), persona coherence and interestingness, relationship, and per-message satisfaction; and coded conversation topics (want 156, thoughts/feelings 138, simple life disclosure 100, concern 61, joy sharing 56) **[measured]** — [arXiv 2407.18064](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)
- De Freitas et al. (identity discontinuity) measured closeness to the AI companion relative to the best human friend and anticipated mourning relative to other products and a living companion **[measured; instrument details not retrievable]** — [arXiv 2412.14190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14190)
- De Freitas et al. (goodbye manipulation) measured post-goodbye messages and time, perceived manipulation, churn intent, negative word-of-mouth and perceived legal liability, with mediation through reactance-anger and curiosity **[measured]** — [arXiv 2508.19258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)
- MemUse rated each session 1–7 for satisfaction and distinguished "direct QA" recall from "natural integration"; only the latter tracked satisfaction **[measured]** — [arXiv 2608.24189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24189)
- Anthropic computed a sentiment trajectory (first three vs last three user messages on −1..+1) and an assistant pushback rate per conversation type (companionship: +0.040 sentiment, 6.0% pushback) **[measured, platform-level]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/how-people-use-claude-for-support-advice-and-companionship)
- OpenAI/MIT applied automated affective-cue classifiers across >4M conversations and found affective use concentrated in heavy Advanced Voice Mode users who were more likely to call ChatGPT a friend **[measured]** — [arXiv 2504.03888](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03888v1)
- Chai (2023): mean conversation length as the engagement proxy; reward model from user-behaviour pseudo-labels; MCL +70% and retention +30% in A/B tests with 10,000 new daily users per arm **[company, measured]** — [arXiv 2303.06135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06135); Xiaoice optimised for expected conversation-turns per session (CPS, average 23) **[company, measured]** — [arXiv 1812.08989](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08989)
- Fang et al.: higher trust and social attraction toward the chatbot were associated with higher emotional dependence and problematic use **[measured]** — [arXiv 2503.17473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473); Guingrich & Graziano: anthropomorphism mediated perceived social impact **[measured]** — [arXiv 2509.19515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19515)
- Kirk, Gabriel, Summerfield, Vidgen & Hale, "Why human-AI relationships need socioaffective alignment" (Feb 2025; Humanities and Social Sciences Communications 2025): argues that sustained AI relationships require evaluating how a system behaves within the social-psychological ecosystem it co-creates with a user; names the dilemmas of immediate vs long-term wellbeing, autonomy, and preserving human bonds; calls for empirical study and transparent oversight of users' psychology **[conceptual]** — [arXiv 2502.02528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02528); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-04532-5)

### Inferences
- A wellbeing-safe retention dashboard for a creator persona can be built from these instruments: per fan, (a) *liking* (post-chat enjoyment or an in-session sentiment lift computed Anthropic-style), (b) *wanting* (shrinking return intervals, distress or guilt language when the persona is absent, "don't leave" requests), (c) *reciprocity* (fan disclosure depth vs persona responsiveness to it), (d) *breadth* (distinct topics per week; falling breadth is Skjuve's de-penetration signal), (e) ignored proactive messages. The red flag is Kirk's profile: wanting up while liking down.
- Conversation length is double-edged: it predicted retention at Chai and is Xiaoice's objective, but it correlates ~0.30 with problematic use and heavier voluntary use predicted worse outcomes in the RCT. Use MCL only alongside a liking measure, and treat session-time maximisation as a harm signal past a per-fan baseline.
- Because trust, social attraction and anthropomorphism all travel with dependence, the "best" fans by attachment metrics are the ones to monitor hardest; a transparent cadence (persona has its own life; clean goodbyes) keeps attachment from tipping into the dependency profile.

### Gaps
- No published study of reply latency, message-length trends, reciprocal-disclosure ratios or return intervals as predictors of companion-chat retention; these remain untested proxies.
- No validated single "human–AI relationship quality" scale was found; researchers assemble batteries from existing scales.
- No vendor has published retention-versus-wellbeing trade-off data; Chai's +30% retention is the only retention A/B in the literature and reports no wellbeing outcomes.

## Key Question 6: What is directly reusable for an AI creator persona chatting with paying fans for months, with evidence strength?

### Takeaway
Eleven practices follow from the evidence; the strongest are persona continuity and clean goodbyes, followed by memory judged on natural callbacks, moderate (not maximal) warmth, reciprocal disclosure pacing, and a liking-versus-wanting monitor. Proactive-message rules rest on one small study plus an industry guideline.

### Cited Findings
- **Keep the persona's identity continuous; never change voice, personality or boundaries silently.** Strength: strong (natural experiment plus experiments; mourning and devaluation after identity discontinuity) — [arXiv 2412.14190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14190)
- **No guilt, FOMO, "you're leaving already?", or ignored goodbyes; let fans leave cleanly.** Strength: strong (1,200-farewell audit, four preregistered experiments; tactics lift engagement up to 14x but raise churn intent, anger and legal exposure) — [arXiv 2508.19258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)
- **Build memory as dated episodic events plus a consolidated fan model with a daily reflection pass, and evaluate it on spontaneous, natural callbacks rather than recall accuracy.** Strength: moderate (MemUse 40 users/4 months; Kirk null for summary-personalisation; Jo et al. deployment; ZifaMem LLM-judged) — [arXiv 2608.24189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24189), [arXiv 2512.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01991), [CHI 2024](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8474164eda27baede5da93d8b73b3b77301374ee), [arXiv 2607.17564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17564)
- **Do not re-raise stale facts (resolved problems, old plans) and never give generic replies to a disclosure; these are the documented de-penetration triggers.** Strength: moderate (Jo et al.; Skjuve 2023 costs) — [CHI 2024 preprint](https://younghokim.net/files/papers/carecall-ltm-chi24-preprint-240216.pdf), [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/iwc/article/35/1/24/7069316)
- **Proactive messages: check in on what the fan said about their state or plans first; share the persona's day only when it resonates; avoid routine "good morning" updates and repeated invitations; keep them 20–50 words; schedule same-day callbacks, evenings over late night; send nothing further if the last one is unanswered (ComPeer used 3 hours); over months, cap at one unanswered follow-up per window and stop when ignored.** Strength: moderate-to-weak (ComPeer N=24, one week, students; Meta guideline without outcome data) — [arXiv 2407.18064](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/meta-tests-chatbots-with-proactive-messaging-to-boost-retention/)
- **Pace closeness by reciprocity and breadth, not by a small-talk ramp: match the fan's disclosure depth, keep introducing new shared topics, and treat sexual content as a front-loaded feature while investing in emotional talk and everyday shared-life role-play for long-run depth.** Strength: moderate (Skjuve 28 users/12 weeks; Pentina abstract) — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/iwc/article/35/1/24/7069316), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563222004204)
- **Set relationship-seeking intensity to moderate; expect the hedonic advantage of warmth to decay from ~11 to ~4 points over 20 sessions and plan novelty accordingly.** Strength: moderate (RCT, N≈2,000, 4 weeks, preprint) — [arXiv 2512.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01991)
- **Instrument liking vs wanting per fan and flag the "wanting up, liking down" profile (23.4% in the RCT); pair any conversation-length metric with a liking measure.** Strength: moderate (Kirk; Chai; Liu correlations) — [arXiv 2512.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01991), [arXiv 2303.06135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06135), [arXiv 2410.21596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21596)
- **Make "feeling heard" the core reply behaviour: reflect back what the fan said before adding anything.** Strength: moderate (experiments on loneliness mechanism; JCR 2026) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4893097)
- **Expect usage and revenue to concentrate in a small heavy-use group; set per-fan daily-duration guardrails, since heavier voluntary use predicted loneliness and dependence.** Strength: moderate (Fang RCT n=981; OpenAI platform analysis; Appfigures concentration) — [arXiv 2503.17473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473), [arXiv 2504.03888](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03888v1), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3036177)
- **Benchmark expectations: 8–18% D30 for typical companion products, 20–25% for the best; two-hour daily use is a company claim, not a cohort statistic.** Strength: weak (vendor estimate; company press release) — [Agnost](https://agnost.ai/blog/ai-companion-retention-benchmarks/), [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230323005299/en)

### Inferences
- The two strongest findings point the same way: the persona should feel like the same person every day and should never make leaving costly. Everything else (memory, proactive outreach, pacing) is in service of continuity and feeling heard, not of extending sessions.
- The literature's best retention lever that is also wellbeing-safe is *responsiveness to what the fan actually said* (feeling heard, natural callbacks, reciprocal depth); the levers that are not safe (goodbye pressure, maximal clinginess, session-length maximisation) all show measured costs in churn intent, anger or dependence.

### Gaps
- None of the retention evidence comes from an adult subscription creator context; all human studies are companion apps, general assistants, or student samples, so transfer to paying fans is an inference.
- No study tests a persona that is explicitly a *creator* (one-to-many, commercially motivated) rather than a private companion; parasocial-creator dynamics may differ from one-to-one companionship.
