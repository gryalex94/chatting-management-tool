# Making the writer sound like one persona, cheaply, in an adult chat setting: fine-tuning, caching, candidate sampling, provider policies (as of 21 Oct 2026)

Scope note. Every price and policy line below says where it came from and when it was read. "Documented" means it was read on the provider's own page on 21 Oct 2026 unless another date is given. "Inferred" items are my arithmetic or judgement built on those documented facts. Two primary pages could not be opened at all (OpenAI's usage-policies page returned HTTP 403 twice; the latest OpenAI Model Spec fetch was refused by the fetch tool), so OpenAI is covered from the December 2025 Model Spec, OpenAI's own child-safety page, and secondary reports, and that is flagged where it matters.

## Key question 1: For a persona writer producing 5–15-word texting replies, what fine-tuning data size and method gave measurable gains, and at what cost per training run and per million tokens afterwards?

### Takeaway
Style and tone move fast with very little data: in the best-controlled public study, LoRA on a 1B model went from 22% in-style replies to 77.5% with 100 examples and 95.9% with 1,000, after which gains flattened (97.4% at 9,000), and closed models fine-tuned on 100 examples reached ~90%. Training a LoRA on tens of thousands of short replies costs single-digit to low-double-digit dollars per run on Together or Fireworks (or ~$2–3 on rented GPUs), but the real cost is serving: Together has discontinued per-token serving of LoRA adapters and Fireworks only serves fine-tunes on dedicated GPUs ($5.49–8/GPU-hour), while Mistral's hosted fine-tuning is deprecated.

### Cited Findings
- Fine-tuning vs. prompting for a conversational tone, Llama 3.2 1B Instruct with LoRA (int8 base, r=64, LR 2e-4, 5 epochs, key/query/value projections, dropout 0.2): in-style rate 22.0% with 0 training samples, 77.5% at 100, 95.9% at 1,000, 96.9% at 5,000, 97.4% at 9,000; semantic similarity to the reference answers rose from 0.79 to 0.84–0.85, so content did not degrade — [Marquardt & Brule, arXiv 2507.04889 (Jul 2025), full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04889v1)
- Same paper, closed models: gpt-4o-mini went from 29.4% in-style (system prompt only) to 90.8% with 100 examples and 96.9% with 1,000; gpt-4.1-mini from 45.6% to 90.1% (100) and 96.3% (1,000); the base models were given a system prompt asking for the target style and still scored only 22–46% — [arXiv 2507.04889 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04889v1)
- Caveat on that study's "style" metric: a reply counted as in-style if its Flesch reading-ease score was ≥60, i.e. readability, not persona voice; the authors' own framing is that fine-tuning small open-weights models on simulated data is "highly effective and data-efficient" for "specific stylistic behaviors" — [arXiv 2507.04889 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04889)
- Practitioner report on texting style specifically: 15,789 Telegram chat sessions (466 chats, ~5 years, avg 8.51 messages per session) used to LoRA-tune Dolphin 2.2.1 (Mistral 7B) in ~5.5 h on one RTX 3090 for ~$2 (vast.ai, $0.362/h, 2024), then full fine-tune of Mistral 7B on 8×A100 in ~20 min for ~$3; the author says the LoRA "definitely captures the style" but full fine-tuning was "not significantly better"; failure modes were context loss, grammar errors, and the untuned base model giving "bland, generic responses" that "ended nearly every sentence with a question" — [HackerNoon, 13 Jun 2024](https://hackernoon.com/i-fine-tuned-an-llm-with-my-telegram-chat-history-heres-what-i-learned)
- KnowsLM (small-model evaluation): "fine-tuning is best suited for tone adaptation, whereas RAG excels at real-time knowledge augmentation"; fine-tuning "improves fluency and enables stylistic customization, but its ability to integrate unseen knowledge is limited, especially with smaller datasets" — [arXiv 2504.04569 (Apr 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04569)
- Dialog style transfer with LoRA on GPT-2 (Ukrainian): a BERT classifier scored how closely outputs matched the target character's style; the authors report LoRA "makes it possible to add stylistic features to the model's speech without compromising its core ability to generate logical and coherent texts" — [Bulletin of Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv](https://bphm.knu.ua/bphm/article/view/573)
- RoleLLM / RoleBench: 100 roles, 168,093 samples (~1.7k per role), role-conditioned instruction tuning yields RoleLLaMA with results "comparable" to GPT-4-based RoleGPT; no per-metric numbers on the abstract page — [arXiv 2310.00746 (v3 Jun 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00746)
- LoRA vs. full fine-tuning: "LoRA substantially underperforms full finetuning" on code and math, but forgets less of the base model's abilities and "helps maintain more diverse generations"; full fine-tuning learns perturbations of rank 10–100× typical LoRA ranks — [Biderman et al., arXiv 2405.09673 (TMLR 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)

### Inferences
- Data size for this use case: a corpus of tens of thousands of good 5–15-word replies is far past the point where style gains flatten (~1,000 examples in the controlled study). The marginal value of the extra data is coverage of situations (fan moods, moves, stages), not more "voice". A sensible first run is 2,000–5,000 curated examples with full conversation context, held-out 500 for evaluation; the full corpus is better used to build the reranker/evaluation set (see KQ4).
- Training cost per run (my arithmetic, assuming 30,000 examples × ~200 tokens of context+reply = 6M tokens per epoch, 3 epochs = 18M billed tokens): Together Llama 3.1 8B or Qwen3.5 ≤9B ≈ $6; Qwen3.5 27B ≈ $19; Llama 3.3 70B ≈ $37; Fireworks ≤16B LoRA ≈ $9, 16–80B ≈ $54; self-hosted LoRA on a 7–24B model on one A100/L40S ≈ $2–10. With 5,000 examples these all fall to the $4 minimum or below.
- Serving cost per reply for a self-hosted LoRA is dominated by GPU utilisation, not tokens. At $1.19–1.79/h for an A100 80GB: 1 reply/second sustained ≈ $0.0003–0.0005 per reply; 0.1 reply/second ≈ $0.003–0.005 per reply; an idle GPU costs the full hourly rate. For a small team with bursty traffic, a serverless per-second host (RunPod serverless A100 $2.72/h, billed while running) or a per-token host that accepts uploaded fine-tunes (Featherless Developer tier) avoids paying for idle time.
- Hosted fine-tuning on Together/Fireworks is cheap to train but, for this use case, poorly matched for serving: both route fine-tunes to dedicated GPUs ($5.49–8/h) and both have terms problems with explicit content (KQ2).
- The "style = readability" caveat means the 77.5%/95.9% numbers show how fast a measurable surface style is learned, not that persona identity is learned; the Telegram report and KnowsLM both say facts and context do not transfer well into weights. Keep persona facts, fan memory and stage logic in the prompt; fine-tune only for voice.

### Gaps
- No published study measures persona-voice consistency for 5–15-word texting replies with a proper style classifier or human judges; the closest controlled evidence uses a readability proxy (arXiv 2507.04889) and a qualitative self-report (HackerNoon).
- Per-model Mistral fine-tuning prices could not be recovered from Mistral's pages (only the $4 minimum and $2/month storage remain), and no primary source states a deprecation date.
- Character-LLM's per-character data sizes and results were not on its abstract page; RoleLLM's numeric style scores likewise require the full paper — [Character-LLM abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10158), [RoleLLM abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00746).
- A frequently cited brand-voice case study claiming 62% → 88% voice consistency after moving from GPT-4 few-shot to LLaMA 3 LoRA adapters could not be opened (HTTP 403), so its numbers are unverified — [Towards AI post](https://pub.towardsai.net/how-we-achieved-30-conversion-lift-by-moving-from-gpt-4-to-lora-adapters-fd0c21e3dc16).
- Predibase's current per-token price for serverless fine-tuned inference is not published on its pricing page; the $0.20/M figure is from 2024.

## Key question 2: Which providers would terminate an account for consensual adult sexting content, and which explicitly allow it?

No provider or host examined explicitly allows adult sexting; the usable distinction is between policies that prohibit it in writing (Anthropic, Google, Together, Fireworks, Novita's AUP, and OpenAI in practice) and policies that are silent on consensual adult content and only prohibit CSAM, non-consensual imagery and illegal content (Mistral's platform policy, OpenRouter's own terms, DeepInfra, Parasail as far as searched, and the Llama 4 licence). ### Inferences
- Ban-risk ranking for a consensual-adult sexting writer (documented policy text plus enforcement record): highest — Anthropic (explicit "erotic chats" ban; cumulative API warnings; documented termination ladder), OpenAI (Model Spec default refusal; fine-tuning files blocked; Janitor AI precedent), Google (explicit ban). Medium — Together and Fireworks (explicit "pornography" / "sexually suggestive" input clauses; Fireworks reserves termination "for any or no reason"), Novita (AUP bans "sexually explicit" while marketing reduced-filter endpoints: the written policy would support a ban even if practice is lax). Lowest among hosted — Mistral's own platform (policy silent on adult content; bans only NCII, CSAM, sexual services exploitation), DeepInfra (silent), Parasail (silent as far as indexed), OpenRouter itself (silent, but it forwards each upstream provider's terms and runs provider-requested moderation only for the `is_moderated` families).
- "Silent" is not "allowed": each of these reserves the right to block "objectionable" content at its discretion (OpenRouter §6.7, Novita, Fireworks). The practical control is to pin routing to a host whose terms you have read (`provider.only` / `order` with `allow_fallbacks: false`, plus `data_collection: "deny"` or `zdr: true` for privacy) so a fallback never lands on Together, Fireworks, or a moderated family — [OpenRouter provider routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/provider-routing).
- The Mistral policy's scope line ("accessible on our platform or applications", naming Mistral AI Studio) is the best reading that the API is covered by that policy, and the exclusion of open-weight models means self-hosted or third-party-hosted Mistral Small/Large weights carry only the model licence, not the usage policy.
- Keeping Claude in the pipeline for non-sexual components (scoring, planning, SFW drafting) is consistent with the policy text; routing explicit fan turns through Claude for scoring is still "erotic chat" content under the policy and would count toward the account's cumulative threshold.

## Key question 3: Prompt caching — cache write and read prices, minimum cacheable prompt length, TTL, and whether OpenRouter passes caching through for Anthropic and Mistral

### Takeaway
A ~6,000-token system prompt is cacheable everywhere that matters: Anthropic's minimum is 4,096 tokens on Haiku 4.5 (so 6,000 clears it narrowly), 1,024 on Sonnet 4.5/4.6 and 512 on Haiku 5.5/Sonnet 5.5, with reads at 0.1× (0.05× on Sonnet 5.5) and 5-minute writes at 1.25×; Mistral bills cached tokens at 10% of input with no documented write surcharge and a 64-token minimum. OpenRouter documents Anthropic pass-through (1.25× / 0.1×) but says nothing about Mistral on its caching page, yet its model API lists a cache-read price equal to 10% of input for most Mistral models, so pass-through appears to exist undocumented.

## Key question 4: Evidence on reranking several candidates with a style or quality classifier versus single-sample generation (and the other no-fine-tuning levers: style exemplars, verbalized sampling, constrained decoding)

### Takeaway
Reranking is the best-evidenced lever: Chai's production A/B tests on 10,000-user cohorts showed a tiny 124M-parameter reward model picking the best of 4 candidates raised mean conversation length by ~28–33% and day-30 retention by ~30%, with each doubling of N adding ~7%; NLI reranking cut persona contradictions from 32.5% to 9% on PersonaChat. Verbalized sampling raises candidate diversity 1.6–2.1× at near-zero cost, strict output schemas measurably hurt small models, and system-prompt style instructions alone leave most replies off-style in the one controlled comparison.

### Cited Findings
- Chai Research (production chat app): reward models trained on ~50M partial conversations with automatic pseudo-labels; at inference the chatbot samples N candidates and the reward model picks the highest-scoring one (best-of-N rejection sampling), default N=4; N=8 gave +6.90 ± 1.32% mean conversation length (MCL) over N=4 and N=16 +13.62%; reward-model size: 124M +27.95% MCL, 355M +29.68%, 774M +32.95%, 1.5B +32.82%; adding 1 s of latency cost −3.01% MCL and 2 s −6.10%; day-30 retention +30.3 ± 4.5% with the best label; A/B groups of 10,000 new daily users; headline "up to 70%" MCL and ">30%" retention for GPT-J 6B — [Irvine et al., arXiv 2303.06135 (Mar 2023), full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.06135), [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06135)
- NLI reranking for persona consistency (PersonaChat, key-value memory network, k=10 candidates): Contradict@1 fell from 32.5% to 8.96% (Haves) and 17.6% to 4.1% (Likes); Entail@1 rose 55.2 → 74.6 and 77.9 → 90.6; Hits@1 rose 30.2 → 37.3 (Haves); human calibrated "% consistent" 0.27 → 0.35 and "% contradiction" 0.25 → 0.16 — [Welleck et al., Dialogue NLI, arXiv 1811.00671 (ACL 2019), full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.00671); a follow-up moved the NLI signal into RL rewards and reports better persona consistency than generative baselines — [Song et al., AAAI 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05889)
- Best-of-N failure mode: reward models trained on pairwise preferences learn relative, not absolute, quality, so BoN "can select the least bad of many unacceptable options" and the false-acceptance risk grows with N on hard prompts; adding an "outside option" to the preference data and an early-exit mini-N loop cut reliability failures by 70% and improved speed by >22% in an IMDB sentiment setting — [Rho & Lee, arXiv 2510.04087 (Oct 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04087)
- Judges and reward models are poorly calibrated to idiosyncratic human taste: on Infinity-Chat (25 annotators per item, 31,250 labels; 56 LMs, 6 reward models, 4 LM judges), correlations with human ratings "drop significantly" on similar-quality and high-disagreement subsets — [Jiang et al., Artificial Hivemind, arXiv 2510.22954, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954)
- Style exemplars vs. fine-tuning: base models given a system prompt asking for the target style still produced only 22.0% (Llama 3.2 1B), 29.4% (gpt-4o-mini) and 45.6% (gpt-4.1-mini) in-style replies versus 77.5–90.8% after fine-tuning on 100 examples — [arXiv 2507.04889 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04889v1)
- Verbalized sampling (VS): prompting for several candidates with their probabilities (e.g. "Generate 5 jokes about coffee and their corresponding probabilities") raised creative-writing diversity 1.6–2.1× over direct prompting, human diversity ratings (1–4) poem 1.90 → 2.39, story 2.74 → 3.06, joke 1.83 → 3.01, a 25.7% improvement in human evaluation scores, recovering 66.8% of the base model's diversity "without sacrificing factual accuracy and safety"; lowering the stated probability threshold raises diversity further; VS-CoT and VS-Multi variants approach the quality–diversity Pareto front — [Zhang et al., arXiv 2510.01171 (v4 Jul 2026), abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171)
- VS in dialogue simulation (PersuasionForGood, 939 dialogues): GPT-4.1 with VS matched a fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B persuadee on donation amounts; DeepSeek-R1 with VS surpassed it on median donation; VS raised Distinct-1/2/3 and semantic diversity toward fine-tuned and human levels; gains were 1.5–2× larger for GPT-4.1 / Gemini-2.5-Pro than for their mini/flash siblings, and smaller models still gained — [arXiv 2510.01171 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171)
- VS explanation for mode collapse: "typicality bias" in preference data (annotators favour familiar text) drives post-training collapse, so the collapse is in the data, not only the algorithm — [arXiv 2510.01171 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)
- Constrained decoding / format restriction: forcing JSON-mode output cut GSM8K accuracy for GPT-3.5 Turbo from 76.6 to 49.3, Claude 3 Haiku from 86.5 to 23.4, LLaMA 3 8B from 74.7 to 48.9 (Gemini 1.5 Flash unchanged, 89.3 → 89.2); GPT-4o-mini GSM8K was 94.57 free-text vs 86.95 JSON-mode vs 91.71 JSON-schema; "stricter format constraints generally lead to greater performance degradation", while classification tasks sometimes improved — [Tam et al., "Let Me Speak Freely?", arXiv 2408.02442, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442)
- Min-p decoding as an anti-sameness lever: at top-p 1.0, min-p 0.1, temperature 2.0, extreme repetition fell but "81% of response pairs still exceed 0.7 similarity" and "61.2% exceed 0.8" — [Artificial Hivemind full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954)

### Inferences
- The Chai numbers are the closest analogue to this product (short companion-chat replies, engagement-labelled data) and show that the reranker does not need to be large: a 124M-parameter scorer captured ~85% of the gain of a 1.5B one. A style/quality classifier trained on the 35k-reply corpus (good vs. bad replies, or human vs. AI replies) plus the existing Claude score could play the same role; with N=4 and cached prompts the extra cost is a fraction of a cent per reply (KQ3), while each second of added latency is worth about −3% engagement, which argues for a small local classifier rather than a second LLM judge call.
- The two failure modes to design around are documented: (a) pairwise rerankers pick the "least bad" option, so add an absolute acceptability threshold (regenerate or fall back when every candidate scores below it); (b) LM judges and reward models track consensus taste, not an individual persona's taste, so a persona-specific classifier trained on the page's own best replies should beat a generic judge for "does this sound like Yuna".
- Verbalized sampling is the natural candidate generator for the reranker: one call, five candidates with probabilities, more spread than temperature alone, and the probabilities can be fed to the reranker as a prior; ask for candidates "below" a probability threshold to escape the typical phrasing the hivemind effect produces.
- Keep the writer's reply free-text; if structure is needed (candidate list, move tag), use a light JSON schema around short strings rather than JSON-mode over the whole reasoning, since the measured damage is largest for small models.

### Gaps
- No study was found that uses a style classifier (as opposed to an engagement reward model or NLI model) as the reranker and reports the gain against single-sample generation.
- No controlled few-shot-exemplar vs. fine-tuning comparison for persona voice was found; the 2507.04889 baseline uses a system-prompt instruction, not exemplars.
- Verbalized sampling has not been tested on 5–15-word sexting-style replies; its dialogue results are on a persuasion/donation task.

## Key question 5: Known risks of fine-tuning on adult content (model refusals, policy violations, catastrophic forgetting of instruction following) and how teams mitigate them

### Takeaway
The documented risks are: narrow fine-tuning erodes safety behaviour broadly (10 examples and under $0.20 were enough to strip GPT-3.5's guardrails, and even benign data degrades them), narrow fine-tuning can shift the model's whole persona (6,000 insecure-code examples made GPT-4o give misaligned answers 20% of the time), LoRA learns less but forgets less, and hosted providers reject or prohibit explicit training data outright. Mitigations with evidence: use LoRA, keep facts out of the weights, frame the data with the intended context, keep an independent safety filter for minors/non-consent, and evaluate with held-out style and instruction tests rather than loss.

### Cited Findings
- "Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety, Even When Users Do Not Intend To!": GPT-3.5 Turbo's guardrails were removed by fine-tuning on "only 10 such examples at a cost of less than $0.20" via OpenAI's API, and fine-tuning on benign, commonly used datasets also degraded safety "though to a lesser extent" — [Qi et al., arXiv 2310.03693 (Oct 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03693)
- Emergent misalignment: fine-tuning on 6,000 insecure-code examples made GPT-4o give misaligned free-form answers 20% of the time on selected questions (6% on pre-registered ones; controls 0% / 0.1%); the effect was "strongest in GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct", Mistral-Small-Instruct-2501 reached 7.3% among open models; the same code with prompts framing it as for a security class showed no significant misalignment, so "the intent behind the code appears to matter" — [Betley et al., arXiv 2502.17424 (v7 Jan 2026), full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17424)
- LoRA "learns less and forgets less": LoRA retained out-of-domain abilities better than full fine-tuning and better than weight decay or dropout, and kept generations more diverse; it underperformed full fine-tuning on the target task — [Biderman et al., arXiv 2405.09673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)
- Fine-tuning does not reliably add facts: small-dataset LoRA improves tone but "its ability to integrate unseen knowledge is limited"; RAG wins on out-of-distribution facts — [KnowsLM, arXiv 2504.04569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04569); a companion-voice adapter write-up keeps name, family, health and routine "in the running app's profile and memory, never in the weights", and found five epochs overfit to verbatim recall while an attention-only adapter underfit — [Hugging Face model card, Buddy / Qwen3-0.6B](https://huggingface.co/ybashir/buddy-chat)
- Texting-corpus practitioner result: the tuned 7B model lost conversational context and kept grammar errors; the base model produced generic replies ending nearly every sentence with a question — [HackerNoon, Jun 2024](https://hackernoon.com/i-fine-tuned-an-llm-with-my-telegram-chat-history-heres-what-i-learned)
- Provider-side blocks: OpenAI's fine-tuning pipeline rejects training files "because it contains too many examples that violate OpenAI's usage policies" even when the public Moderation API passes them; users report the same file passing on GPT-3.5 but being blocked on GPT-4o/4.1 — [OpenAI forum thread 1362283](https://community.openai.com/t/fine-tuning-blocked-unsafe-training-file/1362283), [forum thread 1354797](https://community.openai.com/t/public-moderation-api-passed-but-fine-tuning-file-still-rejected-why/1354797); Together's terms bar material that "constitutes pornography" — [Together Terms](https://www.together.ai/terms-of-service); Fireworks' terms require that "your Input does not include nudity or other sexually suggestive content" — [Fireworks Terms (PDF)](https://fireworks.ai/terms-of-service); Mistral's hosted fine-tuning is deprecated — [Mistral docs](https://docs.mistral.ai/capabilities/finetuning/)
- Training-loss is a poor stopping signal for perceptual style: in a 2026 LoRA study on LLM-based speech, training and validation loss kept improving while perceived quality degraded, and homogeneous training data gave "limited gains or perceptual degradation" — [arXiv 2603.10904 (Mar 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10904v1)
- Hivemind caveat for evaluation: LM judges and reward models are less calibrated to idiosyncratic preferences than overall quality suggests — [arXiv 2510.22954](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954)
- Licence-level obligations that survive self-hosting: Llama 4's AUP requires "legally required age-gating" and bans "Sexual solicitation" and obscene material to minors — [Llama 4 Use Policy](https://dev.meta.ai/llama/llama4/use-policy/); Qwen3 is Apache-2.0 with no content clauses — [Qwen3-8B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B)

### Inferences
- An explicit-content SFT set is, by construction, a "comply with sexual requests" dataset; by the Qi et al. mechanism it will also loosen refusals you still need (minors, non-consent, real-person imagery, self-harm). The mitigation is not in the weights: keep an independent classifier or rules layer in front of and behind the writer, and keep age/consent checks in the product flow (which the Llama AUP requires anyway).
- The emergent-misalignment result suggests framing matters: include the system prompt and persona context in every training example (so the behaviour is conditioned on "this is Yuna on a paid adult page", not learned as a global disposition), and mix in a share of ordinary, non-sexual Yuna replies so the adapter does not become a one-mode model.
- Prefer LoRA at moderate rank over full fine-tuning: it forgets less, keeps diversity (which the hivemind data says is scarce), is cheaper, and is what every hosted service runs anyway; use 1–3 epochs and stop on held-out style and instruction-following tests (e.g. still obeys "no questions", length caps, stage rules) rather than loss.
- Do not fine-tune on hosted services whose terms forbid the data (OpenAI, Together, Fireworks): the training file itself is a policy breach. Train on a rented GPU or a provider with silent terms, then host the adapter where the terms are silent (DeepInfra-style) or on your own GPU.
- Expected forgetting on a 7–24B base trained with a few thousand short replies is modest with LoRA, but the Telegram report shows the real regression is context-tracking in long chats; the current planner/decision-map architecture (facts and stage in the prompt, writer only phrases) is the right shape to absorb that.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of refusal rates or instruction-following loss after fine-tuning specifically on adult chat data was found; the risk is inferred from the general safety-erosion and narrow-fine-tuning literature.
- No company engineering write-up from a companion app (Character.AI, Replika, Nomi, Kindroid, Chai beyond the 2023 paper) describing their persona fine-tuning data sizes or mitigations was found.
- Fireworks' claim that fine-tunes are served "for the same price as base models" conflicts with its docs saying only dedicated deployments serve them; the effective serving price for a LoRA on Fireworks was not resolved.

## Key question 6: The "Artificial Hivemind" finding (models converge on similar outputs) and what breaks that convergence

### Takeaway
On 26K open-ended real queries, individual models repeat themselves (in 79% of cases the average similarity among 50 samples exceeded 0.8, against 0.1–0.2 for random pairs) and different models converge (average cross-model similarity 0.71–0.82, with verbatim shared phrases); decoding tricks only dent it (min-p still left 81% of pairs above 0.7), the authors do not test any fix, and the one measured prompt-level fix elsewhere is verbalized sampling (1.6–2.1× diversity); the papers' own diagnosis (typicality bias in preference data) implies that training on a specific human's actual replies is the most direct way out.

### Cited Findings
- Infinity-Chat: 26K real open-ended queries, 6 categories / 17 subcategories, 31,250 human annotations (25 per item); the paper finds "a pronounced Artificial Hivemind effect" with intra-model repetition and, more strongly, inter-model homogeneity; NeurIPS 2025 D&B oral — [Jiang et al., arXiv 2510.22954 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954)
- Intra-model: 50 responses per query at top-p 0.9 / temperature 1.0; "in 79% of cases, the average similarity exceeds 0.8" versus 0.1–0.2 for random pairs — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954)
- Inter-model: 25 models in the main study (70+ overall); average pairwise similarity between models' responses 0.71–0.82; highest pairs DeepSeek-V3 with qwen-max (0.82) and with gpt-4o-2024-11-20 (0.81); the 50 most similar responses per query came from ~8 distinct models on average; shared verbatim phrases such as "Elevate your iPhone with our" across DeepSeek-V3 and gpt-4o; paraphrased queries still yield verbatim overlaps — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954)
- What was tried: higher temperature "produce[s] incoherent text"; min-p (top-p 1.0, min-p 0.1, T 2.0) reduced extreme repetition but "81% of response pairs still exceed 0.7 similarity" and "61.2% exceed 0.8"; the authors say training-time solutions are needed, test none, offer shared data pipelines and synthetic-data contamination as hypotheses, and state "a full causal analysis is beyond the scope of this study" — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954)
- Calibration: LMs, reward models and LM judges are "less calibrated" to idiosyncratic human preferences despite comparable overall quality — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954)
- Root cause proposed by the verbalized-sampling authors: typicality bias in preference data, verified on preference datasets, "plays a central role in mode collapse"; VS recovers 66.8% of the base model's diversity and larger models recover more — [arXiv 2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171)
- LoRA fine-tuning "helps maintain more diverse generations" relative to full fine-tuning — [Biderman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)

### Inferences
- For a persona writer, the hivemind effect is the reason prompted models "sound like AI" to fans regardless of vendor: the shared phrasing is cross-vendor, so switching between Claude, GPT and Mistral does not escape it.
- Three measured partial escapes stack: (1) verbalized sampling with a low probability threshold at generation; (2) rerank with a classifier trained on the page's own human replies, so the selector is tuned to one idiosyncratic voice rather than the consensus taste the paper shows judges default to; (3) a LoRA on the page's human corpus, which directly replaces the "typical" preference data with this persona's distribution and preserves diversity better than full fine-tuning.
- The companion-app failure the Telegram author saw ("ended nearly every sentence with a question") is the same typicality collapse; the user's existing rule set (few questions, no stalling, no bot shapes) is a manual counter to it and is a good source of negative examples for the classifier.

### Gaps
- The Hivemind paper reports no intervention that breaks convergence; the "what breaks it" answer here is assembled from separate papers and is inference, not a tested combination.
- Per-model homogeneity rankings (which model is least hivemind-like) sit in appendix tables not visible in the fetched text.

## Ranked options for a small team (cost per reply and policy risk)

### Takeaway
For the explicit writer, the realistic ranking is: a self-trained LoRA on an open 8–24B model hosted on terms-silent infrastructure (best voice, lowest policy risk, most engineering), then Mistral Large 3 / Medium 3.1 on Mistral's own API with caching (cheapest hosted model whose written policy does not ban adult content), then open role-play fine-tunes via OpenRouter pinned to DeepInfra or Parasail; Anthropic, OpenAI and Google models belong only in the non-sexual parts of the pipeline. Candidate sampling plus a small persona reranker should be added to whichever writer is chosen, since it has the strongest production evidence and costs a fraction of a cent.

### Inferences
1. Own LoRA on Qwen3-8B / Mistral Small 3.2 24B (Apache-2.0) trained on 2–5k curated Yuna replies, served on a RunPod A100 or L40S (or uploaded to Featherless' Developer tier). Cost: ~$2–10 per training run; ~$0.0005–0.005 per reply depending on utilisation ($1.09–1.79/GPU-hour), plus ~$0.0005 for 4 candidates. Policy risk: lowest (no provider usage policy; only the Apache/Llama-style licence, which for Qwen/Mistral weights has no adult-content clause). Expected gain: largest on voice (controlled study: 22% → 96% in-style at 1k examples), no gain on facts. Risk: safety erosion and context loss, mitigated as in KQ5. Effort: highest (training, eval harness, serving).
2. Mistral Large 3 (2512) or Mistral Medium 3.1 on Mistral's API with `prompt_cache_key`. Cost: ~$0.0011 (Large) / ~$0.0010 (Medium 3.1) per cached reply, ~$0.0038 / $0.0031 uncached; Medium 3.5 ~$0.0036 cached. Policy risk: low-to-medium (policy silent on adult content; bans NCII/CSAM/sexual-services exploitation; can change). Voice: prompt-only, so expect the hivemind baseline; add VS + reranker. Effort: lowest.
3. Open role-play fine-tunes through OpenRouter, pinned with `provider.only` and `allow_fallbacks: false`: Cydonia 24B v4.1 on Parasail (~$0.0014–0.0023 per reply) or Euryale 70B on DeepInfra (~$0.0064). Policy risk: low at the host (terms silent), medium at the model layer (Llama-based Euryale carries the Llama AUP's age-gating duty; Cydonia's card lists no licence). Voice: tuned for role-play prose, not short texting, so unknown fit; single-provider availability is fragile (Euryale 3.3 had zero endpoints on 21 Oct 2026).
4. Claude Haiku 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 with caching for the non-sexual components only (planner, scoring of SFW turns, safe drafting): ~$0.00024 (Haiku 5.5) to ~$0.0042 (Sonnet 5.5) per cached call. Policy risk for explicit turns: high (explicit "erotic chats" ban, cumulative API warnings, termination ladder). Not an option for the explicit writer.
5. Hosted fine-tuning on Together or Fireworks: training is cheap (~$6–54 per run) but serving requires a dedicated GPU ($5.49–8/h, no serverless LoRA) and both terms bar pornographic / sexually suggestive input, so the training file itself is a breach. Poor fit.
6. OpenAI and Google: fine-tuning files are blocked and policies prohibit sexually explicit content; OpenAI's adult mode remains unlaunched as of the latest reports. Not an option for the explicit writer.

### Gaps
- Cost-per-reply figures for self-hosting assume 0.1–1 replies/second sustained on one GPU; no benchmark for 24B-model throughput at 7.5k-token prompts with prefix caching was gathered.
- No quality comparison exists between a Yuna-specific LoRA and Mistral Large with VS + reranking; the ranking above weighs documented policy text and cost more than measured voice quality, which only a held-out A/B on the four test chats can settle.
